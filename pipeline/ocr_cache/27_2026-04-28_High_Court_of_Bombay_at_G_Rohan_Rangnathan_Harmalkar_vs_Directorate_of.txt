ANALYSIS:

8. Atthe outset, before adverting to the submissions made by the
learned counsels for the parties, it would be apt to note that the
PMLA has been enacted to prevent money laundering and to
provide for confiscation of property derived from, or involved in
money laundering, and for the matters connected therewith and
incidental thereto. The PMLA was enacted in view of the political
declaration adopted by the special session of United Nations General
Assembly in June, 1998 calling upon the member states to adopt

national money laundering legislation and programme.

9. Section 3 of the PMLA which pertains to the offence of

money laundering, reads as under: -

“3. Offence of money-laundering. - Whosoever directly or
indirectly attempts to indulge or knowingly assists or knowingly
is a party or is actually involved in any process or activity
connected with the proceeds of crime including its concealment,
possession, acquisition or use and projecting or claiming it as
untainted property shall be guilty of offence of money-

laundering.

14. The definition of proceeds of crime is intentionally wide,
encompassing not only the original tainted property, but also its
transformed form and equivalent value. The legislative intent is to
ensure that the illicit wealth, irrespective of its current form or
possessor does not escape the reach of law. The existence of proceeds
of crime is therefore, a foundational jurisdictional fact for initiating
proceeding under the PMLA. The ED may trace such proceeds
through information derived from a police report or even an FIR
registered for the scheduled offence. Once such information suggests
that property has been generated or obtained from a scheduled
offence, the ED empowered to record an ECIR, which serves as an
internal document analogous to a preliminary record of
investigation. While the ECIR may have its genesis in the FIR of the
predicate offence, the two are distinct. In scope and purpose, the

FIR records the crime; the ECIR traces its final consequence.

15. It is settled law that although the ECIR originates from the
FIR, the moment it is registered, it stands on its own footing and
the connection with the FIR in the predicate offences loses its
relevance. The PMLA is a self-contained Code, and its provisions
give overriding effect to the procedures under the PMLA. The

32. I have also perused the decisions of the Supreme Court and
this Court, relied upon by Mr Amonkar. In those cases, bail is
granted to the applicants therein on grounds of long incarceration
based on the maximum prescribed sentence for the said offence. The
facts involved in the said decisions are distinct from the facts
involved in the present case. In many of the cases cited, it transpired
that the trials in those cases were unlikely to conclude in the near
future. However, Mr Samant, on instructions, states that the trial in
the PMLA case shall conclude within the period of 4 months. The
prosecution’s arguments on framing of charge are concluded. There
is no delay attributed to the prosecution. A 4-month period to
conclude a trial cannot be said to violate the fundamental right
under Article 21 of the Applicant, guaranteeing a speedy trial.
Hence, the contention of Mr Amonkar regarding the Applicant’s

long incarceration also fails.
