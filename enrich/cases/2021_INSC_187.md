**Arun Kumar Jagatramka v. Jindal Steel and Power Ltd. & Anr.**, [2021] 3 SCR 114 : 2021 INSC 187, Civil Appeal No. 9664 of 2019 with connected matters (Dr. D.Y. Chandrachud and M.R. Shah, JJ.; judgment by Chandrachud, J.; 15.03.2021). This is the Gujarat NRE Coke case.

**Held.**
- **Paras 52-53.** *Chitra Sharma*, *ArcelorMittal* and *Swiss Ribbons* read s.29A purposively. Its purpose "is to achieve a sustainable revival and to ensure that a person who is the cause of the problem either by a design or a default cannot be a part of the process of solution". Section 29A also covers conduct in relation to other companies.
- **Paras 67-69.** A s.230 scheme for a company in IBC liquidation follows a s.33 order and forms part of the liquidation process. It would be "far-fetched" to hold that the ineligibility under s.35(1)(f) read with s.29A does not apply to it (para 69).
- **Paras 74-75.** The Court rejected the argument that, because a s.12A withdrawal returns the company to its promoter, a promoter should be allowed to propose a scheme. A s.12A withdrawal comes at the start of the process, restores the status quo ante and is a settlement. A s.31 plan and a sanctioned s.230 scheme are culminations of the process that bind all stakeholders. The two "cannot be equated".
- **Para 76.** The NCLT, when acting under s.230 for a company in IBC liquidation, cannot disregard s.35(1)(f).
- **Paras 85-90.** NCLT and NCLAT innovation within the IBC framework should be kept "at its bare minimum". These remarks were given as guidance.
- **Para 91.** The s.29A and s.35(1)(f) prohibition attaches to a s.230 scheme for a company in IBC liquidation, and the proviso to Regulation 2B(1) is constitutionally valid. The appeals and the writ petition were dismissed.

**IBC-PMLA use.**
- Use paras 53 and 69 to resist an erstwhile promoter, or a person connected with one, regaining a corporate debtor through a scheme during liquidation. Use them with *Swiss Ribbons* (2019 INSC 95, para 69) for purchases in liquidation.
- Use para 53, together with *ArcelorMittal* (2018 INSC 935, para 57), to show that the courts look at substance: who is actually returning to control, and with whose money.
- **Limit.** The judgment distinguishes a s.12A withdrawal (paras 74-75). It does not hold that s.29A bars a promoter-funded s.12A settlement. Compendium view (not a holding): the same substance-over-form test should apply whatever label is used, whether plan, s.12A withdrawal or s.230 scheme. An opponent can answer that argument with paras 74-75. To challenge a s.12A settlement, rely instead on the CoC and NCLT scrutiny of the source of funds under *Swiss Ribbons* (2019 INSC 95, paras 52-53).

**After the 2026 Amendment.** Section 12A has been substituted. Withdrawal is now limited to the window after the CoC is constituted and before resolution plans are invited, with 90% approval. The liquidation provisions have also been restructured: the liquidation moratorium is now in s.33(1)(b), s.52(2) requires 14 days' notice from a secured creditor, and s.54 sets a 180-day period. Before citing para 69, confirm that s.35(1)(f) and Regulation 2B are still in the same form.

**Later history (per compendium).** The compendium places this case at the end of its chapter on settlements as the anchor for the substance-over-form approach. The compendium's own notes on the case were largely lost in OCR, and the digest records no later history.
