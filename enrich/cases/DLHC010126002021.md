**Nitin Jain, Liquidator of PSL Ltd. v. Enforcement Directorate**, W.P.(C) 3261/2021 (Yashwant Varma, J., Delhi High Court, reserved 07.12.2021, pronounced 15.12.2021). The citation 2021 SCC OnLine Del 5281 is given in footnote 24 of *Rajiv Chakraborty* (DLHC010046592020).

**Facts.** PSL Ltd. went into CIRP on 15.02.2019 and into liquidation on 11.09.2020. ED summoned the liquidator and, by e-mail of 25.01.2021, asked him not to dispose of assets. No PAO existed then, and the interim order of 17.03.2021 stayed the e-mail. The liquidator sold the company as a going concern for Rs. 425.50 crore, and the NCLT approved the sale on 08.09.2021. After arguments had begun, ED issued a PAO on 02.12.2021 attaching assets worth Rs. 274.60 crore (paras 3-7 of the judgment).

**Holding** (paragraph numbers from the judgment).
- **PAO during the writ does not end the challenge** (para 101(A)-(B)). The question whether ED may act against the corporate debtor's property after a liquidation measure has been approved remained open to decide.
- **The two statutes** (para 101(C)-(G)). The IBC and PMLA operate over distinct subjects. A court must first ask whether there is real irreconcilability. Where a conflict arises, the court reconciles them, following *Axis Bank*.
- **The answer lies in s.32A** (para 101(H)). The Court held that reconciliation "needs to be answered solely on the anvil of Section 32A". Once Parliament made a specific provision for cessation of liability and prosecution, that provision alone governs how far PMLA powers survive during resolution or liquidation.
- **Trigger in liquidation** (paras 96, 98; para 101(N), (R)-(T)). The defining moment is the Adjudicating Authority's approval of the measure to be implemented. In liquidation, the bar starts when one of the measures in Regulation 32 of the Liquidation Regulations, 2016 is adopted and approved. From then "the power to attach as conferred by Section 5 of the PMLA would cease to be exercisable" and "the PMLA must yield" (para 98). On the facts, the bar ran from the NCLT's approval of the going-concern sale (para 101(T)).
- **Only the corporate debtor's property** (para 98; para 101(Q)). The bar does not extend to the persons in charge of the corporate debtor, or to ED's right to proceed against other property as explained in *Axis Bank*.
- **Eligibility objections left open** (para 99). ED's objections to the choice of the buyer and its eligibility under s.32A were left to the competent forum.
- **Liquidator must cooperate** (para 100). The liquidator holds the corporate debtor's records and must give the PMLA authorities the material and information they ask for.
- **Directions** (para 102). The petition was allowed. ED was restrained from any further action, coercive or otherwise, against the liquidation estate or the sale corpus, and the liquidator could seek release of the escrowed sums from the NCLT.

**IBC-PMLA use.**
- Attach before the NCLT approves the liquidation measure (or the resolution plan). After that date, this judgment treats the corporate debtor's property as beyond s.5 PMLA.
- If the s.32A conditions are doubtful (a buyer connected to the promoters, or one under investigation), raise it before the NCLT or NCLAT at the approval stage. The Court left those objections to that forum (para 99).
- Use para 98 and para 101(Q) to keep the prosecution of individuals and the attachment of their personal assets alive.
- Use para 100 to require liquidators to produce records.

**Later history.**
- ED's appeal, LPA 512/2021, was disposed of on 27.02.2025 (DLHC010404622021). ED stated that the Rs. 274.60 crore realised from the attached assets would be transferred to the liquidator, to be used under the IBC; the liquidator said it would be paid to victims of the fraud. The Division Bench bound the parties to these statements and said its order was not an expression of opinion on the merits or on the questions of law. The single-judge judgment was not set aside, and the appeal did not test its reasoning.
- The compendium (Wazalwar, 2027 ed.) says the restraint here rests on harmonious construction and not on s.32A. That is not what the judgment says: para 101(H) and para 98 rest it on s.32A. Do not adopt the compendium's reading.
- Read with *Rajiv Chakraborty* (DLHC010046592020, paras 105-109), where the same judge treats the s.32A trigger events as the end-point of PMLA power. The NCLAT in *Value Wise Consultancy* (30.06.2026, para 15) declined to discuss s.32A because there was no successful resolution in that case.
- After the IBC (Amendment) Act, 2026, s.32A is unchanged. The old s.33(5) liquidation moratorium is now in s.33(1)(b).
