**Tarsem Lal v. Directorate of Enforcement, Jalandhar Zonal Office**, [2024] 6 SCR 864 : 2024 INSC 434 (Abhay S. Oka and Ujjal Bhuyan, JJ., 16.05.2024). Conclusions are at para 21.

- **(a)** A complaint under s.44(1)(b) is governed by ss.200–205 CrPC.
- **(b)** If ED did not arrest the accused before the complaint, the court should, as a normal rule, issue summons rather than a warrant.
- **(c)** An accused who appears on summons is not in custody and need not apply for bail. The court may take a bond under s.88.
- **(g)** Cancelling a warrant is not a bail application, so s.45(1) does not apply.
- **(h)** Where a warrant or s.82/83 proceedings have issued, a s.88 bond is not enough; the accused must apply to cancel the warrant.
- **(i)** After cognizance, "the ED and its officers are powerless to exercise power under Section 19 to arrest a person shown as an accused in the complaint".
- **(j)** For custody for further investigation, ED must apply to the Special Court, which may allow it only if custodial interrogation is required. ED may still arrest, under s.19, a person *not* named in the complaint.

**Use in replies.**
- Plan arrests before the complaint is filed.
- After cognizance, move the Special Court for custody with reasons.
