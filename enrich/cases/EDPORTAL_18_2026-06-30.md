**Value Wise Consultancy Pvt. Ltd. (Liquidator of Siddhi Vinayak Logistics Ltd.) v. Deputy Director, Directorate of Enforcement & Ors.**, Company Appeal (AT) (Ins) Nos. 1226 and 1227 of 2022 (NCLAT, Principal Bench, New Delhi: Justice N. Seshasayee, Member (Judicial), Arun Baroka and Indevar Pandey, Members (Technical); judgment by Justice Seshasayee, 30.06.2026). The appeals arose from the NCLT Ahmedabad order of 19.07.2022 in IA 453/2019 and IA 773/2021 in CP(IB) 89/2017. The compendium gives a reporter citation "2026 LLBiz NCLAT 261 [?]", not verified. The LawBase citation field for this record shows "2019 SCC OnLine SC 1542". That is the citation of *Embassy Property*, which this order cites, and not the citation of this order.

**Facts** (para 3). ED issued s.50 notices on 24.04.2017 telling the corporate debtor's customers not to release payments. ED provisionally attached the debtor's assets on 08.06.2017. CIRP began on 12.09.2017, and the attachment was confirmed on 24.10.2017, during the moratorium. ED withdrew Rs. 2,29,10,131.06 from the debtor's ICICI Bank account on 02.08.2018. The PMLA Appellate Tribunal set the original attachment aside on 12.12.2018; ED's appeal is pending in the Bombay High Court without a stay. Liquidation was ordered on 19.11.2018. ED attached 6,170 vehicles on 18.06.2019, and the Adjudicating Authority confirmed the attachment for 1,344 of them. The liquidator sought under s.60(5) withdrawal of the attachments, refund of the money and quashing of the s.50 notices.

**Holding** (paragraph numbers from the order).
- **Scope of the moratorium** (paras 11-12). PMLA does not distinguish companies in CIRP from solvent ones. Parliament did not make the IBC "a holy Ganges" to wash the corporate debtor of PMLA criminality, nor a way to legitimise ill-gotten wealth. Sections 14 (CIRP) and 33(5) (liquidation) are meant for the debtor's legitimately acquired assets. They cover proceedings, civil or criminal, that may add to the debtor's existing debt. Penal proceedings of a public-law nature that cannot add to that debt are not affected, "even if they affect the net asset" available for CIRP or liquidation. The Tribunal relied on *Axis Bank* (2019 SCC OnLine Del 7854), *Varrsana Ispat* (with the Supreme Court order in C.A. 5546 of 2019), *Kiran Shah* and *Ashok Kumar Sarawagi*.
- ***P. Mohanraj*** (para 13). Its correctness has been doubted by the Supreme Court in *Dineshchand Surana v. UCO Bank*, which referred the issue to a larger bench.
- **No jurisdiction in IBC fora** (para 14). Following *Embassy Property* ((2020) 13 SCC 308), the NCLT's jurisdiction extends only so far as the working of the IBC requires. The Tribunal noted IBBI Circular No. IBBI/CIRP/87/2025 dated 04.11.2025, which advises insolvency professionals to apply to the Special Court under s.8(7) or 8(8) PMLA for restitution of attached assets. "Necessarily, the tribunals constituted under the IBC are not the forums which can entertain any plea against the ED."
- **s.32A not reached** (para 15). Section 32A applies only where there is a successful resolution, so it was not discussed.
- **Decision** (para 16). Whether to attach a corporate debtor's property is for ED. Any challenge to an attachment, "including any notice issued under Sec.50", lies only in the PMLA adjudicatory mechanism. For the assets released by the PMLA Appellate Tribunal, the liquidator must approach the High Court where ED's appeal is pending.
- **Result** (para 17). The appeals were dismissed and the NCLT order affirmed.

**IBC-PMLA use.**
- Lead authority for a preliminary objection to NCLT or NCLAT jurisdiction over any ED action, in CIRP or liquidation, including withdrawal of attached funds and s.50 restraint letters to customers.
- Direct insolvency professionals to the Special Court (s.8(7)/(8)) or the PMLA appellate route. Cite para 14 and the IBBI circular.
- The order decides forum only. It does not decide whether the attachments were valid, or whether ED may keep funds after the PMLA Appellate Tribunal has set the attachment aside and the High Court has granted no stay.

**After the 2026 Amendment.** Old s.33(5), which para 12 discusses, was omitted by the IBC (Amendment) Act, 2026. The liquidation moratorium is now in s.33(1)(b), which imports s.14(1)(a) and (c). The reasoning in para 12 is not tied to the wording of the old provision and should carry over, but no decision on the new provision is noted yet.
