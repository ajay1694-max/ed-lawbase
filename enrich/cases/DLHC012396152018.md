**Deputy Director, Directorate of Enforcement, Delhi v. Axis Bank & Ors.** (R.K. Gauba, J., Delhi High Court, decided 02.04.2019; reserved 25.02.2019 / 27.03.2019). Five appeals under s.42 PMLA decided by one common judgment: Crl.A. 143/2018 (Axis Bank), 210/2018 (SBI), 623/2018 (IDBI Bank), 764/2018 (Union of India v. PNB, where the resolution professional of a corporate debtor also appeared) and 1076/2018 (ED v. PNB). LawBase holds the same full text under this case_id and under DLHC011608442018 (Crl.A. 764/2018). The record DLHC010007562018 (Crl.A. 143/2018) holds only an interim order of 08.02.2019, not this judgment.

**Holding** (paragraph numbers from the judgment).
- **Three kinds of "proceeds of crime"** (paras 104-111). Section 2(1)(u) covers (i) property derived or obtained from the criminal activity ("tainted property"); (ii) "the value of any such property"; and (iii) property of equivalent value where the tainted property is held abroad. Limbs (ii) and (iii) reach untainted property of the offender, which the Court calls "alternative attachable property" or "deemed tainted property". ED may attach it when the tainted property cannot be traced or is insufficient, but only up to a value near or equal to the proceeds of crime. The officer must assess that value, even tentatively, and revisit it as the investigation proceeds (paras 107, 110).
- **ED is not a creditor** (para 141). When the Government attaches proceeds of crime it "does not stand as a creditor", and the accused does not become a debtor. Proceeds of crime are not "revenues, taxes, cesses" or other Government dues, so the 2016 priority clauses for secured creditors (s.31B RDBA, s.26E SARFAESI) do not subordinate PMLA (paras 140-145).
- **Moratorium** (para 146). The s.14 IBC moratorium "cannot come in the way" of PMLA attachment. A contrary view would let an offender use proceeds of crime to discharge civil debts.
- **No overriding by RDBA, SARFAESI or IBC** (paras 147-148; conclusions (vi)-(viii) in para 171). The objects of the laws differ and there is no inconsistency. PMLA, by s.71, prevails in matters of money-laundering and proceeds of crime. Otherwise the laws must co-exist and be applied in harmony.
- **Bona fide third parties** (paras 149-165). A PMLA attachment is not illegal merely because a bank holds a prior charge, and the attachment does not by itself void that charge. Tainted property is attachable and confiscable even against a bona fide transferee for value, whose remedy lies against the wrongdoer (para 161). For deemed tainted property, a third party who acquired its interest bona fide and for adequate consideration may seek release (para 162). The date or period of the criminal activity is the cut-off: an interest acquired before it cannot have been meant to defeat PMLA (para 163). In that case the attachment stays valid but is confined to the value above the third party's claim (para 164). Where a secured creditor began enforcement before the attachment, the attachment "takes a back-seat" and the surplus goes to PMLA (para 165). An interest created around or after the criminal activity needs proof of due diligence as well (para 170). A charge may be declared void only on material that it was created to defeat PMLA (para 158; conclusion (x)).
- **Forum** (paras 167-170; conclusion (xvii)). Once confirmation of attachment is final, or confiscation is ordered, or the s.4 trial has begun, a third-party claim is decided only by the Special Court under s.8(7)/(8).
- **Burden** (conclusions (v) and (ix)). The accused, and a third party who acquired from the accused or through linked transactions, must prove the facts on which they seek release.
- **Result** (paras 172-176). ED's appeals were allowed. The Appellate Tribunal's orders, which had given the banks priority, were set aside and the matters were sent back to the Tribunal to apply these principles. The Court noted that the attached assets appeared to be deemed tainted property acquired before the criminal activity.

**IBC-PMLA use.**
- Cite para 141 and para 146 against any claim that the moratorium or the IBC waterfall suspends a PMLA attachment.
- Against lenders, first show that the property is tainted (paras 106, 161). If it is only "value" property, contest the date of the security against the period of the offence (para 163) and test the lender's due diligence (para 170).
- Value equivalence matters. Record a working figure for the proceeds of crime before attaching "value" property (paras 107, 110).
- The Delhi High Court in *Rajiv Chakraborty* (2022) quoted paras 141 and 161-165 and read them as giving ED no precedence over secured creditors on untainted property. Expect resolution professionals to cite those paragraphs.

**Status and later history.**
- An extract of the NCLAT Larger Bench decision in *Kiran Shah*, quoted in *Rajiv Chakraborty* (Delhi HC, 11.11.2022, para 89), records that the Supreme Court passed only a status quo order on 30.08.2019 and did not stay this judgment. The appeal's present stage is not verified. Treat the judgment as binding in Delhi but under challenge.
- The P&H High Court in *Seema Garg* (06.03.2020, PHHC011207472019) expressly declined to follow this judgment's reading of "value of any such property" (para 16 of that judgment). The split is not resolved.
- Followed in *Rajiv Chakraborty* (DLHC010046592020) and *Nitin Jain* (DLHC010126002021), and relied on by the NCLAT in *Value Wise Consultancy* (30.06.2026, EDPORTAL_18_2026-06-30, para 12).
- Wazalwar's IBC-PMLA compendium (2027 ed.) gives the supporting paragraphs as 105, 127-128 and 146-148. The corpus text shows the "not a creditor" holding at para 141; paras 105, 127 and 128 deal with other points. Cite from this text.
