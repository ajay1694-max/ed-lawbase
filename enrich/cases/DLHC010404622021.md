**Enforcement Directorate v. Mavent Restructuring Services LLP, Liquidator of PSL Ltd.**, LPA 512/2021 (Rekha Palli and Renu Bhatnagar, JJ., Delhi High Court, order dated 27.02.2025). The LawBase title is "ED v. Nitin Jain, Liquidator, PSL Ltd."

**What the order decided** (paras 1-4).
- ED appealed against the single-judge judgment of 15.12.2021 in W.P.(C) 3261/2021 (DLHC010126002021). That judgment had restrained ED from any further action against the liquidation estate or the corpus from the sale of liquidation assets (para 1).
- The Additional Solicitor General said that the assets found to be proceeds of crime had been sold and Rs. 274.60 crore was available with ED. He said, on instructions, that ED would transfer that sum with accrued interest to the liquidator, and asked that the liquidator use it strictly under the IBC (para 2).
- The liquidator's senior counsel assured the Court that the amount would be used to pay the victims of the fraud committed by the corporate debtor (para 3).
- The appeal was disposed of, "binding the parties to their respective stand". The Bench added that it had expressed no opinion on the merits of the rival claims and that the order "will not be treated as an expression of opinion on the questions of law raised in the present appeal" (para 4).

**Status.** This is a disposal on the parties' statements. It neither affirms nor reverses the single judge's view that s.32A bars PMLA attachment once the NCLT approves a liquidation measure. That judgment remains in force as a single-judge decision.

**IBC-PMLA use.**
- When a liquidator cites *Nitin Jain*, point out that the Division Bench left every question of law open. The single-judge view has not been tested in appeal.
- The order is a working example of a negotiated outcome: ED released realised proceeds to the liquidator, with an undertaking that the money would go to the fraud's victims. If a similar release is considered, record conditions that the money will reach victims or creditors under the IBC. Compare the conditional restorations in the Supreme Court consent orders in *V Hotels* (11.11.2025) and *Shio Charan* (2026) noted in the compendium (not in LawBase).
- Wazalwar's compendium (2027 ed.) reports this disposal accurately as leaving the questions of law open.
