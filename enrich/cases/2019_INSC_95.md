**Swiss Ribbons Pvt. Ltd. & Anr. v. Union of India & Ors.**, [2019] 3 SCR 535 : 2019 INSC 95, W.P.(C) No. 99 of 2018 (R.F. Nariman and Navin Sinha, JJ.; judgment by R.F. Nariman, J.; 25.01.2019). Paragraph numbers below follow the SCR text in the corpus. SCC numbering differs: *Arun Kumar Jagatramka* cites SCR para 52 as para 82.

**Held.**
- **Section 12A (paras 49-53).** Once admitted, a CIRP is a collective proceeding in rem, so the CoC must be consulted before an individual settlement (para 52). Before the CoC is constituted, a party may apply directly to the NCLT under Rule 11 of the NCLT Rules, 2016 (para 52). The 90% threshold is a matter of legislative policy. The CoC does "not have the last word": the NCLT and NCLAT can set aside an arbitrary rejection of a just settlement under s.60 (para 53).
- **No vested right (paras 64-65).** Following *ArcelorMittal*, a resolution applicant has no vested right to have its plan considered, so s.29A takes away no vested right.
- **Section 29A(c) is not limited to wrongdoing (paras 66-71).** A person need not be guilty of misconduct to be excluded. A person who cannot service its own debt beyond the grace period is, as a matter of legislative policy, unfit to be a resolution applicant (para 71). The proviso to s.35(1)(f), which bars sales in liquidation to persons ineligible under s.29A, is valid. "The legislative purpose which permeates Section 29A continues to permeate the Section when it applies not merely to resolution applicants, but to liquidation also." (para 69).
- **Related party (paras 73-76).** "Related party" and "relative" in s.29A(j) are read noscitur a sociis. They cover only persons "connected with the business activity of the resolution applicant" (para 75). A person who is in control while the plan is being implemented is a determinate "connected person" (para 76).
- **Result.** The financial/operational creditor distinction is upheld, and so is the s.53 waterfall (paras 82-84). The Code, "judged by the generality of its provisions", passes constitutional muster (para 85).

**IBC-PMLA use.**
- This is the base authority on the policy of s.29A. Persons who caused the default, and persons connected with them, are not to regain control through the process. Use paras 69 and 71 with *ArcelorMittal* (2018 INSC 935) and *Jagatramka* (2021 INSC 187) when a person linked to the erstwhile promoters tries to take back the corporate debtor or its assets, whether by plan or by purchase in liquidation.
- Para 53 helps in the other direction. NCLT review of CoC decisions under s.60 shows that a CoC vote is not final. ED can ask the NCLT to examine the source of funds behind a s.12A settlement. That application of para 53 is the compendium's view, not a holding.
- The judgment does not consider PMLA, s.32A (which came later) or fraudulent CIRP as such.

**After the 2026 Amendment.** Section 12A has been substituted. Withdrawal is now allowed only after the CoC is constituted and before resolution plans are invited, with 90% CoC approval. The direct Rule 11 route before the CoC is constituted (para 52) needs to be re-checked against the new text before it is cited. The new s.7(5), which makes admission mandatory, does not affect the reasoning on s.29A.

**Later history.** *Arun Kumar Jagatramka* (2021 INSC 187, paras 50-52 and 74; corpus) relies on this judgment for the purposive reading of s.29A and for the 12A withdrawal route. Per compendium, *Sterling Biotech* (NCLAT) relies on it on s.12A. The compendium also cites "para 59" for the NCLT's duty to guard against mala fide CIRP, as relied on in *Hytone*. SCR para 59 is about the RP verifying claims, so that reference is probably to SCC numbering and could not be verified.
