**Seema Garg v. Deputy Director, Directorate of Enforcement**, PMLA Appeal No. 1 of 2019 (O&M), with PMLA Appeals 2 and 3 of 2019 (Jaswant Singh and Sant Parkash, JJ., Punjab and Haryana High Court, 06.03.2020). These were appeals under s.42 against the Appellate Tribunal's order of 09.08.2019 confirming attachment.

**Facts** (para 2). The scheduled offence was a 2013 FIR for fraudulent VAT refunds by a proprietorship concern of Raman Garg. ED provisionally attached, on 13.12.2017, a Ludhiana plot bought in 1991 and mortgaged to a bank since 2009, and a flat bought in 2012. The owners were family members who were not accused in the FIR or in ED's complaint.

**Holding** (paragraph numbers from the judgment).
- **Time cap on attachment** (para 15). The confirmation order itself limited the attachment to 90 days during investigation, and ED could not argue against an order the appellants had appealed. The appellants were not accused in the FIR or in the complaint, so filing a complaint against others did not take away the time cap. Since even 365 days had passed with investigation pending, the attachment had ceased by operation of law.
- **"Value of any such property"** (para 16). Property bought before the scheduled offence is outside the first limb of s.2(1)(u). It falls within the third limb, whatever its date of purchase, only where the tainted property is taken or held abroad; the Court agreed with *Abdullah Ali Balsharaf* (Delhi) on this. The second limb ("value of any such property") cannot be read as "property of equivalent value". That reading would merge the second and third limbs, make the 2015 insertion of the third limb pointless, and empty ss.8(1), 8(6) and 24. "Value of such property" means property into which the proceeds have been converted, or which was obtained from them. The Court expressly declined to follow *Axis Bank* (Delhi) and agreed with the Andhra Pradesh High Court in *Satyam Computer Services v. DoE* (2019 (3) ALD 472). It held that "value of such property" excludes property with no direct or indirect link to the property derived from the crime.
- **Reasons to believe** (para 17). Section 5(1) requires specific reasons, on material, why the property is likely to be concealed, transferred or dealt with. Reproducing all three verbs of the section shows non-application of mind. On the facts (property bought in 1991, mortgaged since 2009, no attempt to sell), no reasons had been recorded.
- **New plea rejected** (para 18). ED's argument that the mortgaged property had secured the credit used in the offence was not raised below and was irrelevant to a VAT-refund fraud.
- **Summary and result** (paras 19-20). (i) Filing a complaint against others does not deprive a person of the 365-day cap while investigation is pending. (ii) Property acquired before the criminal activity, or before PMLA came into force, cannot be attached unless the tainted property is held abroad. (iii) Reasons must be specific. The appeals were allowed and the Tribunal's order set aside.

**IBC-PMLA use.**
- This is the main authority against attaching a borrower's or promoter's pre-offence assets as "value" property. It splits from *Axis Bank* (DLHC012396152018, paras 107-110), which allows such attachment. Inside the P&H High Court's territory, expect it to be followed; elsewhere it is persuasive only.
- In IBC matters lenders use the same split. Under *Axis Bank* they claim priority over "value" property; under *Seema Garg* they say such property cannot be attached at all.
- Whatever the limb, record reasons in the PAO that are specific to the property: a recent transfer, an encumbrance, a sale notice, and so on.

**Status and later history.**
- The Supreme Court has not resolved the conflict with *Axis Bank* (per compendium). Whether ED challenged this judgment in the Supreme Court is not verified; check before citing.
- Section 8(3)(a) has since been amended. Read para 15 with the current text.
- The compendium groups this case with a "Satyam line" (AP). The AP case this judgment follows is *Satyam Computer Services v. DoE* (2019 (3) ALD 472). *B. Rama Raju v. Union of India* (AP, 04.03.2011, HBHC010111342010) is a different judgment that upheld the provisions; it is not part of the narrow line.
