**M/s Arena Superstructures Pvt. Ltd. & Anr. v. State of U.P. & Anr.**, Writ-C No. 6041 of 2024, with Writ-C No. 8447 of 2024 (Mahesh Chandra Tripathi and Prashant Kumar, JJ., Allahabad High Court, reserved 25.11.2024, delivered 24.02.2025; Neutral Citation 2025:AHC:25736-DB). LawBase shows the decision date as 23.02.2025; the judgment itself says it was delivered on 24.02.2025.

**Facts.** Arena Superstructures (ASPL) was a sub-lessee in Noida's 2010-11 Sports City scheme. A financial creditor's s.7 petition was admitted on 29.10.2020. Homebuyers held 71.11% of the CoC vote. The NCLT approved a resolution plan on 19.07.2023, and the NCLAT declined interim relief to the Noida Authority. The petitioners (the corporate debtor and the resolution applicant) sought mandamus against Noida's refusal, on 01.11.2023, to revalidate the layout map that the plan needed (paras 20-35, 151-153).

**Holding** (paragraph numbers from the judgment).
- **Engineered insolvency** (paras 128-132, 189). The Court found that the builder collected about Rs. 387 crore from homebuyers, siphoned off much of it, did not pay Noida's dues, and "orchestrated insolvency" to escape civil and criminal consequences. Noida officials connived in this. The "moot question" is whether the company genuinely became insolvent, which may require piercing the corporate veil.
- **Proceeds of crime and ED** (para 145). Chargesheets had been filed against the directors and promoters under ss.120B and 420 IPC, which are scheduled offences. The Court held that the appropriate agency to investigate the transactions is the Enforcement Directorate.
- **s.32A and promoters** (paras 146-150). Relying on *Manish Kumar* and *Ajay Kumar Radheyshyam Goenka*, the Court held that s.32A protects only the corporate debtor under new management. Persons in charge who were involved remain liable. Letting promoters go free after plan approval would defeat the provision.
- **Finality of plan approval does not cover fraud** (paras 158, 182, 190). The Supreme Court's rulings on the binding effect of an approved plan (*Ghanshyam Mishra*, *Srei*) concern genuine insolvencies. They do not stop scrutiny of an insolvency that is "an outcome of fraud". *Fraus omnia vitiat* applied (para 191).
- **Guidance to the NCLT** (paras 189, 192). In an IBC proceeding against a builder, the NCLT or NCLAT should, before admitting it, satisfy itself that the proceeding is genuine and not filed at the builder's instance "to get away from civil/criminal liabilities after syphoning away the funds". The NCLT should check the balance sheets for this.
- **Consortium guidelines** (paras 184-187). Where a member of a consortium enters CIRP, the IRP and NCLT should first ask whether it can perform its share of the project, then offer the other members the option to complete it.
- **Directions** (paras 191, 194-196). The NCLT's s.7 admission order was not challenged, so the Court did not set it aside. Parties could place this judgment before the NCLT. The Court referred the matter to ED to investigate, trace the siphoned or laundered money, and bring it back into the company to meet the dues to Noida, the State and farmers. A copy was sent to the NCLT and the Director of ED. The writ petitions were disposed of.

**IBC-PMLA use.**
- Where a builder's CIRP follows or anticipates PMLA or police action and homebuyer funds are said to be diverted, cite paras 189 and 192 to ask the NCLT to examine whether the insolvency is genuine, and s.65 IBC (discussed at para 162) for fraudulent initiation.
- Cite paras 146-150 for the rule that s.32A does not protect promoters and directors.
- The judgment is itself a reference to ED. In a related matter, ED can rely on para 194 as the High Court's direction to trace siphoned funds.
- The guidance is given in a real-estate setting and on strong findings of fraud. It does not mean that the timing of a CIRP alone proves collusion.
