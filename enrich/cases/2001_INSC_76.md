**Solidaire India Ltd. v. Fairgrowth Financial Services Ltd. & Ors.**, [2001] 1 SCR 932 : 2001 INSC 76, Civil Appeal No. 3760 of 1995 (B.N. Kirpal, Ruma Pal and Brijesh Kumar, JJ.; judgment by Kirpal, J.). The SCR text is dated February 7, 2001. Old SCR reports have no paragraph numbers, so references are to SCR pages.

**Held.**
- **Interest (p. 935).** The Special Court's award of interest at the claimed rates was upheld.
- **Conflict of special Acts (pp. 935-938).** Both SICA, 1985 (s.32) and the Special Court (Trial of Offences Relating to Transactions in Securities) Act, 1992 (s.13) are special Acts with non obstante clauses. Where that is so, "it is the later Act which must prevail" (p. 936).
- The Court approved the Special Court's decision in *Bhoruka Steel*. The legislature is presumed to know of the earlier Act. Under the 1992 Act, the property of notified persons stands attached and is distributed only by the Special Court. Holding that the 1992 Act prevails lets both Acts work, because public money is recovered first even from a sick company.
- When Parliament wants an earlier law to continue, it says so expressly, as in s.34(2) of the RDB Act (p. 938). No such saving existed here. Recovery proceedings under the 1992 Act were not stayed by the pending BIFR proceedings.

**IBC-PMLA use.**
- RPs cite this case for the argument that the IBC (2016) prevails over PMLA (2002) because it is later. Answer as follows:
  - Parliament expressly dealt with the IBC-PMLA overlap in s.32A (2020). On this judgment's own logic, s.32A is where Parliament chose to protect the corporate debtor from PMLA, and only on its conditions.
  - The Supreme Court applies s.238 IBC only where there is an actual inconsistency (*National Spot Exchange*, 2025 INSC 694, paras 50-51; corpus).
  - PMLA confiscation is not a creditor's claim (*P. Mohanraj*, 2021 INSC 133, para 76; corpus).
- On its facts, the case upheld a special regime that attaches property to recover misappropriated public money against a rescue statute for sick companies. That is an analogy ED can draw. It is ED LawBase's own analysis, not a holding on IBC or PMLA.
- The compendium describes the later-Act rule as a presumption that gives way to the subject and object of the two Acts. That qualification is not stated in this judgment, which puts the rule firmly. Take any qualification from later Supreme Court decisions, after checking them, and not from this case.
- Compendium view (not a holding): courts have harmonised the IBC and PMLA rather than ranking one above the other.
