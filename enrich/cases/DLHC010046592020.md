**Rajiv Chakraborty, Resolution Professional of EIEL v. Directorate of Enforcement**, W.P.(C) 9531/2020 (Yashwant Varma, J., Delhi High Court, reserved 05.09.2022, pronounced 11.11.2022). The resolution professional of Era Infra Engineering Ltd. challenged two provisional attachment orders of 08.07.2020 and 05.08.2020, issued during CIRP, and their confirmation by the Adjudicating Authority on 01.01.2021 and 29.01.2021. Wazalwar's IBC-PMLA compendium gives the citation as 2022 SCC OnLine Del 3703 [?]; that citation is not verified here.

**Holding** (paragraph numbers from the judgment).
- **Writ entertained** (paras 11-12). ED's preliminary objections failed. ED had itself told the Court that the NCLT has no jurisdiction over PMLA attachments, so it could not send the RP back to the NCLT. NCLAT benches had also taken conflicting views.
- **ED is not a creditor** (paras 67-69). Attachment is not a step taken by a person to whom a debt is owed. It restrains the holder from enjoying or alienating proceeds of crime pending trial. The Court relied on *Axis Bank* (2019 SCC OnLine Del 7854), para 141.
- **Moratorium does not reach PMLA attachment** (paras 78-83, 93, 101). The moratorium preserves the estate against creditors' recovery actions. PMLA attachment is not recovery of a debt; it serves the public interest in confiscating crime proceeds. *P. Mohanraj* treated s.138 NI Act proceedings as substantially debt recovery, so it does not apply (paras 79-80). The Court held that "it would be incorrect to read Section 14 as completely shutting out actions under Sections 5 and 8 of the PMLA" (para 83). It approved the NCLAT line in *Varrsana Ispat*, *Rotomac Global* and the Larger Bench in *Kiran Shah*, and noted that *Manoj Kumar Agarwal* did not deal with *Varrsana Ispat* (paras 86-93).
- **Attachment does not transfer title** (paras 94-101). It only prevents private alienation. The property vests in the Union only on confiscation by the Special Court. So provisional attachment does not violate the primary objectives of s.14.
- ***B. Rama Raju* distinguished** (para 85). That judgment decided the validity of PMLA provisions and did not address the moratorium.
- **s.238 IBC is limited by s.32A** (paras 102-109). Both statutes are special laws with non obstante clauses, so the "later Act prevails" rule is not decisive. Section 32A, inserted in 2020, is Parliament's last word on how far PMLA gives way. PMLA attachment and confiscation stop only when a resolution plan is approved or a measure in aid of liquidation is adopted (para 105). Section 238 cannot be read to have "an impact far greater than that envisaged in Section 32A" (para 109).
- **No superior right for ED** (paras 110-113, 116). Following *Axis Bank* paras 149-165, a PAO "does not invest in that authority a superior or overriding right in property" (para 113). Questions of distribution and priority are to be settled independently and according to law. The RP may seek reliefs from the PMLA authorities.
- **Result** (paras 114-116). The writ petition was dismissed. The RP was left free to seek release of the attached properties according to law, and ED's rights were held to be limited to the extent recognised in this judgment and in *Axis Bank*.

**IBC-PMLA use.**
- This is the fullest Delhi High Court answer to an RP's moratorium, *P. Mohanraj* and s.238 arguments. Cite paras 80-83 and 93.
- Time attachments before the s.32A trigger events. After a plan is approved or a liquidation measure is adopted, para 109 treats PMLA attachment of the corporate debtor's property as barred, subject to the conditions of s.32A.
- Expect RPs and lenders to cite paras 112-113 and 116 to say that ED has no priority on attached property. Answer by showing that the property is tainted and not merely of equivalent value (see *Axis Bank* para 161).

**Later history** (per compendium, not verified unless stated).
- Applied in later Delhi High Court and tribunal decisions. ED relied on it, without success on the facts, before the NCLAT in *Vantage Point v. Gaurav Misra* (14.10.2025), which is under appeal in the Supreme Court.
- The NCLAT in *Value Wise Consultancy* (30.06.2026, EDPORTAL_18_2026-06-30, para 13) recorded that the Supreme Court has doubted *P. Mohanraj* in *Dineshchand Surana v. UCO Bank* and referred the question to a larger bench (verified from that order).

**Caution on the compendium's note.** The compendium says the petition "succeeded" to the extent of confirming s.32A insulation, and that the Court excluded promoters from s.32A protection. The judgment in the corpus dismisses the petition. Its s.32A discussion (paras 104-109) answers the s.238 argument, and it contains no ruling on promoters' personal liability. That point is decided in *Nitin Jain* (DLHC010126002021, para 101(Q)).
