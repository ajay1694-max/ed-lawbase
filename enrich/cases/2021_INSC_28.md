**Manish Kumar v. Union of India and Another**, [2021] 14 SCR 895 : 2021 INSC 28 (R.F. Nariman, Navin Sinha and K.M. Joseph, JJ.; judgment by K.M. Joseph, J.; 19.01.2021). The writ petitions challenged the IBC (Amendment) Act, 2020. The Section 32A part of the judgment is at paras 246-259 of the SCR text. Paragraph numbers below follow the SCR text in the corpus. The SCC OnLine numbering is different: SCR para 257 is quoted as para 280 in *P. Mohanraj* (2021 INSC 133, para 32).

**Held (Section 32A).**
- **Para 252 (s.32A(1)).** The corporate debtor's liability for a pre-CIRP offence ceases, and it cannot be prosecuted for that offence, only if four conditions are met:
  - a resolution plan is approved under s.31;
  - the plan results in a change in management or control;
  - the new controller is not a promoter, a person in management or control, or a related party; and
  - the new controller is not a person whom the investigating authority, on material, believes abetted or conspired in the offence and against whom it has filed a report or complaint.
- **Para 253.** The second proviso keeps every officer in default, and every person in charge of or responsible for the business, liable to prosecution and punishment. The same applies to every person associated with the corporate debtor who was directly or indirectly involved in the offence. In the Court's words, criminal liability "will continue to haunt" these persons.
- **Para 254 (s.32A(2)).** The bar on action against property applies only to property covered by the approved plan, and only where the same change-of-control conditions are met. "Action" includes attachment, seizure, retention or confiscation and is read widely. Under the Explanation, action against the property of any other person remains open.
- **Para 255.** The corporate debtor and the protected persons must still assist the investigating authority.
- **Paras 257-259.** Section 32A is not manifestly arbitrary and does not violate Articles 14, 19, 21 or 300A. The Court called it an economic measure that deserves deference. "It is not as if the wrongdoers are allowed to get away. They remain liable." (para 257). The immunity rests on conditions: "The new management cannot be the disguised avatar of the old management." (para 258).
- **Para 372.** All the impugned amendments were upheld. The only relief was limited Article 142 directions on court fees and limitation for allottees.

**IBC-PMLA use.**
- When a resolution applicant or RP pleads s.32A against a PMLA attachment or prosecution, test every condition in para 252 on the facts. The plea fails if any one condition is not met. A change of control that is only on paper, or one that passes to a related party or an accomplice named in a complaint, does not attract s.32A.
- Paras 253-254 are the authority for continuing to prosecute erstwhile promoters, directors and KMPs personally, and for attaching their own assets, whatever happens to the corporate debtor.
- The immunity covers only property "covered under the resolution plan". Assets outside the plan, and assets that have passed to third parties, remain open to attachment.
- Paras 250-251 record the Union's stand and the safeguards it relied on. Use them only as context. The binding reasoning is in paras 252-258.

**After the 2026 Amendment.** Section 32A is unamended. The new s.31(6) extinguishes pre-approval claims "against the corporate debtor and its assets". Whether a PMLA attachment is such a "claim" is an open question. Argue that s.32A remains the specific provision on offences and on action against property, and that *Manish Kumar* reads it as conditional.

**Later history (per compendium).**
- The compendium treats this judgment as the foundation for NCLAT rulings that s.32A operates automatically once its conditions are met (*Vantage Point*; *ED v. V Hotels*). It records that ED relied on the judgment in *Vantage Point*, but the argument failed on the facts.
- *P. Mohanraj* (2021 INSC 133, paras 32-33; corpus) quotes the judgment and explains that s.32A extinguishes liability, whereas a moratorium only suspends proceedings.
