**Nita Puri v. Union of India**, W.P.(C) 261/2025 (Sachin Datta, J., Delhi High Court, 28.08.2025). The petitioner is a former (suspended) director of Moser Baer India Ltd. (MBIL). She challenged the Ministry of Corporate Affairs order of 05.09.2024 under s.212(1)(c) of the Companies Act, 2013 directing the SFIO to investigate MBIL and its subsidiaries, joint ventures and associates. The compendium gives "2025:DHC:7433 [?]" as the neutral citation; not verified.

**Background** (paras 2-10). MBIL entered corporate debt restructuring in 2012 as a "Class B" borrower. It went into insolvency in 2017, with a commencement date of 14.11.2017. A special audit commissioned in CIRP (the Sikdar Report) found no preferential, undervalued, fraudulent or extortionate (PUFE) transactions; the CoC accepted it on 05.05.2018. A second forensic audit (the GSA Report) was given to the liquidator on 03.06.2019. A coordinate bench, on 29.02.2024, set aside Bank of Baroda's wilful-defaulter declaration based on the GSA Report, and a Division Bench dismissed the bank's appeal.

**Holding** (paragraph numbers from the judgment).
- **Wrong factual premise** (paras 35-41). The impugned order said PUFE transactions were "noticed" in the forensic audit reports. Neither report found any. An opinion formed on non-existent grounds "has no legs to stand on" (para 39).
- **Test for s.212(1)(c)** (paras 42-50). The Central Government's opinion is subjective, but the circumstances on which it rests must exist and be "demonstrable", and all relevant circumstances must be considered (*Barium Chemicals*, *Rohtas Industries*, *Rampur Distillery*, and the Bombay High Court in *Parmeshwar Das Agarwal*).
- **Cut-and-paste and ignored judgments** (paras 52-63). Parts of the order were lifted from the Sikdar Report without regard to its disclaimers. The judgments discrediting the audit reports were relevant material and were not considered.
- **No supplementing by affidavit** (paras 64-65). The order must stand on its own reasons, not on reasons added in the counter-affidavit (*Mohinder Singh Gill*; *Opto Circuit India v. Axis Bank*).
- **Result** (paras 70-72). A s.212(1)(c) order "is not a routine administrative measure". It must follow application of mind to all relevant circumstances, not "boilerplate language and/or extrapolations from third party documents" (para 71). The order of 05.09.2024 and all consequential proceedings were quashed.

**IBC-PMLA use.**
- This is a Companies Act case, not a PMLA case. It is likely to be cited by analogy against ED action begun long after a CIRP or liquidation, especially where the IBC-stage audits found no PUFE transactions.
- Before acting on IBC-stage forensic or transaction-audit reports, read what they actually find, and record any judgments or orders that have already commented on them.
- Record the reasons for an ECIR, summons or PAO in the order itself. *Opto Circuit* applies equally to PMLA (para 65).

**Caution on the compendium's note.** Wazalwar's compendium (2027 ed.) presents this as a case on a stale investigation reopened after insolvency without fresh material. The judgment does not rest on delay or on the end of insolvency. It quashes the order for a wrong factual premise and failure to consider relevant material (paras 39, 58, 63, 71). Cite it for that.
