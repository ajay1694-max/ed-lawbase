**Vijay Madanlal Choudhary v. Union of India**, [2022] 6 SCR 382 : 2022 INSC 757 (A.M. Khanwilkar, Dinesh Maheshwari and C.T. Ravikumar, JJ., 27.07.2022). Conclusions verified from the corpus text:

- **(x)** The challenge to the constitutional validity of s.19 is rejected. There are "stringent safeguards provided in Section 19".
- **(xi)** Section 24 "has reasonable nexus with the purposes and objects" of the Act and is not manifestly arbitrary.
- **(xii)** The proviso to s.44(1)(a) is directory and is read down to give the Special Court case-by-case discretion. The challenge to s.44 fails.
- **(xiii)** *Nikesh Tarachand Shah* did not remove s.45 from the statute book, and Parliament could cure the defect it found. The twin conditions as amended are sustained.

The headnote also covers the expansive meaning of "proceedings" and "investigation" in s.2(1)(na), and s.18. Read the full conclusion paragraph before citing any other point.

Later developments to check before citing:
- *Pankaj Bansal* (2023): grounds of arrest must be furnished in writing.
- *Tarsem Lal* (2024): no s.19 arrest after cognizance.
- Any pending review petitions.

**IBC-PMLA use.** The judgment does not consider the IBC. The IBC-PMLA compendium relies on it in four ways, all marked as compendium use and not as holdings on the IBC:
- PMLA is a self-contained penal statute with its own system of attachment, adjudication and appeal, and the IBC does not displace it.
- "Proceeds of crime" reaches property of equivalent value where the tainted property cannot be traced, which matters when tracing diverted funds of a corporate debtor.
- The ECIR is an internal document, and that position stays the same during a CIRP.
- Statements recorded under s.50 remain usable while the corporate debtor is in CIRP.
Read these together with *P. Mohanraj* (2021 INSC 133, para 76) and *Manish Kumar* (2021 INSC 28, paras 252-258).
