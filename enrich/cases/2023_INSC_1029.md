**Pavana Dibbur v. The Directorate of Enforcement**, [2023] 13 SCR 1049 : 2023 INSC 1029, Criminal Appeal No. 2779 of 2023 (Abhay S. Oka and Pankaj Mithal, JJ.; judgment by Abhay S. Oka, J.; 29.11.2023). The conclusions are at para 27.

**Facts.** The appellant was accused no. 6 in a PMLA complaint. She was not an accused in any of the predicate cases. She bought the first property in July 2013. The predicate offence (collection of about Rs. 107 crore from students) took place in 2017. She bought the second property in June 2019 from accused no. 1. In the charge-sheets filed in the predicate cases, the only offence from the Schedule was s.120-B IPC.

**Held (para 27).**
- **(a)** A person charged under s.3 PMLA need not be an accused in the scheduled offence (paras 15-16). Someone who later knowingly helps conceal or use the proceeds can be prosecuted "so long as the scheduled offence exists".
- **(b)** Such a person benefits if all accused in the scheduled offence are acquitted or discharged, or if the scheduled-offence proceedings are quashed (para 16).
- **(c)** "The first property cannot be said to have any connection with the proceeds of the crime as the acts constituting scheduled offence were committed after the property was acquired" (see also para 17).
- **(d)** Whether tainted money was used to buy the second property can be decided only at trial.
- **(e)** "The offence punishable under Section 120-B of the IPC will become a scheduled offence only if the conspiracy alleged is of committing an offence which is specifically included in the Schedule" (paras 22-25).
- **Result (paras 26 and 28).** The predicate charge-sheets alleged no conspiracy to commit a scheduled offence, so no scheduled offence existed. The complaint was quashed, but only as against the appellant.

**Note on the ground of decision.** The complaint was quashed on finding (e), because there was no scheduled offence at all. It was not quashed property by property. Do not cite this case as quashing a prosecution "to the extent" it rested on pre-offence property.

**IBC-PMLA use.**
- **Timing of acquisition.** Before attaching a corporate debtor's asset as proceeds of crime, check when the asset was acquired against the period of the scheduled offence. An asset acquired before the criminal activity is not proceeds of crime in the first sense of s.2(1)(u) (para 27(c)). To reach it, show later improvement, refinancing or other dealing with tainted funds, or proceed under the "value of any such property" limb on a proper basis. The judgment does not decide equivalent-value attachment.
- **Third parties.** Under para 27(a), persons who are not accused in the predicate case, such as group entities, nominees or a resolution applicant's connected persons, can be prosecuted under s.3 if they knowingly dealt with the proceeds.
- **Predicate check.** Before relying on a conspiracy-only predicate, confirm that the conspiracy alleged is to commit an offence in the Schedule. If it is not, the PMLA case against the corporate debtor and its officers is exposed to quashing under para 27(e).
- Corporate debtors and resolution applicants are likely to rely on finding (c) (per compendium).

**Later history (per compendium).** The compendium reads the case as supporting the timing-of-acquisition reasoning in *Seema Garg* (Punjab & Haryana HC).
