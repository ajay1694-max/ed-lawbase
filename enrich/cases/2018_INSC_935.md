**ArcelorMittal India Private Limited v. Satish Kumar Gupta & Ors.**, [2018] 12 SCR 362 : 2018 INSC 935, Civil Appeal Nos. 9402-9405 of 2018 (R.F. Nariman and Indu Malhotra, JJ.; judgment by R.F. Nariman, J.; 04.10.2018). This is the Essar Steel s.29A case.

**Held.**
- **Para 29.** The opening words of s.29A refer to a de facto as opposed to a de jure position. This is "a typical instance of a 'see through provision'", meant to identify the persons who are actually in control, whether jointly or in concert. The provision is given a purposive interpretation, not a literal one.
- **Para 43.** Ineligibility under s.29A(c) attaches "at the time of submission of the resolution plan". The 2018 amendment adding those words only clarified the existing position.
- **Paras 44-50.** "Control" in s.29A(c) means positive control, de jure or de facto, over management or policy decisions, whether through shareholding, agreements "or otherwise". A mere power to block special resolutions is not control.
- **Paras 56-57.** Because s.29A(c) is a see-through provision, care must be taken that persons in charge of the corporate debtor "do not come back in some other form to regain control of the company without first paying off its debts". Facts reasonably close to the date of submission "can always be seen" to decide whether persons are, in substance, avoiding the proviso.
- **Para 113.** Both applicants were ineligible. Because the law was being settled for the first time, the Court used Article 142 to give each of them two weeks to clear the NPAs of connected companies.

**IBC-PMLA use.**
- This is a s.29A case. It does not decide s.32A. The compendium uses it only by analogy. Section 32A asks whether the new controller is a promoter, a person in management or control, a related party, or an accomplice. The see-through test of control in paras 29 and 44-50, and the look-back to reasonably proximate facts in para 57, are the tools for testing whether a claimed change of control is genuine.
- Where shareholding has been rearranged or funds have been routed shortly before a plan is submitted, rely on para 57 to examine the arrangement as a whole.
- The Article 142 cure in para 113 was given once, because the law was new. It is not a general remedy.

**Later history.** *Swiss Ribbons* (2019 INSC 95, paras 64-65; corpus) relied on this judgment for the rule that a resolution applicant has no vested right to have its plan considered. *Arun Kumar Jagatramka* (2021 INSC 187, para 49; corpus) placed it in the line of purposive readings of s.29A.
