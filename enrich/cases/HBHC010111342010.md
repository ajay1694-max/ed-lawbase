**B. Rama Raju v. Union of India & Ors.**, W.P. Nos. 10765, 10769 and 23166 of 2010 (Goda Raghuram and R. Kantha Rao, JJ., High Court of Andhra Pradesh, 04.03.2011). The citation 2011 SCC OnLine AP 152 appears in footnote 35 of *Rajiv Chakraborty* (DLHC010046592020). The judgment has no numbered paragraphs; references below are to its issues and its "Summary of Conclusions".

**Facts.** The son and other relatives and associates of B. Ramalinga Raju (the Satyam accounting fraud) challenged ss.5(1), 8(1)-(4), 23 and 24 PMLA, as amended in 2005 and 2009, and the provisional attachment of 18.08.2009, confirmed on 14.01.2010.

**Holding** (Summary of Conclusions).
- **Issue A.** Property owned by or in the possession of a person other than one charged with a scheduled offence "is equally liable to attachment and confiscation" under Chapter III. Section 2(1)(u) is valid. The Court reasoned that offenders screen illegally acquired property in the names of relatives and associates.
- **Issue B.** The second proviso to s.5(1), in force from 06.03.2009, applies to property acquired before it came into force and is not invalid as retrospective punishment.
- **Issues C and D.** Section 8 is not invalid for vagueness, for uncertainty about onus and standard of proof, or for excluding mens rea. For attachment and confiscation, which the Court treated as civil and economic sanctions, "neither mens rea nor knowledge that a property has a lineage of criminality is either constitutionally necessary or statutorily enjoined". A non-accused holder of proceeds of crime has "a defeasible and not a clear title". Such a holder may still show, at the s.8(2) stage and later, that it acquired the property bona fide without knowledge of the criminality. Section 8(4), which requires possession to be taken on confirmation and before conviction, is valid.
- **Issue E.** The s.23 presumption for interconnected transactions is valid.
- **Issue F.** The s.24 burden of proving that proceeds of crime are untainted applies in both prosecution and Chapter III attachment and confiscation, but only to a person accused of an offence under s.3. It does not apply to a person not so accused. The s.23 presumption applies to both.
- **Result.** The writ petitions were dismissed. The petitioners were left free to pursue their statutory appeals on the merits.

**IBC-PMLA use.**
- Cite Issue A and Issues C-D to attach proceeds of crime held by relatives, associates or group companies without first proving their guilty knowledge. Be ready to meet a bona fide acquisition defence on evidence.
- *Rajiv Chakraborty* (para 85) refused to read this judgment as bringing PMLA attachment within the s.14 IBC moratorium. It decided only the validity of the provisions.
- On "value" property, this judgment does not address the narrow reading adopted in *Seema Garg* (P&H, PHHC011207472019). That narrow reading follows a different Andhra Pradesh judgment, *Satyam Computer Services v. DoE* (2019 (3) ALD 472).

**Status and later history.**
- Issue F has been overtaken by statute. Section 24 was substituted in 2013, and cl.(b) now lets the court presume, in the case of any other person, that the proceeds of crime are involved in money-laundering. Read Issue F with the present s.24 and *Vijay Madanlal Choudhary* (2022 INSC 757).
- Sections 5 and 8 have been amended several times since 2011. Check the current text before relying on the reasoning about particular provisos.
- Per compendium: broadly consistent with *Vijay Madanlal Choudhary* and still cited. Whether the petitioners took the matter to the Supreme Court is not verified.
