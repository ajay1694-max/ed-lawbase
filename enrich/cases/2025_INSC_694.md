**National Spot Exchange Limited v. Union of India & Ors.**, [2025] 7 SCR 252 : 2025 INSC 694, W.P.(C) No. 995 of 2019 (Bela M. Trivedi and Satish Chandra Sharma, JJ.; judgment by Bela M. Trivedi, J.). The SCR text gives the date as 15 May 2025. The corpus metadata shows 14.05.2025; rely on the text.

**Background (paras 2-8).** The NSEL payment default was about Rs. 5,600 crore. ED had attached about Rs. 1,740.59 crore under PMLA, and Maharashtra had attached about Rs. 8,548 crore under the MPID Act. On 04.05.2022 the Supreme Court acted under Article 142 and appointed a Supreme Court Committee (Justice Pradeep Nandrajog, retired). The Committee was empowered to execute NSEL's decrees and to sell judgment-debtors' properties "notwithstanding the attachment thereof" by ED under PMLA or by the State under the MPID Act. The Committee's order of 10.08.2023 held that secured creditors could claim no priority over attached property: for PMLA because the property was proceeds of crime, and for MPID because that Act overrides (para 5). Its order of 08.01.2024 held that MPID-attached property remained available despite IBC moratoria (para 7).

**Held.**
- **Article 142 (paras 13 and 19).** Article 142 is ordinarily not used against express statutory provisions, and it "cannot be used to achieve something indirectly what cannot be achieved directly". The secured creditors' objection to the 04.05.2022 order had, however, lost significance because that order had already been carried out.
- **Question (i): priority (paras 35-44).** PMLA falls under Entry 13 of List I (para 35). SARFAESI and the RDB Act fall under Entry 45 of List I ("Banking"), and the MPID Act under Entries 1, 30 and 32 of List II. These laws have separate fields, so SARFAESI and the RDB Act cannot override the MPID Act (paras 40-41). Deposits recovered under the MPID Act are not a "debt" under s.26E SARFAESI (para 43). Therefore "no priority of interest can be claimed by the Secured Creditors against the properties attached under the MPID Act" (para 44).
- **Question (ii): moratorium (paras 47-52).** Article 254 does not apply because the IBC (List III, Entry 9) and the MPID Act (List II) are under different Lists (para 47). Section 14 IBC concerns debtor-creditor relationships. MPID attachment "is beyond the realm of the Debtor-Creditor relationship" (paras 48-49). There is no inconsistency between the two Acts, so s.238 IBC is not attracted (paras 50-51). MPID-attached properties of judgment-debtors and garnishees remain available for execution by the Committee despite the moratorium (para 52).
- **Para 53.** Question (i) was answered in the negative and question (ii) in the affirmative. Both Committee orders were upheld.

**Scope note.** The Court framed question (i) to cover both PMLA and MPID attachments. Its reasoning and its express conclusion in para 44, however, deal with the MPID Act. On PMLA, the judgment does three things: it records PMLA's object and s.71 (para 23), places PMLA in Entry 13 of List I (para 35), and upholds the Committee's 10.08.2023 order, which had covered PMLA-attached property. Cite the PMLA point as resting on the upheld Committee order read with paras 23, 35 and 53. Do not present it as a separately reasoned holding. The judgment does not deal with a s.230 Companies Act scheme.

**IBC-PMLA use.**
- Against a secured lender claiming SARFAESI or RDB priority over attached property, use paras 43-44 and 53. The point is that recovery for defrauded depositors and investors is not a "debt" owed to the lender.
- Against an RP or NCLT treating a special-statute attachment as caught by the moratorium, use paras 48-51. The attachment is outside the debtor-creditor relationship, and s.238 IBC needs an actual inconsistency. The same reasoning helps for PMLA attachment, alongside *P. Mohanraj* (2021 INSC 133, para 76).
- The Article 142 committee was a remedy specific to this case. Paras 13 and 19 show that the Court itself treats such a route as exceptional. It does not replace the restitution route under s.8 PMLA.

**Later history (per compendium).** On 28.11.2025 the NCLT approved a s.230 scheme funded by 63 Moons to pay traders. In January 2026 the Bombay High Court, hearing a s.42 PMLA appeal, held that an NCLT-sanctioned scheme does not by itself override PMLA attachments or prosecutions. Neither order was checked in the corpus.
