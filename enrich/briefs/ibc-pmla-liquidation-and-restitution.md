## Short answer

- **Liquidation does not dissolve a PMLA attachment.** The NCLT has no power under s.60(5) IBC to revoke, recall or suspend it. The PMLA fora decide whether property is tainted. The NCLT decides how the debtor's legitimate assets are distributed. See *Value Wise Consultancy* (NCLAT, 30.06.2026) *(corpus: EDPORTAL_18_2026-06-30)*, *Kannan Tiruvangadam* (Cal HC, 2023) *(corpus: WBCHCA0048112023)* and *Nathella Sampath* (NCLT Chennai, 2024).
- **ED is not a creditor when it attaches.** The moratorium and the liquidation bar protect the estate against creditors' recovery. They do not reach a penal attachment of proceeds of crime. See *Axis Bank* (Del HC, 2019) paras 141 and 146 *(corpus: DLHC012396152018)* and *Rajiv Chakraborty* (Del HC, 2022) paras 67-69 and 78-83 *(corpus: DLHC010046592020)*. *P. Mohanraj* (SC) para 76 *(corpus: 2021_INSC_133)* endorses the *Axis Bank* reading. *Value Wise* applies it to both s.14 and the old s.33(5).
- **But attachment gives ED no priority over secured creditors.** A bona fide lender whose security predates the criminal activity keeps priority over untainted "value" property (*Axis Bank* paras 149-165). A PAO gives ED no "superior or overriding right", and priority is settled independently (*Rajiv Chakraborty* paras 110-113 and 116). ED's answer is to prove that the property is tainted itself (*Axis Bank* para 161).
- **Time fresh PAOs before the NCLT approves the liquidation measure.** *Nitin Jain* (Del HC, 2021) *(corpus: DLHC010126002021)* is adverse to ED. It rests solely on s.32A (para 101(H)). It held that the s.32A bar takes effect once the NCLT approves a measure under Regulation 32 of the Liquidation Regulations, such as a going-concern sale. From then the s.5 power "would cease to be exercisable" (para 98). ED's appeal was disposed of on consent: Rs 274.60 crore went to the liquidator for the fraud victims. The Division Bench expressed no view on the law *(corpus: DLHC010404622021)*, so the single-judge judgment stands untested.
- **Section 32A does not operate merely because liquidation has started.** *Mekaster* (NCLT Ahmedabad, 2025) and *Value Wise* para 15 both say so. Under s.32A(2), the property of a buyer in a liquidation sale is protected only if the statutory conditions are met. *Manish Kumar* (SC) para 254 *(corpus: 2021_INSC_28)* confirms that s.32A(2) covers liquidation purchasers on those conditions.
- **If a sale cannot be stopped, move the attachment onto the money.** Ask that the attachment be shifted to segregated sale proceeds held in a designated account. The order should record that it makes no finding on the merits of the attachment, and that the personal liability of directors is preserved. *Pankaj Dhanuka* (NCLT Kolkata, 2025) and the interim order in *Jasin Jose* (Ker HC, 2024) follow this model.
- **Release, restoration and restitution are for the Special Court under s.8(6), 8(7) and 8(8) PMLA, not for the NCLT.** IBBI Circular IBBI/CIRP/87/2025 of 04.11.2025 tells insolvency professionals to apply to the Special Court. The Circular is procedural and creates no new right. *Value Wise* para 14 quotes it.
- **Do not seek confiscation under s.8(7) while an appeal against the s.8(3) confirmation is pending.** *Nav Nirman* (SC, 06.02.2026) treats confiscation as embargoed until that appeal ends.
- **Consent orders can serve as templates, but they are not precedents.** *ED v. JSW* (11.12.2024), *Udaipur Entertainment* (10.10.2025), *ED v. V Hotels* (11.11.2025) and *Shiv Charan* (16.07.2026) were each made by consent on their own facts. Each preserved the prosecution of individuals. Never cite them as law on s.32A.
- **Secured creditors cannot claim priority over properties attached under the MPID Act.** *NSEL v. Union of India* (SC, 2025) *(corpus: 2025_INSC_694)* so holds. Its reasoning speaks to MPID attachment. It does not expressly rule on PMLA attachment; see the open points.
- **2026 Amendment.** The old s.33(5) is omitted. The liquidation moratorium is now s.33(1)(b), which imports s.14(1)(a) and (c). Section 32A is unchanged. The cases on why the moratorium does not reach a PMLA attachment should carry across to s.33(1)(b), but no court has yet said so.

## Part A. Liquidation (Chapter 13)

### A1. Does the moratorium protection survive into liquidation? (old s.33(5), now s.33(1)(b))

**Proposition.** At liquidation the s.14 moratorium gives way to the liquidation-stage bar. That was the old s.33(5). After the 2026 Amendment it is s.33(1)(b). Neither bar stops PMLA attachment.

- ***Value Wise Consultancy Pvt. Ltd. v. Deputy Director, ED*** — NCLAT Principal Bench, 30.06.2026, Comp. App. (AT) (Ins) Nos. 1226-1227 of 2022 *(corpus: EDPORTAL_18_2026-06-30)*. Status: recent; no later history known. **Pro-ED.**
  - **Facts.** The appellant was the liquidator of Siddhi Vinayak Logistics Ltd. ED had attached the debtor's assets before CIRP. During the moratorium, ED withdrew about Rs 2.29 crore from the debtor's bank account. During liquidation, it issued a fresh PAO over 6,170 vehicles, of which 1,344 were confirmed.
  - **Holding.** The NCLAT upheld the NCLT's refusal to entertain the liquidator's s.60(5) applications (paras 9-17).
  - **Reasoning.** Sections 14 and 33(5) are concerned with the debtor's lawfully acquired assets and with proceedings that could add to its civil debt. They do not reach penal proceedings over proceeds of crime (para 12). *Embassy Property* settles that IBC tribunals cannot entertain a challenge to an ED attachment (para 14). Only the PMLA mechanism can deal with attachment, including notices under s.50 PMLA (para 16).
  - **Note on s.32A.** The Tribunal said it did not need to discuss s.32A because s.32A "operates only where there is a successful insolvency resolution process" (para 15).
- ***Nitin Jain, Liquidator of PSL Ltd. v. ED*** — Delhi HC (Yashwant Varma J.), 15.12.2021, W.P.(C) 3261/2021, 2021 SCC OnLine Del 5281 *(corpus: DLHC010126002021)*. Status: the single-judge judgment stands. ED's LPA was disposed of on 27.02.2025 on the parties' statements, with no opinion on the law *(corpus: DLHC010404622021)*. **Adverse.**
  - **Facts.** PSL Ltd. was in liquidation. The NCLT approved a going-concern sale for Rs 425.50 crore on 08.09.2021. ED issued a PAO for Rs 274.60 crore on 02.12.2021, after that approval.
  - **Common ground.** The Court treated the IBC and PMLA as working in separate fields, and harmonised them (para 101(C)-(G)).
  - **Ratio.** The reconciliation turns "solely on the anvil of Section 32A" (para 101(H)). The trigger is the Adjudicating Authority's approval of the liquidation measure under Regulation 32 of the Liquidation Regulations. From that point the s.5 power to attach "would cease to be exercisable" (para 98; para 101(R)-(T)).
  - **Order.** ED was restrained from acting against the liquidation estate and against the sale corpus (para 102).
  - **What the Court preserved.** The bar protects only the debtor's property. It does not protect the persons in charge (paras 98 and 101(Q)). The liquidator must still give PMLA investigators information and documents (para 100).
- **How ED should use these.**
  - Lead with *Value Wise* and *Kannan Tiruvangadam* (A4) on maintainability.
  - When *Nitin Jain* is cited, answer with four points:
    - The restraint applies only after the NCLT approves a liquidation measure. It does not apply from the liquidation commencement date.
    - It protects only the debtor's property, never individuals.
    - The Division Bench left the law open.
    - *Value Wise* takes a narrower view of s.32A in liquidation.

> **Corpus and compendium disagree.** The compendium says *Nitin Jain* rested on harmonious construction "not on Section 32A". The judgment says the opposite: para 101(H) makes s.32A decisive, and para 101(R)-(T) applies it. Cite the judgment.

### A2. Does it matter whether the attachment came before or after the liquidation commencement date?

**Proposition.** The PMLA forum decides taint. Once taint is decided, the NCLT decides how assets are applied. A liquidator may be given interim custody, but that custody does not dilute the attachment.

- ***Anil Goel, Liquidator of REI Agro Ltd. v. Deputy Director, ED*** — NCLT Kolkata, 31.08.2018, C.A.(IB) 453/KB/2018 in C.P.(IB) 73/KB/2017. Not in corpus. Status: NCLT, persuasive. **Mixed.**
  - The NCLT held that only the PMLA court can decide whether attached property is proceeds of crime. The NCLT applies the debtor's assets once the PMLA forum has ruled.
  - Meanwhile, it allowed the liquidator to take possession for administration.
- **Timing cases.**
  - *Value Wise* upheld a refusal to interfere with a PAO issued during liquidation (A1).
  - *Nitin Jain* draws the line later in the process, at NCLT approval of the sale.
  - *Anil Kohli* (NCLAT, 2025) looks behind the date of the PAO to the date of the investigation (A7).
- **How ED should use these.** If the NCLT is inclined to give the liquidator possession, ask that the order record two things: possession does not affect the attachment, and it does not affect the question of taint.

### A3. What forms the liquidation estate under s.36?

**Proposition.** A going-concern sale in liquidation can go ahead despite a live attachment if the share of the price attributable to the attached assets is held apart. This is an accommodation on the facts. It is not a rule.

- ***Pankaj Dhanuka v. Deputy Director, ED (Corporate Power Ltd.)*** — NCLT Kolkata, 24.07.2025, I.A.(IB) 162/KB/2025 in C.P.(IB) 23/KB/2019. Not in corpus. Status: NCLT, persuasive. **Mixed.**
  - **Facts.** The attachment was made after CIRP began and continued into liquidation.
  - **Holding.** The NCLT allowed an "as is where is" going-concern sale.
  - **Conditions.** The proceeds attributable to the attached assets were to be kept in a segregated account pending adjudication. The order did not review the attachment. Directors' personal liability was preserved.
- ***Sundaresh Bhatt, Liquidator of ABG Shipyard v. CBIC*** — SC, 26.08.2022, (2023) 1 SCC 472. Not in corpus. Status: good law; not a PMLA case. **Neutral, and will be cited against ED.**
  - Customs may assess duty during the moratorium, but may not recover it by selling the debtor's goods.
  - Goods held by customs form part of the s.36 estate, and the customs claim ranks under s.53.
- **How ED should use these.** Distinguish *Sundaresh Bhatt*. Customs there was a creditor recovering a debt. ED does not act as a creditor when it attaches. *P. Mohanraj* para 76 *(corpus: 2021_INSC_133)* quotes *Axis Bank* (paras 139, 141) and agrees that confiscation of proceeds of crime is done by the government "acting statutorily and not as a creditor".

### A4. Can the liquidator take possession of attached assets, sell them or transfer them?

**Proposition.** No. The NCLT cannot revoke a s.5(1) attachment under s.60(5), and a liquidation order has no effect on the attachment.

- ***S. Palaniappan [?], Liquidator of Nathella Sampath Jewellery Pvt. Ltd. v. Joint Director, ED*** — NCLT Chennai, 25.01.2024, M.A. 30(CHE)/2021 [?] in C.P. 129/IB/2018 [?]. Not in corpus. Status: NCLT. **Pro-ED.**
  - The NCLT held that a PMLA attachment is governed by the PMLA alone, so a s.60(5) application to revoke it is not maintainable.
  - The liquidation order did not touch the attachment.
- ***Kannan Tiruvangadam (Liquidator, Jas Infrastructure & Power Ltd.) v. Union of India*** — Calcutta HC (Rajasekhar Mantha J.), 28.06.2023, W.P.A. 2343 of 2023 *(corpus: WBCHCA0048112023)*. Status: no later history known. **Pro-ED.** The PAO and its confirmation came before liquidation, and ED had already applied to the Special Court for confiscation. The Court dismissed the liquidator's writ, which sought leave to ignore the attachment and sell.
  - **Forum.** Relief against the attachment could be sought only before the Special Court, or the Supreme Court (paras 26-27).
  - **Conduct.** The Court noted that no secured creditor supported the petition. It doubted the liquidator's bona fides and said it could not rule out that the promoters were behind the petition (paras 29-32). It also recorded that ED had never been notified of the NCLT proceedings (paras 7 and 31).
- ***Liquidator of Devas Multimedia Pvt. Ltd. v. ED*** — Karnataka HC, 26.07.2021, W.P. 12118/2021 *(corpus: KAHC010229702021)*. **Neutral.**
  - This was a Companies Act winding up, not an IBC liquidation.
  - ED did not object to releasing the interest earned on the attached sum. The Court allowed the liquidator's s.8(7) application and directed release of the interest to the Official Liquidator, subject to the NCLT's supervision.
  - It shows that the s.8(7) route through the Special Court works for liquidators.
- **How ED should use these.**
  - Object to maintainability in every liquidator's application under s.60(5).
  - Where ED was never served in the CIRP or liquidation, say so. *Kannan* treats that as a point against the liquidator.

### A5. Can a going-concern sale in liquidation attract s.32A?

**Proposition.** Section 32A(1) protects the corporate debtor from prosecution only after approval of a s.31 resolution plan with a genuine change of control. Section 32A(2) is wider. It also protects property bought in a liquidation sale, but only by a buyer who meets the same eligibility conditions.

- ***Manish Kumar v. Union of India*** — SC (R.F. Nariman J.), 19.01.2021, (2021) 5 SCC 1; [2021] 14 SCR 895 *(corpus: 2021_INSC_28)*. Status: binding. **Mixed.**
  - The Court read s.32A(1) as subject to cumulative conditions. These are: an approved plan; a change in management or control; and a new controller who is not a promoter, not a related party, and not a person the investigating agency has reported as an abettor or conspirator (para 252).
  - Under the second proviso, the officers and persons in charge stay liable (para 253).
  - Section 32A(2) protects the debtor's property, and also the property of a person who buys it in the CIRP or liquidation, if the same conditions are met (para 254).
  - The Court upheld s.32A because "the wrongdoers" are not let off: "They remain liable" (para 257).
- ***Chandra Prakash Jain, Liquidator of Mekaster Engineering Ltd. v. ED*** — NCLT Ahmedabad, 04.02.2025, I.A. 331/2020 in CP(IB) 46/2017. Not in corpus. Status: NCLT. **Pro-ED.**
  - The NCLT refused to de-freeze a receivable of Rs 4,04,98,451 owed by HPCL to the debtor.
  - Its reasons:
    - Section 60(5) gives jurisdiction, but it does not give the liquidator a right to release.
    - Section 32A cannot be used just because liquidation has begun.
    - The offence came before the insolvency.
    - The liquidator did not answer ED's evidence that the assets were funded from proceeds of crime.
- ***C.A. Jasin Jose, Liquidator v. ED*** (Atlas Gold Township) — Kerala HC, interim order 12.04.2024; writ dismissed as withdrawn 17.12.2024, 2024:KER:95794 [?]. Not in corpus. Status: interim only. **Mixed.** The Court did not decide the merits. It lifted the attachment from the property to allow the sale, and moved it onto the sale proceeds held in a designated account, with notice to ED.
- **How ED should use these.**
  - Claims of s.32A(1) immunity in a liquidation should fail on the text.
  - For claims under s.32A(2), test the buyer against all the *Manish Kumar* para 252/254 conditions.
  - **Open point.** *Nitin Jain* treats the NCLT's approval of the sale as the trigger. *Value Wise* para 15 says s.32A operates only on a successful resolution.

### A6. A confirmed attachment or confiscation, the liquidation sale, and s.53 distribution

**Proposition.** Attached property is not a claim to be ranked in the s.53 waterfall. It is property that the State says is not the debtor's to distribute. The tax and customs cases deal with creditor claims and do not apply.

- ***State Tax Officer v. Rainbow Papers Ltd.*** — SC, 06.09.2022, (2023) 9 SCC 545. Not in corpus. Status: confined by *Paschimanchal*, and reversed for the future by the 2026 Amendment (see Part C). **Neutral.** The Court held that a statutory charge under the Gujarat VAT Act made the State a secured creditor.
- ***Paschimanchal Vidyut Vitran Nigam Ltd. v. Raman Ispat Pvt. Ltd.*** — SC, 17.07.2023, 2023 SCC OnLine SC 842. Not in corpus. Status: good law. **Neutral.** *Rainbow Papers* turned on its own statute and does not give priority to statutory dues generally. Distribution follows s.53.
- ***Deputy Director, ED v. Axis Bank*** — Delhi HC (R.K. Gauba J.), 02.04.2019, 2019 SCC OnLine Del 7854; (2019) 259 DLT 500 *(corpus: DLHC012396152018)*. Status: under appeal. The Supreme Court passed only a status quo order on 30.08.2019 and did not stay the judgment. *Seema Garg* (P&H) declined to follow its reading of "value of any such property". **Pro-ED on the moratorium; mixed on lenders.** (The LawBase record DLHC010007562018 holds only an interim order and should not be cited.)
  - **ED is not a creditor, and the moratorium does not apply.** The State attaching proceeds of crime "does not stand as a creditor" (para 141). The s.14 moratorium cannot stop a PMLA attachment (para 146).
  - **Attachment and secured interests.** A PMLA attachment is lawful even over property carrying a prior charge (para 149). Property that is tainted in itself can be attached and confiscated even against a bona fide transferee for value (para 161).
  - **Lenders who took security before the crime.** A bona fide third party who acquired an interest before the criminal activity is not defeated by attachment of untainted "deemed tainted" property. The date of the criminal activity is the cut-off (paras 162-163).
  - **Where the lender sued first.** If that party began enforcement before the attachment, the attachment stays valid but "takes a backseat". ED gets any surplus (paras 164-165).
- **How ED should use these.**
  - Keep proceeds of crime that are tainted in themselves apart from equivalent-value property. The *Axis Bank* protection for lenders reaches only the latter, and only for security taken before the crime.
  - Where segregated proceeds are later distributed after release, *Paschimanchal* governs the ranking.

### A7. Auction purchasers and clean title

**Proposition.** A buyer cannot get better title than the estate had. Courts look at when the investigation began, not just at the date of the PAO.

- ***Anil Kohli, RP of Dunar Foods Ltd. v. ED*** — NCLAT, 03.07.2025 [?], CA(AT)(Ins) 389/2018, (2025) ibclaw.in 470 NCLAT. Not in corpus. Status: NCLAT; the digest says other benches have followed it. **Pro-ED.**
  - Assets attached after CIRP began, in an investigation that started before CIRP, are not available for resolution.
  - Section 238 does not override PMLA for proceeds of crime.
  - Section 32A does not operate in advance, on admission.
- **How ED should use it.** Plead the date of the investigation, and the link of each asset to it. The compendium itself notes that the date of the investigation is not enough without that link.

### A8. Dissolution while PMLA proceedings are pending

**Proposition (LawBase analysis).** No reported case in this set deals directly with dissolution of a debtor whose assets remain under attachment. The working position has three parts:
- Attached assets do not enter the estate, so they are not "distributed" before dissolution.
- Dissolution does not end the prosecution of individuals. The compendium relies here on *Manish Kumar* and on *Ajay Kumar Radheyshyam Goenka* (SC, 15.03.2023).
- Once a s.32A(2)-compliant liquidation sale has taken place, the bar on action against the property sold applies.

- **Siva Industries and Holdings Ltd.** — NCLT Chennai, 12.08.2021. Not in corpus. Status: **reversed** by the Supreme Court in *Vallal RCK v. Siva Industries* (03.06.2022, (2022) 9 SCC 803). **Neutral.** The NCLT rejected a 94.23% CoC-approved settlement under s.12A as open-ended and ordered liquidation. The Supreme Court restored the settlement even though liquidation had begun. The compendium places the case here only because the rejection led to liquidation. For its treatment, see the brief on fraudulent CIRP and settlement.
- **How ED should use this.** Before dissolution, ask the NCLT to record three things: attached assets were outside the estate; ED's rights over them continue; and the prosecution of individuals is unaffected.

## Part B. Release, restoration and restitution (Chapter 14)

### B1. Release, restoration and restitution under s.8(6), 8(7) and 8(8) PMLA

**What the statute says.** The text below was checked against the PMLA in LawBase.
- **Section 8(6).** After trial, if the Special Court finds there was no money-laundering, or that the property is not involved in it, the property is *released* to the person entitled to it.
- **Section 8(7).** Where the trial cannot be held, or has begun but cannot be concluded, the Special Court orders *confiscation or release*. It acts on an application by the Director or by a person claiming possession of property covered by a s.8(3) order. The listed reasons are the death of the accused, the accused being a proclaimed offender, or "any other reason".
- **Section 8(8).** After confiscation under s.8(5), the Special Court may direct *restoration* to a claimant with a legitimate interest who suffered a quantifiable loss. The claimant must have acted in good faith, taken reasonable precautions, and not been involved in the offence. The second proviso allows the claim to be considered *during the trial*. Rule 3A of the Prevention of Money-laundering (Restoration of Property) Rules, 2016 governs restoration during trial.

**Compendium view (not a holding).** The compendium describes s.8(7) as interim relief where withholding the property would cause "undue hardship". It describes restitution under the s.8(8) second proviso as needing ED's consent. Neither description matches the text of s.8 above. Check the current text of Rule 3A before asserting any consent requirement.

- ***Kalyani Transco v. Bhushan Power and Steel Ltd.*** — SC (Gavai CJI, S.C. Sharma and K.V. Chandran JJ.), 26.09.2025, 2025 INSC 1165. Not in corpus. Status: final. The judgment of 02.05.2025 (2025 INSC 621) was recalled on 31.07.2025. **Mixed.**
  - The Court explained the object of s.32A (paras 116-117).
  - It treated ED's pending attachment as one reason why the delay in implementing the plan was not JSW's fault (paras 118-124).
  - Para 2.22 records that the 11.12.2024 order (B3) expressly reserved the s.32A question.
  - The compendium uses it as the threshold authority that restitution must go to the Special Court. Its own note admits that the case does not decide NCLT jurisdiction over a PMLA attachment. Use *Embassy Property* and *Value Wise* for that.
- **How ED should use this.** Keep three things separate: s.32A immunity under the IBC, restitution under s.8(8) PMLA ordered by the Special Court, and the prosecution of individuals. Restitution never ends personal liability.

### B2. IBBI Circular IBBI/CIRP/87/2025 dated 04.11.2025

**Proposition.** The Circular was issued under s.196 IBC. It advises insolvency professionals that, where ED has attached the debtor's assets, they may apply to the Special Court under s.8(7) or 8(8) PMLA. The application must carry a standard undertaking that IBBI framed in consultation with ED. The Circular is procedural and creates no substantive right.

- *Value Wise* para 14 *(corpus: EDPORTAL_18_2026-06-30)* quotes para 2 of the Circular. The NCLAT relied on it to confirm that IBC tribunals are not the forum for any plea against ED.
- **How ED should use it.**
  - Insist that every restitution request goes to the Special Court with the IBBI undertaking.
  - Check the stage the s.8 proceedings have reached (B6).
  - Oppose any use of the Circular to sidestep scrutiny under the PMLA.

### B3. Stage one: during CIRP, before a plan is approved

**Proposition.** Restitution sought during CIRP is the clearest case for the Special Court. The RP is in office, and the CoC is the body with the interest.

- ***ED v. JSW Steel Ltd.*** (the BPSL handover order) — SC, 11.12.2024, CA 3362/2020 and connected. Not in corpus. Status: made on ED's affidavit; it gave no view on s.32A or on ED's power to attach during CIRP. **Neutral.** The Court directed ED to hand the debtor's attached properties to the successful resolution applicant (SRA), treating the handover as restitution under s.8(8) read with Rule 3A.
- ***Directorate of Enforcement v. V Hotels Ltd.*** (Cox & Kings group) — SC, 11.11.2025, Crl. Appeal 2925/2025. Not in corpus. Status: consent order; expressly non-precedential; questions of law left open. **Mixed.**
  - The Court confirmed the setting aside of the PAO and restored the properties to the SRA under the second proviso to s.8(8).
  - The debtor was dropped from prosecution under s.32A. Former directors and abettors stay prosecuted.
  - ED has liberty to act if the SRA proves to be connected to the old management.
  - The compendium places this here as an illustration. On the facts, it arose after the plan had been approved.

> **Internal inconsistency in the compendium.** Chapter 14 records the 11.11.2025 order as a consent order on the s.8(8) second proviso. Appendix A says the Court held that ED had "no lien" because the plan had been substantially implemented, and released deposits with interest. We have not seen the order. Read it before relying on either description.

### B4. Stage two: after the plan is approved and the RP has left office

**Proposition.** Once the plan is approved, the RP's office ends. The SRA, or a monitoring committee, is then the party that applies. The date and service of the PAO relative to plan approval can decide the outcome.

- ***V Hotels Ltd. v. ED*** — Bombay HC, 26.11.2024 or 28.11.2024 [?], 2024:BHC-OS:20171-DB [?]. Not in corpus. Status: superseded by the Supreme Court consent order, which did not affirm the High Court's reasoning. **Neutral.**
  - The Court examined a disputed timeline. The email said to carry the PAO had bounced, and there were signs that the PAO was actually issued after the plan was approved.
  - The compendium records no final ruling on the substantive question at this stage.
- **How ED should use this.** Keep exact records of the date a PAO issued, how it was sent, and proof of service. Where plan approval is close, serve by more than one mode.

### B5. Who may apply: the SRA, monitoring committee, liquidator, creditors and others

- ***Udaipur Entertainment World Pvt. Ltd. v. Union of India*** (Royal Raj Villas) — SC, 10.10.2025. Not in corpus. Status: consent order; expressly non-precedential; questions of law left open. **Mixed.**
  - On ED's affidavit, the PAO was partly set aside, and units were restored to the SRA for genuine homebuyers under the second proviso to s.8(8).
  - The attachment continued over eleven identified units.
  - The debtor was dropped from prosecution under s.32A, while the individuals stayed prosecuted. ED kept its liberty to act.
- ***Shiv Charan*** — Bombay HC, 01.03.2024; SC, CA 9692-9693/2024, 16.07.2026. Not in corpus. Status: the SC disposed of the appeal by consent, held the order non-precedential, and left all questions open. The HC's reasoning was neither affirmed nor reversed. **Mixed.**
  - Bank balances and fourteen flats were restored to the SRAs as restitution under the s.8(8) second proviso.
  - The SC did not decide whether s.32A by itself cancels an attachment made before CIRP.
- **Liquidators.**
  - In *Nitin Jain* (LPA), ED stated that it would transfer Rs 274.60 crore of sale proceeds of assets it had found to be proceeds of crime. The liquidator assured the Court that the money would be used to pay the victims of the debtor's fraud (paras 2-4) *(corpus: DLHC010404622021)*.
  - *Devas* (A4) shows a liquidator using s.8(7).
- **How ED should use these.**
  - The consent template has five parts:
    - ED's affidavit; no finding on the merits.
    - An SRA that is unconnected and has not benefited from proceeds of crime.
    - Attachment retained over the specific assets that warrant it.
    - The prosecution of individuals preserved.
    - A liberty clause allowing ED to act if the facts change.
  - Never let the template be presented as law.

### B6. Forum, procedure, undertaking and evidence before the Special Court

- ***Nav Nirman Builders & Developers Pvt. Ltd. v. Union of India*** — SC (M.M. Sundresh and N.K. Singh JJ.), 06.02.2026, 2026 INSC 130. Not in corpus; LawBase has only the earlier Jharkhand HC stage, JHHC010348042022. Status: binding. **Adverse to ED on sequence.**
  - Once the s.8(3) confirmation is challenged before the Appellate Tribunal, the High Court or the Supreme Court, the appellate order replaces it.
  - Confiscation under s.8(7) must wait until that challenge ends. The Court set aside the confiscation order and kept the s.8(7) proceedings pending.
- ***Rajiv Chakraborty, RP of Era Infra Engineering v. ED*** — Delhi HC (Yashwant Varma J.), 11.11.2022, W.P.(C) 9531/2020, 2022/DHC/004739 *(corpus: DLHC010046592020)*. Status: good law in Delhi. The RP's writ was **dismissed** (para 114). **Pro-ED on the moratorium; adverse on priority.**
  - ED is not a creditor (paras 67-69). It would be wrong to read s.14 as shutting out action under ss.5 and 8 PMLA (para 83). *P. Mohanraj* does not apply (paras 79-80). Attachment transfers no title (paras 94-101).
  - Section 238 IBC reaches PMLA only as far as s.32A provides. Attachment and confiscation stop once a plan is approved or a liquidation measure is adopted (paras 105 and 109).
  - The RP may seek release from the PMLA authorities (paras 113 and 115).
  - A PAO gives ED no "superior or overriding right" in the property, including against secured creditors. Questions of distribution and priority are settled independently (paras 110-113 and 116).
  - The judgment does not rule on promoters' personal liability. For that point, use *Nitin Jain* para 101(Q) and *Manish Kumar* para 253.
- **How ED should use these.**
  - Check whether any appeal against confirmation is pending before moving for confiscation.
  - Insist that the applicant files the IBBI undertaking.
  - Require proof of legitimate interest, and of good faith under s.8(8).
  - Give notice to the CoC during CIRP, or to known creditors in liquidation.

### B7. Restitution pending trial, and after conviction, acquittal or closure

- ***Three C Green Developers Pvt. Ltd. v. Three C Infratech Pvt. Ltd.*** — NCLT New Delhi Bench-II, 12.03.2025, C.P.(IB) 617/ND/2022, (2025) ibclaw.in 387 NCLT. Not in corpus. Status: NCLT. **Pro-ED.**
  - In the admission order itself, the NCLT directed that the moratorium would not apply to land ED had already attached.
  - It also preferred an independent IRP to the one proposed by the group-company creditor.
- **Statute.**
  - Before the trial ends, restitution lies under the second proviso to s.8(8) read with Rule 3A.
  - After an acquittal or a finding of no money-laundering, release lies under s.8(6).
  - Where the trial cannot be held or concluded, the route is s.8(7).
  - After confiscation, restoration lies under s.8(8).
- **How ED should use this.** At admission, ask the NCLT to record that property ED has already attached is outside the moratorium.

### B8. Stage three: restitution in liquidation, and the effect on distribution

- ***Nishit Badola v. Union of India*** (Three C Shelters) — Delhi HC, 20.07.2026, W.P.(C) 174/2024, 2026:DHC:5770. Not in corpus. Status: disposed of as satisfied. **Neutral.**
  - Supreme Court orders (20.11.2025 and 06.02.2026) and a Special Court order (27.11.2025) had directed restitution and monetisation of attached assets for the financial creditors.
  - The writ became redundant and was disposed of.
- *Pankaj Dhanuka* (A3) is the liquidation-sale template. *Nitin Jain* (LPA) shows a negotiated transfer of sale proceeds.
- ***ED v. Manoj Kumar Agarwal*** (Sterling SEZ) — NCLAT, 09.04.2021. Status: **disapproved** by the NCLAT Larger Bench in *Kiran Shah v. ED* (CA(AT)(Ins) 817/2021). *Rajiv Chakraborty* and *Value Wise* both record this. **Adverse; historical only.** It had held that attached property becomes available to the IBC process once CIRP begins.

### B9. Parallel State investor-protection law: the MPID Act

- ***National Spot Exchange Ltd. v. Union of India*** — SC (Bela M. Trivedi and S.C. Sharma JJ.), 15.05.2025, 2025 INSC 694; [2025] 7 SCR 252 *(corpus: 2025_INSC_694; the LawBase metadata shows 14.05.2025, but the judgment text reads 15 May 2025)*. Status: binding. **Pro-ED in effect.**
  - **Question (i).** The Court framed it as whether secured creditors have priority over assets attached under *the PMLA and the MPID Act*. It answered in the negative.
    - The MPID Act is State-list legislation, so the SARFAESI and RDB Acts cannot override it (paras 40-41).
    - Depositors' money is not a "debt" under s.26E SARFAESI (para 43).
    - "No priority of interest can be claimed by the Secured Creditors against the properties attached under the MPID Act" (para 44).
  - **Question (ii).** Property attached under the MPID Act is available to the Supreme Court's committee despite the s.14 moratorium. There is no inconsistency with the IBC, so s.238 does not come into play (paras 48-52).
  - **Article 142.** The Court accepted that its 04.05.2022 Article 142 order, which let the committee sell property despite the PMLA and MPID attachments, had the potential to conflict with other statutes. It declined to revisit the order because it had already been implemented (para 19).
- **What the judgment says about PMLA.** The Court framed question (i) to include PMLA-attached property, but its reasoning and its conclusion in para 44 address the MPID Act. On PMLA, it upheld the Committee's order of 10.08.2023, which had held that secured creditors have no priority over PMLA-attached property because that property is proceeds of crime (paras 5 and 53). Cite the PMLA point as resting on the upheld Committee order, not as a separately reasoned holding.
- **Bombay HC, January 2026** *(per compendium; not seen)*. In a s.42 PMLA appeal, the Court held that a Rs 1,950 crore scheme under s.230 of the Companies Act, sanctioned by the NCLT on 28.11.2025, does not by itself override PMLA attachments or prosecutions.

> **Corpus and compendium disagree.** Chapter 14 says *NSEL* held that SARFAESI/RDB lenders cannot claim priority over property attached under the PMLA *or* the MPID Act. Appendix A reverses this and says secured creditors "have priority" [?]. The judgment's reasoned holding (para 44) concerns property attached under the MPID Act, and Appendix A is wrong. For PMLA-attached property, *NSEL* helps only through the upheld Committee order of 10.08.2023 (paras 5 and 53). The Delhi line in *Axis Bank* and *Rajiv Chakraborty* gives a bona fide prior lender priority over untainted "value" property, so first prove that the property is tainted.

## Open points and risks

1. **The *Nitin Jain* timing rule.** The judgment stands, and the LPA did not test its reasoning. *Rajiv Chakraborty* (paras 105, 109) takes the same view, so this is the settled Delhi position. *Value Wise* para 15 does not contradict it; it only declined to discuss s.32A because there had been no successful resolution. Until a Division Bench or the Supreme Court decides the point, assume the following in Delhi: once the NCLT approves a liquidation measure, ED cannot attach the debtor's assets or the sale corpus.
2. **Priority over secured creditors.** In Delhi, attachment gives ED no priority over a bona fide lender's prior security on untainted property (*Axis Bank* paras 149-165; *Rajiv Chakraborty* paras 110-113). *Seema Garg* (P&H) goes further and holds that pre-offence property cannot be attached as "value" property at all, unless the tainted property is abroad. Prove taint property by property.
3. **Section 32A(2) and liquidation buyers.** *Manish Kumar* para 254 confirms that liquidation buyers are protected, subject to the conditions. No reported case in this set tests a buyer's independence under s.32A(2) in liquidation.
4. **The compendium misdescribes s.8(7) and s.8(8).** Rely on the statute text as quoted in B1.
5. **Consent orders are not precedents.** *ED v. JSW*, *V Hotels*, *Udaipur* and *Shiv Charan* are all consent orders. Opposing counsel will present them as a settled practice of release. Answer with the non-precedent clause in each order.
6. **NSEL does not decide PMLA priority by its own reasoning.** Its reasoned holding concerns the MPID Act. On PMLA it only upheld the Committee's order.
7. **P. Mohanraj may be reconsidered.** *Value Wise* (para 13) records that the Supreme Court doubted *P. Mohanraj*'s s.138 holding in *Dineshchand Surana v. UCO Bank* and referred it to a larger bench. The referring order itself is not in LawBase. Para 76, which says ED is not a creditor, is a separate point, but watch the reference.
8. **Vantage Point II** (NCLAT, 14.10.2025) held that s.32A operates automatically on plan approval. It is under appeal in SC CA 1567/2026, admitted 06.02.2026 with interim directions. It is not settled law; see the brief on fraudulent CIRP and settlement.

## After the 2026 Amendment

The IBC (Amendment) Act, 2026 (Act 6 of 2026) brought most of its provisions into force on 26.05.2026 (S.O. 2625(E) of 22.05.2026). The changes relevant here:
- **The liquidation moratorium has moved.** Old s.33(5) is omitted, and s.33(1)(b) now imports s.14(1)(a) and (c). *LawBase analysis:* the reasoning of *Axis Bank* (para 146), *Rajiv Chakraborty* (paras 78-83) and *Value Wise* (para 12) was that s.14(1)(a) does not reach PMLA attachment. That reasoning should apply equally to s.33(1)(b). No court has yet said so. *Value Wise* was decided after the commencement date but on facts that arose under the old s.33(5).
- **Section 32A is unchanged.** *Manish Kumar*, *Nitin Jain* and the consent orders keep their present weight.
- **The clean slate is codified.** Sections 31(5)-(6) and Explanations I-III codify it. Section 31(6) extinguishes pre-approval claims "against the corporate debtor and its assets". Whether a PMLA attachment is such a "claim" is an open question. ED's answer follows *P. Mohanraj* para 76: the State is not a claimant or creditor when it attaches proceeds of crime.
- **Timelines are shorter.** Under s.52(2), a secured creditor realising outside liquidation must give 14 days' notice. Under s.54, liquidation must finish in 180 days. ED should file restitution, confiscation and objection applications early, so that a timeline is not used to force a sale.
- ***Rainbow Papers* is reversed for the future.** The Explanation to s.3(31) and s.53(1)(e)(i) do this. NCLT Mumbai, in *Sane Guruji Premises v. ARCK Resolution Professional LLP* (IA 2372(MB)/2026), held the change prospective from 26.05.2026. That point is unsettled. It does not affect PMLA-attached property, which is not ranked in s.53.
