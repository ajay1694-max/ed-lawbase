## Short answer

- **The moratorium does not touch a PMLA attachment.** ED does not attach as a creditor and the attachment is not a debt, so s.14(1)(a) IBC does not suspend provisional attachment, the confirmation complaint, the confirmation order or prosecution. The Delhi High Court held this in *Axis Bank* (paras 141, 146) and *Rajiv Chakraborty* (paras 67-69, 78-83, 93), and the NCLAT Larger Bench in *Kiran Shah*. The Supreme Court agreed with *Axis Bank*'s "not a creditor" reasoning in *P. Mohanraj* (para 76).
- **An attachment made before CIRP stays in force.** CIRP does not pull the property into the estate free of it (*Varrsana Ispat*, *Rotomac*, *Kiran Shah*). *Manoj Kumar Agarwal* (Sterling SEZ), which said the opposite, was disapproved by the Larger Bench. Never cite it.
- **NCLT and NCLAT cannot release, defreeze or de-attach.** The challenge lies only in the PMLA hierarchy: Adjudicating Authority, Appellate Tribunal, High Court under s.42, and then the Supreme Court. This rests on *Embassy Property* and is applied in *Foxdom*, *Dreamz Infra*, *Value Wise Consultancy* (NCLAT, 30.06.2026) and the final BPSL judgment, *Kalyani Transco*. The IBBI Circular of 04.11.2025 sends insolvency professionals to the Special Court under s.8(7)/8(8).
- **The same applies in liquidation.** *Value Wise* applies the same rule to s.33(5) as to s.14. The exception is *Nitin Jain* (Delhi HC, 2021), which is **adverse**. It held, solely on s.32A, that once the NCLT approves a liquidation measure under Regulation 32 of the Liquidation Regulations, such as a going-concern sale, ED's s.5 power to attach the corporate debtor's property "would cease to be exercisable" (paras 98, 101(H), (R)-(T)). ED's appeal was disposed of on the parties' statements, with no opinion on the law, so the judgment stands. Attach before that approval.
- **A PAO made after CIRP starts can rest on a pre-CIRP investigation.** In *Dunar Foods* (NCLAT, 2025) a PAO made after admission was upheld because the investigation began before CIRP. Prove the link between that investigation and the specific asset.
- **Section 32A is separate from s.14.** It operates only when a resolution plan is approved with a genuine change of control to an unconnected person. It frees the corporate debtor and the property covered by the plan, never the individuals (*Manish Kumar*; *Nitin Jain* para 101(Q); *Kalyani Transco* paras 116-117). *Rajiv Chakraborty* (paras 105, 109) treats plan approval, or adoption of a liquidation measure, as the point after which PMLA attachment and confiscation of the corporate debtor's property stop.
- **Whether s.32A ends a pre-CIRP attachment automatically is unsettled.** *Vantage Point II* (NCLAT, 14.10.2025) says it does, but it is under appeal in the Supreme Court (CA 1567/2026, admitted 06.02.2026). The Supreme Court's BPSL, Udaipur, V Hotels and Shiv Charan orders were made by consent or on ED's affidavit, are expressly non-precedential and left the law open.
- **Lenders' priority is limited to untainted substitute property.** A bona fide secured creditor whose charge predates the criminal activity can be paid first out of untainted property attached as equivalent value. It has no priority over directly tainted property (*Axis Bank*, paras 149-165, especially 161 and 163-165). *Rajiv Chakraborty* (paras 110-113, 116) adds a point against ED: a PAO gives ED no superior or overriding right in the property, and priority is settled independently, according to law.
- **Check the asset's date and its link to the crime before attaching.** Property acquired before the scheduled offence is not its proceeds (*Pavana Dibbur*, para 27(c)). Equivalent-value attachment needs specific recorded reasons and proof that the person received proceeds (*Seema Garg*; *Sahana Builders*, ATFP 2026). A company's assets cannot be attached only because an accused heads it (*Usha Ananthasubramanian*).
- **Do not press confiscation while confirmation is under appeal.** Under *Nav Nirman Builders* (SC, 06.02.2026), s.8(7) confiscation cannot be concluded while an appeal against the s.8(3) confirmation is pending.

## Part A. Attachment made before CIRP (Chapter 1)

### 1.1 Attachment made before CIRP is admitted

**Proposition.** A provisional attachment that was made and confirmed before CIRP is not undone by the later insolvency. It is not a creditor's claim, so s.14(1)(a) does not reach it, and the NCLT/NCLAT cannot recall it or sit in appeal over it.

**Authorities.**
- ***Deputy Director, ED v. Axis Bank***, Delhi HC (R.K. Gauba, J.), 02.04.2019, 2019 SCC OnLine Del 7854 : (2019) 259 DLT 500 *(corpus: DLHC012396152018; the same text is also held as DLHC011608442018, while DLHC010007562018 is only an interim order of 08.02.2019)*. Five s.42 appeals by ED and the Union against Appellate Tribunal orders that had given banks holding charges priority over attached property.
  - **ED is not a creditor (para 141):** when the Government attaches proceeds of crime it "does not stand as a creditor", and the accused does not become a debtor. The 2016 secured-creditor priority clauses (s.31B RDB Act, s.26E SARFAESI) therefore do not subordinate the PMLA (paras 140-145).
  - **Moratorium (para 146):** the s.14 moratorium "cannot come in the way" of PMLA attachment.
  - **No override by the IBC (paras 147-148; para 171, conclusions (vi)-(viii)):** the objects of the laws differ. By s.71 the PMLA prevails in matters of money-laundering and proceeds of crime; otherwise the laws co-exist and are applied in harmony.
  - **Result (paras 172-176):** ED's appeals were allowed, and the matters were sent back to the Tribunal to apply these principles.
  - **The compendium's paragraph references (105, 127-128) are wrong.** Those paragraphs deal with other points; cite paras 141 and 146.
  - **Status: under appeal.** *Kiran Shah* (NCLAT LB, para 109, *as quoted in Rajiv Chakraborty*) records that the Supreme Court passed only a status quo order on 30.08.2019 and did not stay the judgment. The present stage of that appeal is not verified. Treat it as binding in Delhi but under challenge.
- ***Varrsana Ispat Ltd. v. Deputy Director, ED***, NCLAT, 02.05.2019 (per compendium), Company Appeal (AT) (Ins) 493/2018, 2019 SCC OnLine NCLAT 236 *(citation as cited in Rajiv Chakraborty, fn. 14)*. ED attached and confirmed before CIRP; the RP sought release because of the moratorium.
  - **Held (per compendium):** the attachment creates no debt and vests no ownership in the Government; it only restrains alienation. Because it was on record before CIRP, the moratorium gives the RP nothing. The NCLT/NCLAT cannot recall a confirmed PMLA attachment.
  - **What NCLAT itself said (*as quoted in Rajiv Chakraborty*, para 86):** s.14 does not apply to criminal proceedings or to penal action relating to the proceeds of crime.
  - **Status:** the Supreme Court dismissed Civil Appeal 5546/2019 on 22.07.2019. The compendium calls that dismissal non-speaking and says it declares no law under Art.141. The NCLAT Larger Bench in *Kiran Shah* (para 108, *as quoted in Rajiv Chakraborty*) and the NCLAT in *Value Wise* (para 12) both describe the NCLAT ruling as "confirmed" by the Supreme Court. Describe the dismissal accurately as a dismissal, and rest the argument on the reasoning.
- ***Rotomac Global Pvt. Ltd. v. Deputy Director, ED***, NCLAT, 02.07.2019 [?], Company Appeal (AT) (Ins) 140/2019, 2019 SCC OnLine NCLAT 961 *(citation as cited in Rajiv Chakraborty, fn. 16)*. This was a release application by the RP/liquidator over pre-CIRP attachments. The NCLAT followed *Varrsana*: PMLA proceedings over proceeds of crime are not the proceedings s.14 suspends. *Rajiv Chakraborty* para 88 confirms that *Rotomac* reiterated *Varrsana*.

**ED use.** Cite *Varrsana* and *Rotomac* with *Kiran Shah* whenever an RP says CIRP or the moratorium by itself releases a pre-CIRP attachment. Use *Axis Bank* para 141 through *P. Mohanraj* para 76, which is a Supreme Court reading of it. Neither *Varrsana* nor *Rotomac* is authority on a fresh PAO made after CIRP began, or on the position after s.32A.

### 1.2 Whether pre-attached property forms part of the estate

**Proposition.** Property under a pre-CIRP attachment does not become part of the estate, free of the attachment, merely because CIRP has started. Neither s.238 IBC nor s.71 PMLA gives a blanket override; the two Acts are read together.

**Authorities.**
- ***Kiran Shah, RP of KSL and Industries Ltd. v. ED***, NCLAT (Larger Bench), Company Appeal (AT) (Ins) 817/2021, 2022 SCC OnLine NCLAT 2 *(citation as cited in Rajiv Chakraborty, fn. 18)*; date read as 03.01.2022 [?].
  - **Background:** the RP relied on *Manoj Kumar Agarwal*, which conflicted with *Varrsana* and *Rotomac*, so the question went to a Larger Bench.
  - **Held:** *Manoj Kumar Agarwal* was decided without dealing with the earlier co-ordinate Bench rulings, so it was per incuriam and contrary to stare decisis. A PAO passed and confirmed before CIRP is neither nullified nor suspended by CIRP or the moratorium (per compendium). *Rajiv Chakraborty* para 89 confirms that the Larger Bench restored *Varrsana* and disapproved *Manoj Kumar Agarwal*.
  - **Status:** controlling within the NCLAT.
- ***Directorate of Enforcement v. Manoj Kumar Agarwal, RP*** (Sterling SEZ), NCLAT, Company Appeal (AT) (Ins) 575 & 576/2019, 2021 SCC OnLine NCLAT 121 *(citation as cited in Rajiv Chakraborty, fn. 13)*. The compendium gives the date as 09.04.2021; one entry reads 09.08.2021 [?].
  - **Held:** attached property should become available for the IBC process once CIRP begins.
  - **Status:** disapproved in *Kiran Shah*; not good law. Retain it only as history.
- ***Nitin Jain, Liquidator of PSL Ltd. v. ED***, Delhi HC (Yashwant Varma, J.), 15.12.2021, W.P.(C) 3261/2021, 2021 SCC OnLine Del 5281 *(corpus: DLHC010126002021; citation as given in Rajiv Chakraborty, fn. 24)*. The compendium gives the date as 18.12.2021; the judgment shows 15.12.2021.
  - **Facts (paras 3-7):**
    - PSL Ltd. went into CIRP in 2019 and liquidation in 2020.
    - ED asked the liquidator by e-mail not to dispose of assets when no PAO existed; the Court stayed that e-mail.
    - The NCLT approved a going-concern sale on 08.09.2021.
    - ED issued a PAO for about Rs. 274.60 crore on 02.12.2021, during the hearing.
  - **The two Acts (para 101(C)-(G)):** the IBC and PMLA cover distinct subjects. A court must first ask whether they genuinely conflict, and must reconcile them if they do.
  - **The answer lies solely in s.32A (para 101(H)).** Reconciliation "needs to be answered solely on the anvil of Section 32A". Once Parliament made a specific provision, that provision alone fixes how far PMLA powers survive during resolution or liquidation.
  - **Trigger in liquidation (paras 96, 98; para 101(N), (R)-(T)):** the bar starts when the NCLT adopts and approves a measure under Regulation 32 of the Liquidation Regulations. From then "the power to attach as conferred by Section 5 of the PMLA would cease to be exercisable" (para 98). On the facts the bar ran from approval of the going-concern sale.
  - **Only the corporate debtor's property (para 98; para 101(Q)):** the bar does not reach the persons in charge, or ED's right to proceed against other property.
  - **Eligibility left open (para 99):** ED's objections to the buyer's eligibility under s.32A were left to the competent forum.
  - **Liquidator to cooperate (para 100):** the liquidator must give the PMLA authorities the records and information they ask for.
  - **Directions (para 102):** the petition was allowed. ED was restrained from any further action, coercive or otherwise, against the liquidation estate or the sale proceeds.
  - **The compendium misreads it.** It says the restraint rests on harmonious construction and "not on s.32A". Paras 98 and 101(H) say the opposite; cite the judgment.
  - **Later history:** *ED v. Mavent Restructuring Services LLP (Liquidator of PSL Ltd.)*, LPA 512/2021, Delhi HC DB, 27.02.2025 *(corpus: DLHC010404622021)*. ED stated that it would transfer the Rs. 274.60 crore realised from the attached assets to the liquidator; the liquidator said it would go to victims of the fraud. The Bench bound the parties to these statements and said its order expressed no opinion on the merits or the questions of law.
  - **Status:** good law (Single Judge). The appeal did not set it aside or test its reasoning. **Adverse.**

**ED use.** Answer any reliance on *Manoj Kumar Agarwal* with *Kiran Shah*. For *Nitin Jain*:
- Attach before the NCLT approves the liquidation measure or the resolution plan; after that date this judgment treats the corporate debtor's property as beyond s.5.
- If the buyer may be connected to the promoters, object to eligibility before the NCLT/NCLAT at the approval stage (para 99).
- Use para 98 and para 101(Q) to keep individuals and their personal assets within reach, and para 100 to obtain the liquidator's records.
- Remember that *Value Wise* (2026) declined to discuss s.32A (para 15). It does not displace *Nitin Jain* on what happens after a liquidation measure is approved.

When an RP argues that the IBC, being later, overrides the PMLA, answer with *Rajiv Chakraborty* para 103: the "later special statute" rule is not inviolable. That paragraph discusses *Solidaire India v. Fairgrowth*, [2001] 1 SCR 932 *(corpus: 2001_INSC_76)*.

### 1.3 Possession and custody after the IRP/RP is appointed

**Proposition.** The moratorium does not suspend PMLA attachment or prosecution, and *P. Mohanraj* does not extend to the PMLA. Assets attached after CIRP in an investigation that began before CIRP are not released to the estate. In liquidation, a going-concern sale may go ahead if the proceeds attributable to tainted assets are kept apart for ED.

**Authorities.**
- ***Rajiv Chakraborty, RP of Era Infra Engineering Ltd. v. ED***, Delhi HC (Yashwant Varma, J.), 11.11.2022, W.P.(C) 9531/2020 *(corpus: DLHC010046592020)*. The compendium's citation, 2022 SCC OnLine Del 3703 [?], is not verified, and it lists the case as a W.P.(Crl.); the judgment is a W.P.(C). The RP challenged two PAOs made during CIRP and their confirmation.
  - **ED is not a creditor (paras 67-69),** relying on *Axis Bank* para 141.
  - **Moratorium (paras 78-83, 93):**
    - PMLA attachment is not recovery of a debt.
    - *P. Mohanraj* rests on the debt-recovery character of s.138, so it does not apply (paras 79-80).
    - "it would be incorrect to read Section 14 as completely shutting out actions under Sections 5 and 8 of the PMLA" (para 83).
    - The NCLAT line in *Varrsana*, *Rotomac* and *Kiran Shah* is correct (paras 86-93).
  - ***B. Rama Raju* distinguished (para 85):** it decided the validity of the provisions, not the moratorium.
  - **Attachment moves no title (paras 94-101):** it only prevents private alienation. The property vests in the Union only on confiscation by the Special Court.
  - **s.238 is limited by s.32A (paras 102-109):** the "later Act prevails" rule is not decisive between two special laws. Section 32A is Parliament's last word. PMLA attachment and confiscation stop when a resolution plan is approved or a measure in aid of liquidation is adopted (para 105). Section 238 cannot have "an impact far greater than that envisaged in Section 32A" (para 109).
  - **No superior right for ED (paras 110-113, 116):** following *Axis Bank* paras 149-165, a PAO "does not invest in that authority a superior or overriding right in property" (para 113). Priority and distribution are settled independently, according to law, and the RP may seek relief from the PMLA authorities. This part is adverse to ED.
  - **Result (paras 114-116):** the writ petition was **dismissed**. The RP was left free to seek release according to law, and ED's rights were limited to the extent recognised in this judgment and *Axis Bank*.
  - **Where the compendium goes wrong.** It says the petition partly succeeded and that the Court ruled on promoters' personal liability. The petition was dismissed, and the judgment contains no ruling on promoters; that point is decided in *Nitin Jain* (para 101(Q)).
  - **Status:** good law; binding in Delhi, persuasive elsewhere. **Pro-ED** on the moratorium; adverse on priority.
- ***P. Mohanraj v. Shah Brothers Ispat Pvt. Ltd.***, SC (R.F. Nariman, Navin Sinha, K.M. Joseph, JJ.), 01.03.2021, [2021] 14 SCR 204 : (2021) 6 SCC 258 *(corpus: 2021_INSC_133)*.
  - **Held:** a s.138/141 NI Act proceeding against the corporate debtor is a "proceeding" under s.14(1)(a), because in substance it is compensatory.
  - **The natural persons (para 77):** the moratorium applies only to the corporate debtor, and the natural persons under s.141 stay liable.
  - **On *Axis Bank* (para 76):** the Court quoted para 141 of *Axis Bank* and said that confiscation of proceeds of crime is done by the government acting under statute, not as a creditor.
  - **Status:** binding. *Value Wise* (para 13) records that another Bench of the Supreme Court has doubted *P. Mohanraj* and referred it to a larger Bench in *Dineshchand Surana v. UCO Bank*, (2026) ibclaw.in 402 SC *(as quoted in Value Wise; not read)*.
- ***Anil Kohli, RP of Dunar Foods Ltd. v. ED***, NCLAT, 03.07.2025 [?], Company Appeal (AT) (Ins) 389/2018 [?], (2025) ibclaw.in 470 NCLAT. The appeal number and the date look inconsistent in the compendium.
  - **Facts (per compendium):** ED traced allegedly tainted funds from an associate company into Dunar Foods. The investigation and ECIR began before CIRP, but the formal attachment came after admission.
  - **Held:**
    1. The date the investigation began decides the matter, not the date the PAO was signed.
    2. s.238 does not override the PMLA's confiscation machinery for proceeds of crime.
    3. s.32A is conditional and operates only from actual approval of a compliant plan, not from admission.
  - **Corroboration:** the Supreme Court's limitation ruling in the same CIRP records the claim that ED's investigation found a sister concern had siphoned funds into Dunar Foods (*National Spot Exchange Ltd. v. Anil Kohli*, [2021] 7 SCR 1024, *corpus: 2021_INSC_476*). It is not a ruling on the attachment.
  - **Status:** NCLAT; fact-specific.
- ***Pankaj Dhanuka v. Deputy Director, ED*** (Corporate Power Ltd.), NCLT Kolkata, 24.07.2025, I.A. (IB) 162/KB/2025 in C.P. (IB) 23/KB/2019. In liquidation, the NCLT let the liquidator sell the corporate debtor as a going concern despite a post-CIRP attachment. The condition was that the part of the price attributable to tainted assets goes into a separate account, preserving ED's rights. The order said it neither reviews the attachment nor absolves any individual. **Status:** first instance; persuasive.
- ***Embassy Property Developments v. State of Karnataka***, SC, 03.12.2019, (2020) 13 SCC 308. The NCLT's residuary jurisdiction under s.60(5)(c) does not extend to judicial review of public-law decisions taken under another statute; the remedy is a writ or the special forum. **Status:** binding. Not in LawBase, but cited by that citation in *Rajiv Chakraborty*, *Nitin Jain* and *Value Wise* (para 14).
- ***DoE v. Hi-Tech Mercantile India Pvt. Ltd.***, Delhi HC. The compendium says *Rajiv Chakraborty* reviewed it. No such reference was found in the LawBase text of *Rajiv Chakraborty*, so do not cite it on this point.

**ED use.**
- Lead with *Axis Bank* paras 141 and 146, *Rajiv Chakraborty* paras 67-69, 79-83 and 93, and *P. Mohanraj* para 76, against any moratorium argument.
- Where the PAO postdates admission, use *Dunar Foods* and prove the asset's link to the earlier investigation.
- In liquidation, offer the *Pankaj Dhanuka* terms yourself: allow the sale, require a segregated account for the tainted part, and state that the order is not a review of the attachment.

### 1.4 Secured creditors and bona fide third parties in pre-attached property

**Proposition.** A bona fide secured creditor whose interest arose before the criminal activity is not displaced when ED attaches untainted property as equivalent value. It has no such priority over property that is itself tainted.

**Authority.** ***Axis Bank*** (above), paras 149-170, read with ***Rajiv Chakraborty*** paras 110-113 and 116.
- **Prior charge (paras 149-158):** a PMLA attachment is not illegal merely because a bank holds a prior charge, and it does not by itself void that charge. A charge may be declared void only on material that it was created to defeat the PMLA (para 158; conclusion (x)).
- **Tainted property (para 161):** it can be attached and confiscated even against a bona fide transferee for value, whose remedy lies against the wrongdoer.
- **Deemed tainted (equivalent-value) property (para 162):** a third party who acquired its interest bona fide and for adequate consideration may seek release.
- **Pre-offence interest (para 163):** the date or period of the criminal activity is the cut-off. An interest acquired before it cannot have been meant to defeat the PMLA.
- **Effect (paras 164-165):** the attachment stays valid but covers only the value above the third party's claim. Where the secured creditor began enforcement before the attachment, the attachment "takes a back-seat" and the surplus goes to the PMLA.
- **Interests created around or after the crime (para 170):** the claimant must also prove due diligence.
- **Forum (paras 167-170; conclusion (xvii)):** once confirmation is final, or confiscation is ordered, or the s.4 trial has begun, the claim goes only to the Special Court under s.8(7)/(8).
- ***Rajiv Chakraborty* adds** that a PAO gives ED no superior or overriding right in the property (para 113). Expect RPs and lenders to cite it to deny ED any priority.

**ED use.** Against a lender's priority claim:
- show that the property is directly tainted, not substitute property;
- show that the charge was created after the criminal activity began; or
- show that the lender was not diligent or not bona fide.

### 1.5 Effect of later approval of a resolution plan, and s.32A

**Proposition.** Whether s.32A ends a pre-CIRP attachment by itself on plan approval is pending in the Supreme Court. The Supreme Court's orders that restored property to resolution applicants were made by consent and decide no law. The exact date of a PAO relative to plan approval can itself be fought over.

**Authorities.**
- ***Vantage Point Asset Management Pte. Ltd. v. Gaurav Misra, RP of Alchemist Infra Realty Ltd.***, NCLAT Principal Bench (Ashok Bhushan, J., Chairperson; Barun Mitra, Member (T)), 14.10.2025, Company Appeal (AT) (Ins) 1495/2024, 2025 SCC OnLine NCLAT 1652 [?].
  - **Facts:** ED's PAO was made in 2019, more than three years before CIRP. A Delhi HC Division Bench had ordered status quo in the PMLA proceedings. The NCLT approved the plan but refused to release the property.
  - **Vantage Point I:** the NCLAT's first order of 13.08.2024 was passed without ED and was recalled on 09.01.2025.
  - **Vantage Point II (14.10.2025):** after hearing ED, the NCLAT held that s.32A operates on approval of a plan that changes control, so the pre-CIRP PAO ceases from that date. The resolution applicant need not apply to the PMLA authorities.
  - **Facts the NCLAT relied on:** s.8 proceedings had not begun; depositors formed the majority of the CoC; and the plan refunded depositors in full.
  - **Status:** **under appeal**, SC Civil Appeal 1567/2026, admitted 06.02.2026. Interim safeguards were reportedly ordered [?]. The recalled order has no value. **Adverse.**
- ***Udaipur Entertainment World Pvt. Ltd. v. Union of India***, SC, 10.10.2025, appeals arising out of SLP(C) 10734/2025 and 26154-26155/2025. On ED's affidavit and by settlement:
  - the PAO was partly set aside, and units were restored to the resolution applicant only for genuine homebuyers, under the second proviso to s.8(8);
  - eleven units stayed attached;
  - the corporate debtor was dropped from the prosecution under s.32A, while the prosecution of former directors, persons in control and abettors continued;
  - ED kept liberty to act if the applicant proves connected or a beneficiary.

  **Status:** consent order; expressly not a precedent, with all questions of law open (paras 2-13).
- ***V Hotels Ltd. v. ED***, Bombay HC (DB), 28.11.2024, W.P.(L) 32216/2024, 2024:BHC-OS:20171-DB. The Court examined, document by document, whether the PAO was in fact issued before or after plan approval. The email relied on for an earlier date had bounced, and there were signs that the date may have been wrongly inserted. No final ruling on the substantive question is recorded. **Status:** neutral; overtaken by the SC order.
- ***DoE v. V Hotels Ltd.***, SC, 11.11.2025, Criminal Appeal 2925/2025. This was a consent restitution under the second proviso to s.8(8):
  - the corporate debtor was dropped from the prosecution under s.32A;
  - individuals' prosecution was preserved;
  - the relief was conditional on the resolution applicant being unconnected and not a beneficiary, with liberty to ED.

  **Status:** non-precedential; law open.
- ***JSW Steel Ltd. v. Mahender Kumar Khandelwal, RP of Bhushan Power and Steel Ltd.***, NCLAT, 2020 SCC OnLine NCLAT 431. This was interim protection against ED's attachment of plant assets made after the NCLT approved the plan; the compendium calls it interim. *Rajiv Chakraborty* (fn. 25) cites 2020 SCC OnLine NCLAT 431, and ED's appeal in the Supreme Court is described as against a judgment of 17.02.2020. **Status:** superseded; background only. **Adverse.**
- ***DoE v. JSW Steel Ltd.***, SC, 11.12.2024, Civil Appeal 3362/2020 and 14503-14504/2024.
  - **ED's affidavit:** s.32A, in force from 28.12.2019, is not retrospective. On the peculiar facts, however, the resolution applicant could take the attached properties as restitution under s.8(8) read with Rule 3A of the Restoration of Property Rules, 2016.
  - **Order:** the Court directed handover on that basis and expressly gave no view on s.32A or on ED's power to attach during CIRP.
  - **Status:** not authority on either point.
- ***Kalyani Transco v. Bhushan Power and Steel Ltd.***, SC (B.R. Gavai, CJI; Satish Chandra Sharma and K. Vinod Chandran, JJ.), 26.09.2025, 2025 INSC 1165. This judgment upheld JSW's plan and dismissed the appeals.
  - **The CoC (per compendium):** the CoC continues until the plan is implemented or liquidation is ordered.
  - **s.32A (paras 116-117):** its object is to give the corporate debtor and its property conditional immunity from the date of approval.
  - **Delay (paras 118-124):** the Court treated ED's continuing prosecution and the provisional attachment among the reasons why implementation was delayed. It held that the charge of deliberate delay against the resolution applicant failed.
  - **The handover order (para 2.22):** it records the reservation attached to the 11.12.2024 order.
  - **Status:** binding. It supersedes the judgment of 02.05.2025 (2025 INSC 621), which was recalled on 31.07.2025. Not in LawBase.
- ***Manish Kumar v. Union of India***, SC, 19.01.2021, [2021] 14 SCR 895 : (2021) 5 SCC 1 *(corpus: 2021_INSC_28)*. This upheld s.32A as valid; it immunises the resolved corporate debtor but not the persons responsible (per compendium).
- ***Embassy Property*** and ***Rajiv Chakraborty***: see 1.3. ***Anil Kohli*** and ***Manish Kumar*** were relied on by ED in *Vantage Point* and not accepted on those facts.

**ED use.**
- Treat *Vantage Point II* as sub judice and confine it to its facts.
- Insist on being impleaded in every plan proceeding that touches attached assets; *Vantage Point I* was recalled because ED was not heard.
- Keep verifiable records of the date and mode of issue and service of every PAO (*V Hotels*).
- When consenting to restitution, follow the conditions in *Udaipur*, *V Hotels* and *Shiv Charan*:
  - restore under the second proviso to s.8(8);
  - drop only the corporate debtor;
  - keep the prosecution of individuals and their properties alive;
  - retain specific units where needed;
  - reserve liberty if the resolution applicant proves connected or a beneficiary.
- Expect resolution applicants to cite *Kalyani Transco* paras 118-124 to excuse delay.

## Part B. Attachment after CIRP begins (Chapter 2)

### 2.1 Fresh provisional attachment after CIRP has started

**Proposition.** The compendium poses whether ED may issue a wholly fresh PAO once CIRP has started. No appellate decision in it decides that exact question in ED's favour, but:
- *Rajiv Chakraborty* para 93 accepts that the moratorium does not prevent ED's powers under ss.5 and 8 "notwithstanding the pendency of the CIRP";
- *Dunar Foods* upheld a post-admission PAO where the investigation predated CIRP;
- the contrary *Manoj Kumar Agarwal* view is disapproved.

An NCLT admitting a petition may also carve the attached property out of the moratorium.

**Authorities.**
- ***Manoj Kumar Agarwal*** (1.2): history only.
- ***Kiran Shah*** (1.2).
- ***Three C Green Developers Pvt. Ltd. v. Three C Infratech Pvt. Ltd.***, NCLT New Delhi [?], 12.03.2025 [?], C.P.(IB) 617/ND/2022, (2025) ibclaw.in 387 NCLT.
  - **Admission:** on an intra-group s.7 petition, the NCLT rejected a s.65 (collusive initiation) objection and admitted the petition.
  - **Carve-out:** in the admission order it directed that the moratorium would not apply to a 9.2-acre plot already attached by ED, and that ED's proceedings on it could continue.
  - **Status:** first instance; persuasive. **Pro-ED.**

**ED use.** When CIRP is sought against an entity holding attached property, ask the NCLT to write an express carve-out into the admission order. Before a fresh PAO during CIRP:
- record the pre-CIRP start of the investigation and the asset's link to it (*Dunar Foods*);
- note that *Nitin Jain* bars fresh action once a liquidation sale has been approved.

### 2.2 Property already in the custody or control of the IRP/RP

**Proposition.** Possession by the IRP/RP under ss.17, 18 or 25 IBC does not displace a confirmed PMLA attachment, and the NCLT/NCLAT cannot recall it. Attachment creates no debt and no ownership. The date of the investigation, not the date of the PAO, decides whether the asset is treated as pre-CIRP tainted property.

**Authorities.** *Varrsana Ispat*, *Rotomac* (1.1); *Dunar Foods* (1.3).

**ED use.** Do not concede that the RP's custody gives the NCLT any power over the attachment. The RP holds the asset subject to it.

### 2.3 Attachment while the s.14 moratorium is running

**Proposition.**
- The moratorium does not suspend attachment, continuation of attachment, confirmation or prosecution.
- Confirmation proceedings and a confirmation order passed during CIRP are not barred.
- The NCLT cannot use s.60(5) to defreeze an account frozen under the PMLA, even to fund CIRP costs.
- A pre-CIRP confirmed attachment is not available to the estate.

**Authorities.**
- ***Rajiv Chakraborty*** (1.3) and ***P. Mohanraj*** (1.3).
- ***Vikram Kumar, RP of Dr. Jain Video on Wheels Ltd. v. ED***, NCLT New Delhi, 02.09.2025, I.A. 4373/2021 and 5680/2023 in C.P.(IB) 843(ND)/2018.
  - **Facts:** the RP asked the NCLT to stay the confirmation proceedings before the PMLA Adjudicating Authority as s.14 "proceedings". The Authority confirmed the attachment during CIRP anyway, and the RP then sought a declaration that this breached s.14.
  - **Held:** following *Varrsana*, *Axis Bank* and *Kiran Shah*, confirmation proceedings belong to the PMLA's penal scheme, are not recovery proceedings and fall outside s.14. Both applications were dismissed.
  - **Scope:** confined to the confirmation stage.
  - **Status:** first instance; persuasive. **Pro-ED.**
- ***Shailendra Singh, RP of Foxdom Technologies Pvt. Ltd. v. ED***, NCLT New Delhi, 11.02.2025, I.A. 4689/2023 in IB-102(ND)/2022.
  - **Facts:** the RP wanted the NCLT to direct ED to defreeze the debtor's bank account, frozen on the PMLA Adjudicating Authority's directions, to pay CIRP costs.
  - **Held:** relying on *Embassy Property*, *Kiran Shah*, *Varrsana* and *DSK Motors Pvt. Ltd. v. Deputy Director, ED* (court and date not given in the compendium), the NCLT said it has no such power. s.60(5) cannot be used as a back door against action under a separate penal statute, and the RP's funding difficulty does not create jurisdiction.
  - **Status:** first instance. **Pro-ED.**
- ***Ramanathan Bhuvaneshwari, RP of Dreamz Infra India Pvt. Ltd. v. ED***, NCLT Bengaluru, 28.07.2025, I.A. 28/2024 in C.P.(IB) 113/BB/2022. The compendium reads the RP's first name both as "Ramanathan" and as "Sathyanathan" [?].
  - **Facts:** the attachment (July 2022) and confirmation (December 2022) both predated CIRP (August 2023).
  - **Held:** relying on *Dunar Foods* and *Kalyani Transco*, s.238 does not override the PMLA for tainted assets. Applying *Embassy Property*, the NCLT's powers are limited to ss.31 and 60, and it cannot decide whether an ED attachment should continue.
  - **Status:** first instance. **Pro-ED.**
- ***Value Wise Consultancy Pvt. Ltd. v. Deputy Director, ED***, NCLAT Principal Bench (N. Seshasayee, J.; Arun Baroka and Indevar Pandey, Members (T)), 30.06.2026, Company Appeal (AT) (Ins) 1226 and 1227/2022 *(corpus: EDPORTAL_18_2026-06-30; the citation field in that record shows the Embassy Property citation, not this order's)*.
  - **Facts:** ED withdrew funds from the debtor's account during the moratorium and attached vehicles by a fresh PAO during liquidation.
  - **Scope of s.14 and s.33(5) (para 12):** they protect the legitimately acquired assets of the corporate debtor. They do not cover action under penal statutes of a public-law nature that cannot add to the debtor's civil debt-liability. The Parliament did not make the IBC "a holy Ganges" that washes away criminality under the PMLA.
  - **Forum (paras 14 and 16):** following *Embassy Property* and the IBBI Circular of 04.11.2025, IBC tribunals cannot entertain any plea against ED. Whether to attach is ED's decision, and challenges, including to notices under s.50 PMLA, lie only in the PMLA mechanism.
  - **s.32A (para 15):** not discussed, because it operates only where resolution succeeds.
  - **Result (para 17):** appeals dismissed.
  - **Status:** NCLAT. **Pro-ED.**
- ***Three C*** (2.1) applies here as well.

**ED use.**
- Pursue the original complaint and confirmation during CIRP.
- Oppose every s.60(5) application for release or defreezing at the threshold, citing *Embassy Property*, *Value Wise*, *Foxdom* and *Dreamz Infra*.
- Direct the IP to the Special Court route under s.8(7)/8(8).

### 2.4 Attachment after the plan is approved but before it is implemented

**Proposition.** The gap between plan approval and implementation is where the BPSL dispute arose. The NCLAT's 2020 order gave only interim protection to the resolution applicant. The Supreme Court's 11.12.2024 handover rested on ED's own affidavit and on s.8(8) PMLA with Rule 3A of the 2016 Rules, and it declined to interpret s.32A.

**Authorities.** *JSW Steel v. Mahender Kumar Khandelwal*; *DoE v. JSW Steel* (11.12.2024); *Kalyani Transco* (all at 1.5).

**ED use.** Do not let the 11.12.2024 order be cited on s.32A or on automatic de-attachment. It turned on ED's own affidavit, and a different stand by ED takes a case outside it.

### 2.5 Consequences of an attachment made in breach of the moratorium or s.32A

**Proposition.** An attachment later found inconsistent with the moratorium or s.32A is not automatically void. Correction comes through the appellate route or a Larger Bench. The compendium offers no separate case; *Manoj Kumar Agarwal*, as disapproved in *Kiran Shah*, is the illustration.

**ED use.** If an RP calls an attachment void and asks the NCLT to ignore it, answer that only the PMLA hierarchy can set it aside (*Value Wise*, para 16).

## Part C. The moratorium at each PMLA stage (Chapter 4)

Identify the stage first. The table states the position the authorities support.

| Stage | Effect of s.14 / s.33 | Main authority |
|---|---|---|
| PAO made before CIRP | Survives; NCLT cannot recall | *Varrsana*, *Rotomac*, *Kiran Shah* |
| PAO made after CIRP in a pre-CIRP investigation | Upheld on facts; prove the link to the asset | *Dunar Foods*; *Rajiv Chakraborty* para 93 |
| Fresh PAO after the NCLT approves a liquidation measure (Reg. 32) | Barred under s.32A (adverse; judgment stands, appeal disposed of without ruling on law) | *Nitin Jain* paras 98, 101(H); *Rajiv Chakraborty* para 105 |
| Confirmation complaint and confirmation order during CIRP | Not suspended | *Dr. Jain Video on Wheels*; *Rajiv Chakraborty* |
| Confiscation | Judicial act after trial; only this passes title | *Rajiv Chakraborty* paras 94-101; *Vantage Point* |
| Prosecution | Not suspended; only the corporate debtor is discharged under s.32A | *P. Mohanraj* paras 33, 77; *Nitin Jain* para 101(Q); *Manish Kumar* |
| After a compliant plan with change of control | s.32A protects the corporate debtor and plan property; whether release is automatic is pending | *Vantage Point II* (under appeal) |

### 4.1 Pre-CIRP confirmed attachment once CIRP intervenes

A PAO confirmed before CIRP is unaffected by s.14 and cannot be recalled by the NCLT/NCLAT (*Varrsana*, *Rotomac*). The compendium stresses that this line does not decide whether a wholly fresh PAO may be issued after CIRP begins.

### 4.2 PAO before CIRP, confirmation complaint filed during CIRP

*Manoj Kumar Agarwal* took the view that such property must be available to the IBC process. *Kiran Shah* superseded it, so cite *Manoj Kumar Agarwal* only for history. File the complaint within time regardless of CIRP.

### 4.3 Confirmation complaint before CIRP; Adjudicating Authority confirms during CIRP

Pending confirmation proceedings are not suspended (*Dr. Jain Video on Wheels*). *Dunar Foods* supports the related point that a later attachment relates back to a pre-CIRP investigation, but only if the chronology and the link to the asset are proved.

### 4.4 Legal effect of a confirmation order passed during the moratorium

The moratorium does not suspend attachment, confirmation or prosecution, and *P. Mohanraj* does not reach the PMLA (*Rajiv Chakraborty*, paras 79-83, 93). Keep this point apart from that judgment's s.32A discussion (paras 102-109).

### 4.5 Confiscation during CIRP where attachment was confirmed before it

ED does not act as a creditor at any stage, from provisional attachment to confiscation (*Axis Bank* para 141; *P. Mohanraj* para 76). *Nitin Jain* is the limit at the liquidation stage: from the NCLT's approval of a Regulation 32 measure, s.32A bars further attachment of the corporate debtor's property (paras 98, 101(H)). The compendium says that limit rests on harmonious construction; the judgment rests it on s.32A (see 1.2).

### 4.6 Confirmation by the Adjudicating Authority versus confiscation by the Special Court

Confirmation under s.8(3) continues the restraint and does not divest ownership. Confiscation follows trial and conviction under s.8(5), or the other provisions named in *Rajiv Chakraborty* para 101, and only then does title vest in the Union. *Vantage Point* is also cited for this distinction. The compendium's reference to s.58B here is legible only in part [?].

### 4.7 Sections 14, 32A and 238 IBC at each stage

Neither s.238 IBC nor s.71 PMLA overrides the other across the board; the Acts are read together stage by stage (*Axis Bank* paras 147-148; *Rajiv Chakraborty* paras 102-109; *Nitin Jain* para 101(C)-(H)). In both Delhi judgments the outer limit of PMLA power over the corporate debtor's property is set by s.32A.

- ***Shiv Charan & Ors. v. Adjudicating Authority under PMLA***, Bombay HC, 01.03.2024 (day not legible in one entry [?]), W.P.(L) 9943/2023 with W.P.(L) 29111/2023 (another entry reads 9111 [?]), (2024) ibclaw.in 154 HC [?].
  - **Facts:** DSK Southern Projects. ED's attachment was confirmed in 2019; the NCLT approved the resolution applicants' plan on 17.02.2023.
  - **High Court:** applying s.32A, it protected the resolution applicant despite the earlier confirmed attachment, while the former promoters and directors stayed liable.
  - **Supreme Court:** *Deputy Director, ED v. Shiv Charan*, Civil Appeal 9692-9693/2024, order of 16.07.2026; one entry reads "2_.07.2026" [?]. By consent, it restored bank balances and fourteen residential units covered by the plan under the second proviso to s.8(8), and deleted the corporate debtor from the prosecution under s.32A. It kept the ECIR and prosecution alive against the former directors, persons in control and abettors, and their properties, and made the relief conditional on the resolution applicants being unconnected and not beneficiaries. It did not decide whether s.32A by itself ends a pre-CIRP attachment.
  - **Status:** the Supreme Court order is not a precedent, and the High Court's reasoning was neither affirmed nor reversed. **Mixed.**
- ***Kalyani Transco*** (1.5). The compendium cites it for the point that the NCLT/NCLAT cannot rule on the validity of an attachment, confirmation or confiscation at any stage. This is derived from *Embassy Property*; *Value Wise* para 14 states the same rule directly.

**ED use (Chapter 4).**
- Name the stage in every reply.
- Keep filing original complaints and pressing confirmation during CIRP.
- Attach before the NCLT approves a resolution plan or a liquidation measure. After that, *Nitin Jain* (and *Rajiv Chakraborty* para 105) treat the corporate debtor's property as beyond s.5.
- Follow the *Shiv Charan* consent model when s.32A restitution is agreed.

## Part D. Nature and incidents of PMLA attachment (Chapter 5)

### 5.1 Provisional attachment under s.5

A provisional attachment restrains alienation until adjudication. It moves no title, creates no debt and is not a creditor's act (*Axis Bank* para 141; *Varrsana*; *Rajiv Chakraborty* paras 94-101, which describe it as a symbolic taking of custody for preservation).

### 5.2 Confirmation by the Adjudicating Authority (s.8)

- ***Vijay Madanlal Choudhary v. Union of India***, SC (3J), 27.07.2022, [2022] 6 SCR 382 : 2022 INSC 757 *(corpus: 2022_INSC_757)*. It upheld the attachment, adjudication and confiscation scheme with its safeguards: a time-limited PAO, confirmation, appeal, and confiscation only after trial. It also upheld the s.24 burden and the s.45 conditions. It did not consider the IBC. Some issues are reportedly under review; the core holdings govern.

### 5.3 Confirmed, stayed and quashed attachments

- ***Seema Garg v. Deputy Director, ED***, P&H HC (DB, Jaswant Singh and Sant Parkash, JJ.), 06.03.2020, PMLA Appeal 1 of 2019 (O&M) and connected appeals, 2020 SCC OnLine P&H 738 *(corpus: PHHC011207472019)*. ED attached family members' properties, bought in 1991 and 2012, for a 2013 VAT-refund fraud. The owners were not accused.
  - **Time cap (para 15):** filing a complaint against others did not take away the time limit on the attachment, and the attachment had ceased by operation of law.
  - **"Value of any such property" (para 16):** this limb is not "property of equivalent value". It means property into which the proceeds were converted, or which was obtained from them. Pre-offence property can be reached only under the third limb, where the tainted property is taken or held abroad. The Court declined to follow *Axis Bank* and agreed with the Andhra Pradesh High Court in *Satyam Computer Services v. DoE*, 2019 (3) ALD 472.
  - **Reasons to believe (para 17):** specific reasons on material are needed. Copying all three verbs of s.5(1) shows non-application of mind.
  - **Result (paras 19-20):** the appeals were allowed, and the Tribunal's order of 09.08.2019 was set aside.
  - **Status:** the compendium says the SLP was dismissed, which makes no Art.141 law; whether ED challenged the judgment is not verified. **Adverse;** in conflict with *Axis Bank* (paras 104-111). Section 8(3)(a) has since been amended, so read para 15 with the current text.
- ***Prakash Industries Ltd. v. ED*** (Chotia and Fatehpur coal blocks), Delhi HC DB (Kshetarpal and Vaidyanathan Shankar, JJ.), 17.10.2025 (LPA 590/2022 with 588/2022 [?], 2025:DHC:9229-DB) and 03.11.2025 (LPA 102/2023 and 138/2023, 2025:DHC:9626-DB).
  - **Chotia:** a coal-block allocation letter obtained by misrepresentation is itself "property", and gains from exploiting it are proceeds of crime, even though the later chargesheet was narrower.
  - **Fatehpur:** gains from a preferential share sale procured by a false stock-exchange disclosure stay tainted.
  - **Both:** the PAO is the root order and, if sustainable, should run its course before the Adjudicating Authority. ED's appeals were allowed and the attachments restored.
  - **Not in LawBase (DB):** LawBase holds the Single Judge decisions that the DB reversed: W.P.(C) 14999/2021 and 15000/2021, 19.07.2022 *(corpus: DLHC010403972021)*, and W.P.(C) 13361/2018 and 4962/2019, 24.01.2023 *(corpus: DLHC015369112018)*. Do not cite those as current law.
  - **Status:** binding in Delhi. **Pro-ED.**

### 5.4 Freezing, seizure, retention and possession

The compendium cross-refers to *Prakash Industries* and discusses no separate case. In the IBC setting:
- *Foxdom* holds that the NCLT cannot order defreezing;
- *Value Wise* paras 10 and 16 put challenges to s.50 notices sent to the debtor's customers, and to funds ED took during the moratorium, inside the PMLA hierarchy.

*Value Wise* left open whether ED may keep funds after the Appellate Tribunal set the attachment aside, while ED's High Court appeal is pending without a stay.

### 5.5 Confiscation after trial; release on acquittal

- ***B. Rama Raju v. Union of India***, AP HC (DB, Goda Raghuram and R. Kantha Rao, JJ.), 04.03.2011, W.P. 10765, 10769 and 23166 of 2010, 2011 SCC OnLine AP 152 *(corpus: HBHC010111342010)*.
  The judgment has no numbered paragraphs; the references below are to its Summary of Conclusions.
  - **Third-party holders (Issue A):** property held by a person not charged with a scheduled offence "is equally liable to attachment and confiscation".
  - **Mens rea (Issues C-D):** neither mens rea nor knowledge of the property's taint is needed. A non-accused holder has "a defeasible and not a clear title", but may prove bona fide acquisition without knowledge.
  - **Possession before trial (Issues C-D):** s.8(4) possession before conviction is valid.
  - **Burden (Issue F):** the s.24 burden was held to apply only to an accused. This has been overtaken by the 2013 substitution of s.24.
  - **Not part of the "narrow line":** the narrow reading of "value of any such property" comes from a different AP judgment, *Satyam Computer Services v. DoE* (2019). *Rajiv Chakraborty* para 85 held that *B. Rama Raju* says nothing on the moratorium.
  - **Status:** persuasive. **Pro-ED.**
- ***M/s Nav Nirman Builders & Developers Pvt. Ltd. v. Union of India***, SC (M.M. Sundresh and N. Kotiswar Singh, JJ.), 06.02.2026, 2026 INSC 130.
  - **Held:** once the s.8(3) order is under appeal before the Appellate Tribunal, High Court or Supreme Court, that forum's order replaces it. s.8(7) confiscation must not be concluded meanwhile. The confiscation order was set aside, and the s.8(7) proceedings must await the outcome of the appeal.
  - **Status:** binding. **Adverse.** Not in LawBase.

**ED use.** Before pressing s.8(7), including an IP's restitution application under the IBBI circular, confirm that no appeal against the s.8(3) order is pending anywhere.

### 5.6 Actual proceeds and equivalent value

- ***Seema Garg*** (5.3, para 16, following *Satyam Computer Services*) is the narrow view.
- ***Axis Bank*** (paras 104-111) is the wider view. Limbs (ii) and (iii) of s.2(1)(u) reach untainted "deemed tainted" property of the offender when the tainted property cannot be traced or is insufficient, up to a value near or equal to the proceeds. The officer must record a working figure for the proceeds (paras 107, 110).
- ***Sahana Builders & Developers Pvt. Ltd. v. ED***, Appellate Tribunal under SAFEMA, New Delhi (Munishwar Nath Bhandari, J., Chairman), 06.07.2026, FPA-PMLA-946/MUM/2025.
  - **Facts:** in 2004 the appellant received Rs. 10 crore from Unitech for shops it was to develop. The identified diversion period began in 2006-07. ED attached the appellant's fixed deposit.
  - **Held:** an ordinary payment received before the crime period is not proceeds of crime. Its later non-return is a civil liability. Equivalent-value attachment needs proof that the person first received, or was the destination of, proceeds. Confirmation was set aside.
  - **Status:** further challenge not known. **Adverse.** Not in LawBase.

**ED use.** For equivalent-value attachment, record two things specifically: that the person received or routed proceeds, and why the actual proceeds are unavailable. Map dates against the crime period. Where an earlier transaction is said to be sham, backdated or benami, plead and prove that.

### 5.7 Third-party, secured-creditor, guarantor, benami and related-party property

- ***B. Rama Raju*** (5.5) and ***Axis Bank*** (1.4).
- ***Pavana Dibbur v. ED***, SC (Abhay S. Oka and Pankaj Mithal, JJ.), 29.11.2023, [2023] 13 SCR 1049 : 2023 INSC 1029 *(corpus: 2023_INSC_1029)*.
  - **Accused under s.3 (para 27(a)):** a person charged under s.3 need not be an accused in the scheduled offence.
  - **Pre-offence property (para 27(c)):** property acquired before the acts constituting the scheduled offence cannot be connected with its proceeds. This is a finding, not the ground of decision.
  - **Ground and result (paras 22-28):** s.120-B IPC is a scheduled offence only where the conspiracy is to commit an offence in the Schedule (para 27(e)). No such conspiracy was alleged, so no scheduled offence existed, and the complaint was quashed as against the appellant (para 28).
  - **The compendium's note** says the prosecution was quashed only "to the extent it rested on" the earlier property. That is not what para 28 says.
  - **Status:** binding. **Mixed.**
- ***Usha Ananthasubramanian v. Union of India***, SC, 09.12.2019, (2020) 4 SCC 122. A bank's assets could not be attached because its MD, who was accused over her tenure at another bank, headed it. The entity's own involvement must be shown. **Status:** binding. **Adverse.** Not in LawBase.

**ED use.** In group-entity attachments, prove each entity's own receipt of or dealing with proceeds.

### 5.8 Legal title, beneficial ownership and traceable proceeds

The compendium places ***Shiv Charan*** (4.7) here: on the Bombay High Court's view, s.32A can protect a resolution applicant despite a confirmed attachment, while directors stay liable. The Supreme Court's consent order left that open. ***Prakash Industries***, ***V Hotels*** and ***Kalyani Transco*** are cross-referred. *Rajiv Chakraborty* paras 94-101 are the clearest LawBase statement that attachment, even when confirmed, does not take title from the corporate debtor.

### 5.9 The s.14 moratorium and s.32A immunity are different things

Keep them apart in every reply.

| | s.14 moratorium | s.32A immunity |
|---|---|---|
| When it applies | From admission to plan approval or liquidation | From approval of a compliant plan; in liquidation, from the NCLT's approval of a Regulation 32 measure (*Nitin Jain* para 98; *Rajiv Chakraborty* para 105) |
| Condition | None | A genuine change of control to a person who is neither a promoter or related party nor an abettor or conspirator |
| Effect on PMLA | None (*Axis Bank* para 146; *Rajiv Chakraborty* paras 79-83, 93) | Ends the corporate debtor's liability and bars action against the property covered |
| Individuals | Never protected (*P. Mohanraj* para 77) | Never protected (*Manish Kumar*; *Nitin Jain* para 101 Q) |

Authorities: *Rajiv Chakraborty*; *Nitin Jain*; *Manish Kumar*; *Embassy Property*; *P. Mohanraj*.

## Open points and risks

1. **Automatic release under s.32A.** *Vantage Point II* is pending in the Supreme Court (CA 1567/2026). Until it is decided, both sides will cite the consent orders (*JSW*, *Udaipur*, *V Hotels*, *Shiv Charan*), none of which is a precedent.
2. ***Nitin Jain* is adverse and stands.** The compendium says the restraint is "not based on s.32A". The judgment (paras 98, 101(H), (R)-(T)) rests it solely on s.32A: from the NCLT's approval of a Regulation 32 liquidation measure, ED cannot attach the corporate debtor's property. The LPA was disposed of on statements and did not disturb it. *Value Wise* (2026) supports ED on forum in liquidation, but it expressly declined to discuss s.32A (para 15).
3. ***Rajiv Chakraborty* cuts both ways.** The compendium describes partial success for the RP; the judgment dismissed the petition (para 114). Two parts will be used against ED. Paras 105 and 109 treat plan approval or adoption of a liquidation measure as the end point of PMLA attachment. Paras 110-113 and 116 say a PAO gives ED no superior right over secured creditors. The compendium's statement that it ruled on promoters' personal liability is not borne out by the text.
4. ***Axis Bank* in the Supreme Court.** A status quo order of 30.08.2019 is recorded in *Kiran Shah* (as quoted). The present status of that appeal was not verified.
5. ***P. Mohanraj* referred to a larger Bench.** *Value Wise* para 13 reports that *Dineshchand Surana v. UCO Bank* doubted it. A narrower *P. Mohanraj* helps ED. Its para 76 (ED not a creditor) and para 77 (moratorium protects only the corporate debtor) should survive any narrowing, but verify after the reference is decided.
6. **The *Varrsana* SLP.** The compendium treats the 22.07.2019 dismissal as non-speaking; two NCLAT benches call it a confirmation. Do not overstate it either way.
7. **Equivalent value.** *Seema Garg* (narrow, following *Satyam Computer Services*, AP 2019) and *Axis Bank* (wide) are unreconciled; *Sahana Builders* adds a receipt-of-proceeds requirement. The compendium calls the narrow view the "Satyam line" and groups *B. Rama Raju* (2011) with it; *B. Rama Raju* upheld the PMLA broadly and is not part of that line.
8. **Delay.** *Kalyani Transco* (paras 118-124) lets a resolution applicant blame ED's attachment for delay. The compendium views this as tied to BPSL's own history (compendium view, not a holding).
9. **Fresh PAO during CIRP, with no pre-CIRP investigation.** No appellate decision in the compendium squarely decides it. *Rajiv Chakraborty* para 93 is the best general support.
10. **Compendium views (not holdings).** These are drafting advice, not law:
    - ask for moratorium carve-outs at admission;
    - use segregated-account terms in liquidation sales;
    - in its Ready Reckoner, a fresh ED attachment in CIRP is shown as permitted [?], and s.32A may apply by analogy to a liquidation going-concern sale.

## After the 2026 Amendment

The IBC (Amendment) Act, 2026 (Act 6 of 2026; most provisions in force from 26.05.2026) affects this topic as follows.
- **s.32A is unamended,** so the authorities above on its scope still apply.
- **s.14 now carries a new s.14(2A)** (critical supplies) and an Explanation in s.14(3)(b) on sureties. Neither brings PMLA action within the moratorium; the reasoning in *Rajiv Chakraborty* and *Value Wise* is unaffected.
- **s.31(5)-(6) and Explanations I-III codify the clean slate.** s.31(6) extinguishes pre-approval claims "against the corporate debtor and its assets". Resolution applicants will argue that a PMLA attachment is such a "claim". ED's answer is the line that attachment is neither a debt nor a creditor's claim (*Axis Bank* para 141; *P. Mohanraj* para 76; *Rajiv Chakraborty* paras 67-69, 79-83). This is an open question.
- **Old s.33(5) is omitted.** The liquidation moratorium is now in s.33(1)(b), importing s.14(1)(a) and (c). Cases that speak of s.33(5) (*Value Wise*, *Pankaj Dhanuka*) should be read against the new clause; the substance appears unchanged.
- **s.7(5) as substituted makes admission mandatory.** *Three C*-type carve-outs therefore matter more, because ED cannot expect an admission to be refused.
- **s.12A as substituted** allows withdrawal only after the CoC is constituted and before resolution plans are invited, with 90% CoC approval. Watch for withdrawal used to move assets out of a running attachment.
- **Group insolvency (s.59A), Chapter IV-A creditor-initiated insolvency and cross-border provisions** are enacted but not yet notified. Group-wide attachments have no IBC-side rule yet.
