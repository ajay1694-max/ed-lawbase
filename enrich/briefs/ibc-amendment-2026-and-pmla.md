Verify every commencement point below against the notifications current on the day you rely on it.

## Short answer

- **Section 32A is unamended.** The immunity of the corporate debtor and plan-covered property, the change-of-control conditions, and the continuing liability of individuals stand as before. The Chapter 9 and 10 case law (Manish Kumar and others) is unaffected in substance.
- **Section 14 now has an exception for "critical supplies" (s.14(2A)).** This opens a new argument: an RP may say that attached funds or property are needed to keep the debtor running. The Act does not release attached property and gives the RP no power over an attachment. Treat any such plea as **open**, and insist that it be made before the PMLA forum.
- **Section 31(6) codifies the "clean slate".** On plan approval, pre-approval claims "against the corporate debtor and its assets" stand extinguished. ED's position is that an attachment is a statutory restraint on proceeds of crime, not a "claim". Expect RPs and resolution applicants to argue otherwise. The question is **open**.
- **Mandatory admission (s.7(5)) and a narrow withdrawal window (s.12A)** move the fight against collusive or abusive insolvency to s.65 IBC and the avoidance and fraud provisions. They do not affect ED's separate PMLA inquiry into the source of settlement money.
- **New s.28A** lets a guarantor's asset, already in a creditor's possession, be transferred as part of the debtor's resolution. Whether an attached guarantor asset can be transferred, and whether the buyer takes it subject to the attachment, is **open**. Section 32A, being unamended, does not on its face extend to guarantor assets.
- **The liquidation moratorium has moved.** Old s.33(5) is omitted; s.33(1)(b) now imports clauses (a) and (c) of s.14(1). Arguments built on the old s.33(5) need rewriting.
- **Not yet in force:** creditor-initiated insolvency (Chapter IV-A, ss.58A-58K), group insolvency (s.59A) and cross-border insolvency (s.240C).

## 1. Enactment and commencement

- **Passage.** The Bill (No. 107 of 2025) was introduced on 12.08.2025 and sent to a Select Committee. Lok Sabha passed it on 30.03.2026 and Rajya Sabha on 01.04.2026. It received assent on 06.04.2026 and became **Act No. 6 of 2026**.
- **Staggered commencement.** Section 1(2) allows different dates for different provisions. MCA notification **S.O. 2625(E) dated 22.05.2026** brought most provisions into force on **26.05.2026**.
- **Left out of that notification:**
  - the creditor-initiated insolvency chapter;
  - group insolvency (s.59A);
  - cross-border insolvency and the electronic portal (ss.240B, 240C).
- **Transitional limits:** several provisions carry their own savings (for example, substituted s.52(2) does not apply to liquidations that began on or before commencement). New s.242(1A) lets the Government remove difficulties for five years.

## 3. Open questions to watch (none has authoritative answers yet)

1. **Critical supplies and attached property (s.14(2A)).** Can an RP seek release, restoration, limited use or modification of an attachment to pay current dues for critical supplies, and which forum decides? ED should say that only the PMLA Adjudicating Authority or Special Court can, under the PMLA's own provisions (s.8(7)/8(8) and Rule 3A).
2. **Section 31(6) and an attachment.** Is an attachment a "claim" against the corporate debtor and its assets? ED's line: no; it is a restraint on proceeds of crime or equivalent value, and s.32A is the specific code for criminal liability and property. The amended wording gives the other side more textual force than before.
3. **Retrospectivity and "finality".** For a plan approved before 26.05.2026 with an attachment still under challenge, decide case by case. Do not assume an answer from the approval date.
4. **Creditor-initiated insolvency of a debtor under investigation.** With the board still in charge, who represents the debtor? How are records preserved, and how is asset movement controlled?
5. **Section 28A and an attached guarantor asset.** Can it be transferred at all? Does the buyer take subject to the attachment? Does s.32A extend to it?
6. **Mandatory admission and collusive initiation.** Objections to a friendly s.7 petition now have to be framed under s.65 and the fraud and avoidance provisions, not as a discretion to refuse admission.
7. **Group insolvency.** How will attachments across group entities be treated once s.59A rules exist?

## 4. Corrections the compendium itself makes to older propositions

- **Swiss Ribbons para 59.** Any holding that the NCLT must use discretion to keep a debtor out of a mala fide CIRP even when s.7 is satisfied must now be read subject to the new s.7(5). The separate power under s.65 against fraudulent or malicious initiation is unaffected and is now the main route.
- **Rainbow Papers.** The decision was already criticised and distinguished in *Paschimanchal Vidyut Vitran Nigam v. Raman Ispat* (SC 2023). It is now displaced by statute for the post-commencement regime. The timing of that change is unsettled (*Sane Guruji*).
- **Group insolvency.** Do not say the Code has no provision. Section 59A exists but is not in force and has no rules.
