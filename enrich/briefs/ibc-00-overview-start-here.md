## Short answer

1. **The IBC does not suspend the PMLA.** The s.14 moratorium stops recovery of debts from the corporate debtor. A PMLA attachment is not a debt claim, creates no debt and moves no title, so attachment, confirmation and prosecution all continue during CIRP. Leading authorities: *Axis Bank* (Delhi HC 2019) and *Rajiv Chakraborty* (Delhi HC 2022). The NCLAT Larger Bench in *Kiran Shah* disapproved the contrary NCLAT view in *Manoj Kumar Agarwal*.
2. **Ask "what is the property?", not "which Act prevails?".** Property that is itself proceeds of crime was never the debtor's to distribute. Untainted property attached only as **equivalent value** is different. A bona fide secured creditor whose security predates the offence can claim priority over that property (*Axis Bank*, paras 149-165). A provisional attachment gives ED no overriding right over secured creditors; priority is decided separately (*Rajiv Chakraborty*, paras 110-113). Property acquired before the scheduled offence is not proceeds of crime (*Pavana Dibbur*, SC 2023; *Seema Garg*, P&H HC 2020).
3. **Section 32A IBC is the only statutory immunity, and it is conditional.** It protects the **corporate debtor and plan-covered property** once a resolution plan is approved **and** control passes to persons unconnected with the offence. It never protects promoters, directors or officers (*Manish Kumar*, SC 2021). If the conditions were never met, the immunity never arose.
4. **Where s.32A applies, timing is everything.** The Delhi High Court treats approval of the resolution plan, or of a liquidation measure, as the point after which ED can no longer attach the corporate debtor's property (*Rajiv Chakraborty*, paras 105-109; *Nitin Jain*, para 98). Attach, and raise any objection to the buyer's eligibility, before that approval. Its residuary power under s.60(5) IBC does not reach a public-law power exercised under another statute (*Embassy Property*, SC 2019). Challenges go to the PMLA Adjudicating Authority, then the Appellate Tribunal, then the High Court under s.42 PMLA. Restoration or restitution goes to the **Special Court** under s.8(7)/8(8) (NCLAT Principal Bench, *Value Wise Consultancy*, 30.06.2026).
6. **Attachment, confirmation and confiscation are three different acts.**
   - Provisional attachment (s.5) only restrains alienation.
   - Confirmation (s.8(3)) keeps that restraint alive.
   - Only confiscation after trial (s.8(5)-(6)) vests the property in the Government.
   - Confiscation cannot be finalised while an appeal against confirmation is pending (*Nav Nirman Builders*, SC 06.02.2026).
7. **What matters is the date of the investigation, not the date of the attachment order.** A post-CIRP attachment arising from a pre-CIRP investigation does not fall into the insolvency estate (*Anil Kohli*, NCLAT 03.07.2025; the compendium reads this as fact-specific).
8. **Individuals stay exposed throughout.** The moratorium protects only the corporate debtor. Directors and signatories can be prosecuted during CIRP and after the plan is approved, and a plan clause cannot wipe out their personal criminal liability (*Ajay Kumar Radheyshyam Goenka*, SC 2023, a s.138 NI Act case applied by analogy). Section 32A itself never protects individuals (*Manish Kumar*; *Nitin Jain*, Delhi HC 2021, para 101(Q)). Approval of the company's plan does not discharge a personal guarantor (*Lalit Kumar Jain*, SC 2021). One limit applies: after cognizance, ED cannot arrest under s.19 an accused named in the complaint (*Tarsem Lal*, SC 2024).
9. **Settlements are a PMLA question too.** The NCLT's and the CoC's approval of a s.12A settlement says nothing about whether the money paid is clean. That question is left to ED.
10. **Main adverse authorities:** *Nitin Jain* (above). Also *Vantage Point v. Gaurav Misra* (NCLAT, 14.10.2025) held that a pre-CIRP attachment ends automatically on plan approval. ED's appeal, CA 1567/2026, was admitted by the Supreme Court on 06.02.2026. Treat the point as unsettled.

## The four acts on property

| Act | Provision | Nature | Effect on title | Forum to challenge |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Provisional attachment | s.5 PMLA | Administrative, on "reason to believe" | None; restrains transfer for up to 180 days | Contested at confirmation before the Adjudicating Authority; writ only in exceptional cases |
| Confirmation | s.8(1)-(3) PMLA | Quasi-judicial, after notice and hearing | None; continues the restraint pending trial | Appellate Tribunal (s.26), then High Court (s.42), then SC |
| Confiscation | s.8(5)-(6) PMLA | Judicial, after trial | Vests in Central Government | Criminal appellate route |
| Section 32A immunity | s.32A IBC | Statutory, on plan approval and genuine change of control | Ends the corporate debtor's liability and bars action against plan-covered property | Release is sought from the PMLA forum; whether it is automatic is pending (*Vantage Point*). The Delhi HC treats plan approval, or approval of a liquidation measure, as the end-point of the attachment power (*Rajiv Chakraborty*; *Nitin Jain*). |

## Stage-by-stage reckoner

| Stage of the insolvency | What ED may do | Points to watch |
|---|---|---|
| Before CIRP is admitted | Attach, confirm and prosecute in the ordinary way | A confirmed pre-CIRP attachment survives CIRP; the property stays outside the estate (*Kiran Shah*, *Varrsana Ispat*) |
| CIRP admitted (moratorium running) | Continue the investigation; make fresh provisional attachments; file and pursue the confirmation complaint; search, summon and arrest individuals | s.32A is not yet in play. The IRP/RP is a party to notice, not an adjudicator of ED's action. Objections go to the Adjudicating Authority. |
| Resolution plans invited; s.29A eligibility checked | Same as above. Give the RP and CoC the facts of attachment and investigation so that bidders price them in | Connection between the resolution applicant and former promoters is the key s.32A risk; flag it early |
| Plan approved under s.31 | If control has genuinely passed to unconnected persons, the corporate debtor and plan-covered property are protected; individuals are not | Test the change of control on substance (Ch.10). Consent restorations (*V Hotels*, *Udaipur*) keep ED's liberty to re-examine. |
| Liquidation | Attachment continues; the old s.33(5) gave only narrow protection (now s.33(1)(b) after the 2026 Amendment). **Attach before the NCLT approves the sale measure.** | Liquidation alone does not trigger s.32A (*Mekaster*, NCLT Ahmedabad 2025). **But once the NCLT approves a Regulation 32 measure (such as a going-concern sale), s.32A bars further attachment of the corporate debtor's property** (*Nitin Jain*, Delhi HC 15.12.2021, paras 98, 101(H); adverse; ED's LPA disposed of on consent without deciding the law). Restitution applications go to the Special Court. |
| Settlement or withdrawal (s.12A) | ED can examine and, where warranted, attach the settlement money | CoC "commercial wisdom" (*Vallal RCK*) answers the commercial question, not the money-laundering one |

## Third-party claims: a decision path

1. **Nexus.** Was the property acquired before the scheduled offence? If so, it is not proceeds of crime (*Pavana Dibbur*; *Seema Garg*). It can be reached only as equivalent value, and then only on property-specific reasons.
2. **Bona fides.** Did the claimant acquire it for value without notice (*B. Rama Raju*, AP HC 2011)?
3. **Tainted or substitute property?** If the property is actual proceeds, ED keeps it pending trial and confiscation follows conviction. If it is a substitute, a bona fide prior secured creditor can claim priority (*Axis Bank*). Some authorities also require ED to show that the actual proceeds are beyond reach, for example abroad (*Seema Garg*).
4. **Section 32A.** Has a genuine change of control occurred under an approved plan (*Manish Kumar*; *Vantage Point*, pending)? If so, the asset goes to the resolution estate. Third-party claims are still decided on steps 1-3.

## Where the law stood when the compendium went to press (31.07.2026)

**Treated as settled:**
- The moratorium does not suspend PMLA attachment, confirmation or prosecution.
- An attachment creates no debt and no Government ownership.
- Insolvency tribunals cannot rule on the validity of an attachment.
- Only confiscation passes title.
- Property acquired before the scheduled offence is not proceeds of crime.
- Section 32A is valid but conditional, and never protects individuals.
- Section 238 IBC does not give the Code precedence over the PMLA; the two are read together.

**Unsettled:**
- Whether release after plan approval is automatic (*Vantage Point*, in the SC).
- How far equivalent-value attachment reaches when the proceeds are abroad.
- Whether s.32A applies by analogy to a going-concern sale in liquidation.
- What happens to s.32A protection if its conditions are later shown never to have been met.
- Assets attached across group companies whose insolvencies are consolidated.
- Section 32A after confiscation has already vested the property.
- Avoidance recoveries under ss.43-51 IBC versus s.8(8) restitution aimed at the same value.
- Whether state depositor-protection laws other than Maharashtra's MPID Act give the *NSEL* result.
- Every interface question created by the IBC (Amendment) Act, 2026 (see `ibc-amendment-2026-and-pmla`).

## How the case law developed, 2018-2026

- **2018-2019: a jurisdictional fight.** The NCLAT in *Varrsana Ispat* and *Rotomac* held that ED is not a creditor, so s.14 does not reach attachment. The Supreme Court dismissed the appeal in *Varrsana* without reasons, which declares no law.
- **2019-2022: a question of property.** The Delhi High Court in *Axis Bank* separated tainted property from equivalent-value property. *Embassy Property* (SC) confined the NCLT to its statute. *Rajiv Chakraborty* (Delhi HC 2022) dismissed an RP's challenge and explained why *P. Mohanraj* (moratorium covers s.138 NI Act cases) does not reach PMLA attachment. The Supreme Court has since referred *P. Mohanraj* itself to a larger bench (*Dineshchand Surana v. UCO Bank*, as recorded in *Value Wise*, para 13).
- **December 2019: legislation.** Section 32A was inserted by Ordinance and then by Act 1 of 2020. The SC upheld it in *Manish Kumar* (2021) as immunity for the entity, never for individuals.
- **2022: constitutional consolidation.** *Vijay Madanlal Choudhary* upheld the PMLA scheme as self-contained. It did not decide insolvency questions.
- **2024-2026: reconciliation.**
  - The BPSL litigation ended in *Kalyani Transco* (SC 26.09.2025), and the plan was implemented.
  - Restoration during the trial under the second proviso to s.8(8) PMLA (read with the Restoration of Property Rules, 2016) became the usual route. The statute does not require ED's consent, but in these cases the orders rested on ED's no-objection or affidavit (*V Hotels*, *Udaipur*, *Shiv Charan*). Those orders say in terms that they are not precedents.
  - *Nav Nirman* sequenced confiscation after confirmation appeals.
  - *Value Wise* (NCLAT Principal Bench) closed the NCLT door during both CIRP and liquidation.
  - The IBBI's Circular of 04.11.2025 set the procedure for an insolvency professional's restitution application to the Special Court.

## Using these briefs

- Cite the judgment, not this page. Where a case is in LawBase, its brief gives the case_id. Otherwise obtain the order from the tribunal's website.
- The compendium separates holdings from its compilers' views. These briefs keep that separation; a "Compendium view" is argument, not authority.
- Consent orders and SLP dismissals are recorded as such. They bind the parties but declare no law.
