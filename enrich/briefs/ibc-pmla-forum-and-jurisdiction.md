## Short answer

1. **The PMLA has its own ladder.** It runs PAO (s.5), then the Adjudicating Authority (s.8), then the Appellate Tribunal (s.26), then the High Court on law or fact (s.42), then the Supreme Court. Restoration, release and restitution go to the Special Court under s.8(5)-(8). The PMLA is "a self-contained Code" for attachment (*UoI (ED) v. Udaipur Entertainment World*, Rajasthan HC 2025, para 15). Its scheme was upheld in *Vijay Madanlal Choudhary* (SC 2022).
2. **The NCLT/NCLAT is not on that ladder.** Its residuary jurisdiction under s.60(5) IBC does not extend to public-law action taken under another statute (*Embassy Property*, SC). The NCLAT Principal Bench has now said so for CIRP and liquidation alike: "the tribunals constituted under the IBC are not the forums which can entertain any plea against the ED" (*Value Wise*, 30.06.2026, para 14).
3. **Who may challenge.** The corporate debtor, acting through the RP or liquidator; the SRA; a secured creditor or other third party with a "legitimate interest"; homebuyers. Each goes to the PMLA forum, or to the Special Court for restitution (*Rajiv Chakraborty*, Delhi HC 2022, paras 110-115; *Value Wise* paras 14, 16; IBBI Circular of 04.11.2025).
4. **Object to maintainability at once** whenever an RP, liquidator or SRA asks the NCLT/NCLAT to release, refund, suspend or ignore an ED attachment, or to quash a s.50 PMLA notice (*Value Wise* para 16). Rely on *Embassy Property*, *Value Wise*, *Axis Bank*, *Varrsana Ispat* and *Kiran Shah*.
5. **If *Manoj Kumar Agarwal* (NCLAT 2021) is cited, answer with *Kiran Shah*.** The compendium reports that the NCLAT Larger Bench disapproved it. *Value Wise* para 12 now follows the *Varrsana Ispat* and *Kiran Shah* line.
6. **Insist on being impleaded.** An NCLAT order on attached property passed without hearing ED was recalled (*Vantage Point I*). The Rajasthan High Court restored a stay because ED had not been made a party before the NCLT (*Udaipur*, para 21).
7. **The NCLAT's own adverse ruling, *Vantage Point II* (14.10.2025), is under appeal** (SC CA 1567 of 2026, admitted 06.02.2026). It treated the attachment as ceasing automatically under s.32A. Do not let it be cited as settled.
8. **Writ petitions will be entertained.** The Delhi High Court decided *Rajiv Chakraborty* and *Nitin Jain* under Article 226 despite the PMLA appeals, and quashed proceedings against a resolved company in *Bhushan Power & Steel*. Do not rely on the alternate-remedy objection alone; reply on merits.
9. **Section 238 IBC does not rank the IBC above the PMLA.** The courts harmonise the two statutes, and s.32A marks how far the PMLA gives way (*Rajiv Chakraborty* paras 102-109; *Nitin Jain* para 101(C)-(H)).
10. **Use *Kalyani Transco* (SC, 26.09.2025) carefully.** It is strong on the object of s.32A and on attachment as a cause of implementation delay. Despite the compendium's banners, its own compilers' note says it does not decide whether the NCLT can rule on a PMLA attachment.

## 1. The forum map

| Step | Provision | Forum | What is decided |
|---|---|---|---|
| Provisional attachment | s.5 PMLA | Authorised officer of ED | Restraint on transfer for up to 180 days, on recorded "reasons to believe" |
| Confirmation | s.8(1)-(3) PMLA | Adjudicating Authority (PMLA) | Whether the property is involved in money laundering; hearing for all persons with an interest |
| First appeal | s.26 PMLA | Appellate Tribunal | Appeal by ED or any aggrieved person |
| Second appeal | s.42 PMLA | High Court | Questions of law or fact arising from the Tribunal's order |
| Further appeal | Art. 136 | Supreme Court | Discretionary |
| Release, restoration, restitution | s.8(5)-(8) PMLA, Rule 3A Restoration Rules | Special Court | Confiscation after trial; release if no offence; interim release and restitution to legitimate claimants |
| Writ | Arts. 226 and 32 | High Court, Supreme Court | Available, mainly on jurisdiction and vires |

**Authorities.**
- ***Vijay Madanlal Choudhary v. Union of India***, SC, 27.07.2022, [2022] 6 SCR 382 *(corpus: 2022_INSC_757)*. Status: good law; some aspects referred for review, but the core holdings govern (per compendium). It upheld the attachment, adjudication, confiscation and prosecution scheme with its built-in safeguards: a time-bound PAO, confirmation by the Adjudicating Authority, appeal to the Appellate Tribunal, and confiscation only after trial. It does not consider the IBC.
- ***UoI (ED) v. Udaipur Entertainment World***, Rajasthan HC (Division Bench), D.B. Special Appeal Writ 685/2024, 28.03.2025, para 15 *(corpus: RJHC020983432024)*. A confirmed attachment can be challenged only before the Appellate Tribunal and then the High Court; for attachment, the PMLA is a self-contained code. Status: interim order.
- ***Seema Garg v. Deputy Director, ED***, P&H HC (Division Bench), PMLA Appeal 1 of 2019 and connected, 06.03.2020 *(corpus: PHHC011207472019)*. This is an example of the s.42 appeal in use. The High Court set aside the Tribunal's order (paras 19-20). The case is not an IBC case. Its narrower view of equivalent-value attachment is in tension with *Axis Bank* (per compendium).

## 2. Locus: who can challenge, and how

- **The corporate debtor, through the IRP/RP.** Under s.17 IBC the corporate debtor is managed by the IRP/RP. Under s.25(2)(b) the RP represents and acts for it in judicial and quasi-judicial proceedings. The RP therefore defends the debtor before the PMLA Adjudicating Authority and appeals to the Tribunal.
  - *Rajiv Chakraborty, RP of Era Infra Engineering v. ED*, Delhi HC, W.P.(C) 9531/2020, 11.11.2022 *(corpus: DLHC010046592020)*. The RP challenged the PAOs and their confirmation. The petition was dismissed (para 114), but the RP was left free to seek release "in accordance with law" (para 115). The Court said it was open to the RP "to approach the competent authorities under the PMLA" (para 113).
- **The liquidator.**
  - *Value Wise Consultancy v. Deputy Director, ED*, NCLAT Principal Bench, Comp. App. (AT)(Ins) 1226-1227 of 2022, 30.06.2026 *(corpus: EDPORTAL_18_2026-06-30)*. The liquidator's applications under s.60(5) IBC sought withdrawal of two PAOs, refund of about Rs. 2.29 crore that ED had withdrawn from the debtor's account during the moratorium, and quashing of s.50 PMLA notices to the debtor's customers. The NCLT dismissed them for want of jurisdiction, and the NCLAT agreed (paras 14, 16-17).
  - On the money released by the Appellate Tribunal's order (under challenge in the Bombay High Court without a stay), the NCLAT said the liquidator "may have to approach the High Court" (para 16).
- **Insolvency professionals generally.** IBBI Circular IBBI/CIRP/87/2025 of 04.11.2025, para 2, advises an IP to apply to the Special Court under s.8(7) or 8(8) PMLA for restitution of attached assets (quoted in *Value Wise* para 14). Compendium view (not a holding): the circular is procedural and confers no right to restitution.
- **Secured creditors and other bona fide third parties.**
  - *Deputy Director, ED v. Axis Bank*, Delhi HC (R.K. Gauba J.), Crl.A. 143/2018 and connected, 02.04.2019, 2019 SCC OnLine Del 7854 : (2019) 259 DLT 500 *(corpus: DLHC012396152018)*. Status: under appeal; the Supreme Court passed only a status quo order on 30.08.2019 and did not stay the judgment (as recorded in *Kiran Shah*, quoted in *Rajiv Chakraborty* para 89).
  - Held. ED attaching proceeds of crime "does not stand as a creditor" (para 141), and the s.14 moratorium cannot stop a PMLA attachment (para 146). A person with a legitimate interest may go to the Adjudicating Authority, the Tribunal and the court, show good faith, and seek release or restoration (para 150). A bona fide interest acquired before the criminal activity is not defeated by attachment of untainted, equivalent-value property (paras 162-163). The attachment stays valid but "takes a back-seat" while the secured creditor realises its security (paras 164-165). Tainted property gets no such protection (para 161).
  - **Forum.** Once confirmation is final, or confiscation is ordered, or the trial has begun, a third-party claim is decided only by the Special Court under s.8(7)/(8) (paras 167-170).
  - *Rajiv Chakraborty* paras 110-113 and 116 read *Axis Bank* to mean that a PAO gives ED no "superior or overriding right in property"; priority between ED and secured creditors is settled independently. Expect RPs and lenders to cite this. Answer by showing that the property is tainted (*Axis Bank* para 161).
  - Compendium view (not a holding): *Axis Bank* is also authority that any challenge, by the debtor or a creditor, lies before the PMLA forums and not the NCLT/NCLAT.
- **The SRA.** In practice SRAs have obtained release by consent restitution under the second proviso to s.8(8) in the Supreme Court, not by an NCLT order (*ED v. JSW Steel*, 11.12.2024; *Udaipur*, 10.10.2025; *V Hotels*, 11.11.2025; *Shiv Charan*, 16.07.2026 [?]; all per compendium, the first also as quoted in *corpus: DLHC010218602024*). The one appellate order that let an SRA bypass the PMLA forums is *Vantage Point II*, which is under appeal.
- **Financial creditors by writ.** *Nishit Badola v. Union of India*, Delhi HC, W.P.(C) 174/2024, 2026:DHC:5770, July 2026 [date ?] (per compendium). A creditor's writ was disposed of once the Supreme Court (20.11.2025, 06.02.2026) and the Special Court (27.11.2025) had ordered restoration and monetisation of attached assets for creditors. The operative relief came from those courts, not the writ court.

**Use.** When an IP writes to ED or files in the NCLT for release, point to the Special Court route under s.8(7)/8(8), the IBBI circular and *Value Wise*. Engage on merits only in the PMLA forum.

## 3. Limits of the NCLT's s.60(5) jurisdiction

- ***Embassy Property Developments Pvt. Ltd. v. State of Karnataka***, SC, 03.12.2019 [?], (2020) 13 SCC 308 : 2019 SCC OnLine SC 1542. The citations are verified as given in *Value Wise* paras 8(d) and 14; the judgment is not in the corpus. Status: binding.
  - Held (per compendium). Section 60(5)(c) is wide but does not make the NCLT a court of plenary jurisdiction. Decisions taken in public-law functions under another statute (there, refusal of a mining lease) are challengeable only by writ or before the forum that statute creates. The NCLT and NCLAT erred in entertaining the challenge.
  - *Value Wise* para 14 reads *Embassy* as limiting the NCLT's jurisdiction to what the IBC requires "and no more".
- ***Value Wise***, paras 12, 14 and 16 *(corpus: EDPORTAL_18_2026-06-30)*.
  - The moratorium under s.14 (CIRP) and the old s.33(5) (liquidation) reaches only proceedings that could add to the debtor's civil debt. It does not reach penal, public-law proceedings, even if they reduce the assets available (para 12).
  - The IBC is not "a holy Ganges" to wash the debtor of PMLA criminality (para 12).
  - Whether to attach is for ED; the PMLA's adjudicatory mechanism alone hears any challenge, including to a s.50 notice (para 16).
- ***UoI (ED) v. Udaipur Entertainment World***, Rajasthan HC DB, 28.03.2025, paras 18-19, 21, 24 *(corpus: RJHC020983432024)*.
  - The NCLT is a tribunal of limited jurisdiction (para 18).
  - By vacating a confirmed attachment that was under appeal, with a status quo order of the Appellate Tribunal in force, the NCLT had in effect ended the PMLA proceedings. Whether it had "inherent jurisdiction" to do so was a serious question (para 19).
  - Where the challenge is to inherent lack of jurisdiction, ED is not barred by its failure to appeal or to participate, especially as it was never made a party (paras 21, 24).
  - Status: interim order restoring a stay; the dispute later ended in the Supreme Court consent order of 10.10.2025 (per compendium).

**Use.** Lead with *Embassy Property* for the principle and *Value Wise* for its application to ED. Add *Udaipur* (Rajasthan HC) where the NCLT has already acted without hearing ED.

## 4. Exclusive jurisdiction of the PMLA Adjudicating Authority, Appellate Tribunal and Special Court

- **Statutory basis.** Sections 5, 8, 26 and 42 PMLA build the hierarchy. Section 41 PMLA bars civil courts from matters within the competence of the Adjudicating Authority and the Tribunal (ED relied on it in *Value Wise* para 8(f)). Section 71 gives the PMLA overriding effect.
- **Authorities.** *Value Wise* para 16 *(corpus)*; *Udaipur* (Rajasthan HC) para 15 *(corpus)*; *Axis Bank* (per compendium, as above); *Varrsana Ispat* (below).
- **Keep the forum point separate from Axis Bank's priority point.** Who decides (the PMLA forum) is a different question from what a secured creditor may claim (priority over untainted substitute property only).

## 6. Writ jurisdiction of the High Courts despite the statutory appeals

- ***Rajiv Chakraborty***, W.P.(C) 9531/2020 *(corpus: DLHC010046592020)*. The Delhi High Court entertained the writ although the PAOs had been confirmed and an appeal lay to the Tribunal. ED's preliminary objection failed partly because ED had itself argued that the NCLT has no jurisdiction, so it could not send the RP back there, and because NCLAT benches had differed (paras 11-12). The petition was dismissed on merits (para 114).
- ***Nitin Jain, Liquidator of PSL Ltd. v. ED***, W.P.(C) 3261/2021, 15.12.2021 *(corpus: DLHC010126002021)*. The Court rejected ED's preliminary objection that a PAO issued while the writ was pending made the petition infructuous. The jurisdictional challenge survived (para 101(A)-(B)). The Court then restrained ED under s.32A (para 102). ED's LPA was disposed of by consent on 27.02.2025, with the law left open *(corpus: DLHC010404622021)*. **Adverse.**
- ***Bhushan Power & Steel Ltd. v. UoI***, W.P.(CRL) 1261/2024, 30.01.2025 *(corpus: DLHC010218602024)*. A petition under Article 226 and s.482 CrPC. Cognizance and proceedings in the PMLA complaint were quashed as against the resolved company only, on ED's concession under s.32A(1).
- ***UoI (ED) v. Udaipur Entertainment World***, Rajasthan HC DB *(corpus: RJHC020983432024)*. ED itself used the writ route to attack an NCLT order for inherent lack of jurisdiction (paras 21, 24). The Court noted that the Bombay High Court had dismissed a former director's writ against the same NCLT order for not using the statutory appeal (para 22).

**Compendium view (not a holding).** The writ remains available especially where a substantial question about the interplay of the two statutes arises. The compendium also cross-refers *P. Mohanraj v. Shah Brothers Ispat* here.

**Use.**
- Plead the alternate remedy, but file a full reply on merits.
- Where the NCLT has acted on an attachment, ED may itself seek a writ on inherent lack of jurisdiction rather than an IBC appeal, as in *Udaipur*. File early to avoid an objection of delay.

## 7. Sections 60(5), 63, 231 and 238 IBC, and the non-obstante clauses

**The provisions (our summary).**
- s.60(5): the NCLT's residuary jurisdiction over applications by or against the corporate debtor and questions arising out of or in relation to its insolvency resolution or liquidation.
- s.63: civil courts and authorities may not entertain suits or proceedings on matters within the NCLT/NCLAT's jurisdiction.
- s.231: civil courts have no jurisdiction where the Adjudicating Authority is empowered to pass orders, and no injunction lies against action under its orders.
- s.238: the IBC overrides inconsistent laws.
- On the PMLA side: s.41 (civil court bar) and s.71 (overriding effect).

**LawBase analysis.** Sections 63 and 231 protect matters on which the NCLT *has* jurisdiction. Since the NCLT has no jurisdiction over the validity of a PMLA attachment (*Embassy Property*; *Value Wise*), those sections do not bar the PMLA forums. We found no authority applying ss.63 or 231 against ED.

**Authorities on s.238 versus s.71.**
- ***Rajiv Chakraborty*** paras 102-109 *(corpus: DLHC010046592020)*.
  - The later-enactment rule for competing non-obstante clauses is not "the solitary principle" (para 103).
  - The IBC and the PMLA have distinct subjects (para 108).
  - Section 32A, inserted in 2020, is the legislature's last word on how far the PMLA yields to the IBC. Section 238 cannot be read to have "an impact far greater than that envisaged in Section 32A" (paras 108-109).
- ***Nitin Jain*** para 101(C)-(H) *(corpus: DLHC010126002021)*.
  - First ask whether there is a real, irreconcilable conflict between the two statutes (para 101(C)).
  - The two statutes occupy distinct fields (para 101(F)).
  - Courts must reconcile them where they meet (para 101(G)).
  - That reconciliation is answered "solely on the anvil of Section 32A" (para 101(H)).
  - The compendium groups this case with *Anil Kohli* as harmonious-construction authority but says it did not rest on s.32A. The judgment shows that it did.
- ***Solidaire India Ltd. v. Fairgrowth Financial Services Ltd.***, SC, [2001] 1 SCR 932 = (2001) 3 SCC 71 *(corpus: 2001_INSC_76)*. LawBase dates it 07.02.2001; the compendium says 06.02.2001. Ordinarily the later special statute prevails. *Rajiv Chakraborty* para 103 treats this as a rule of construction that yields to subject matter and object.
- ***Innoventive Industries Ltd. v. ICICI Bank***, SC, 31.08.2017, (2018) 1 SCC 407 (per compendium). Section 238 overrode a State relief law in the same commercial field. Distinguish it: the PMLA occupies a different, penal field and has its own non-obstante clause.
- ***P. Mohanraj v. Shah Brothers Ispat***, SC, 01.03.2021, [2021] 14 SCR 204 = (2021) 6 SCC 258 *(corpus: 2021_INSC_133)*. A s.138 NI Act proceeding against the corporate debtor falls within s.14 because it is in substance compensatory. Two points help ED: para 76 (SCR numbering; para 100 in SCC) accepts that PMLA confiscation is done by the Government acting under statute "and not as a creditor", and para 77 holds that the moratorium protects only the corporate debtor, not its directors. *Rajiv Chakraborty* paras 79-80 confine it to debt-recovery proceedings and hold that PMLA attachment is not such a proceeding. *Value Wise* para 13 records that another Bench of the Supreme Court has doubted *P. Mohanraj* and referred the issue to a larger bench (*Dineshchand Surana v. UCO Bank*, as cited there).

## 8. Can the NCLT set aside, ignore, suspend or merely declare the cessation of an attachment?

- **Set aside, recall or suspend: no.** *Value Wise* para 16; *Embassy Property*; *Varrsana Ispat* (per compendium); *Udaipur* (Rajasthan HC) para 19 (prima facie).
- **Ignore it in the plan: no.** In *Vantage Point* the NCLT itself declined to release the attached property and left the SRA to the PMLA forums (per compendium). That is the correct course.
- **Declare that it has ceased under s.32A: contested.**
  - *Vantage Point II* (NCLAT 14.10.2025) did so. It is under appeal.
  - Compendium view (not a holding): recognising cessation by force of law is permissible, while setting aside the attachment is not. No court has adopted this distinction. It also sits uneasily with *Value Wise* para 16 and *Rajiv Chakraborty* paras 113-115.
- **BPSL history.** The NCLAT did set aside a post-approval PAO in 2020 (see section 5). ED's challenge was resolved by consent in the Supreme Court without any ruling.

**Use.** Ask the NCLT to record that any question about an ED attachment is left to the PMLA forums, as the NCLT did in *Vantage Point* at the plan-approval stage.

***Kalyani Transco v. Bhushan Power and Steel Ltd.***, SC (B.R. Gavai CJ, Satish Chandra Sharma and K. Vinod Chandran JJ.), 26.09.2025, 2025 INSC 1165, CA 1808 of 2020 and connected (per compendium; not in corpus). Status: binding.

**Held (per compendium).**
- The CoC is not functus officio on plan approval; it continues until the plan is implemented or liquidation is ordered (para 85).
- Appeals under ss.61(3) and 62 IBC lie only on the specified grounds or on questions of law (paras 89-96; some numbers [?]).
- Extension of implementation time by the CoC did not make the plan open-ended (paras 99-102 [?]).
- Object and effect of s.32A (paras 116-117).
- ED's continued attachment and prosecution prevented the handover of unencumbered assets, and the delay was not the resolution applicant's fault (paras 118-124).
- Para 2.22 records the reservation attached to the 11.12.2024 order.

**The compendium is inconsistent about what *Kalyani Transco* decides.**
- Its Chapter 12 and 14 banners, and its Chapter 10 note, present the judgment as the Supreme Court's statement that the NCLT, whose powers are confined to ss.31 and 60 IBC, cannot decide the validity of an ED attachment, confirmation, confiscation or re-attachment.
- Its compilers' note in Chapter 12 says the judgment does **not** decide NCLT/NCLAT jurisdiction over the validity of a PMLA attachment, and that *Embassy Property* is the authority for that point.
- We have not been able to read the judgment in LawBase. Until a reader confirms the paragraph, **do not cite *Kalyani Transco* for the jurisdictional bar.** Cite *Embassy Property* and *Value Wise* instead.

**Use.** Expect SRAs to cite paras 118-124 to excuse delay wherever an attachment subsists. Answer that those paragraphs turn on BPSL's own history of NCLAT and Supreme Court stays. Expect them also to say that upholding the NCLAT judgment of 17.02.2020 affirmed its s.32A ruling on the PAO. Answer with the 11.12.2024 reservation, which *Kalyani Transco* itself records at para 2.22.

## 10. Concluding authority: *Vijay Madanlal Choudhary*

*Vijay Madanlal Choudhary v. Union of India*, SC, 27.07.2022, [2022] 6 SCR 382 *(corpus: 2022_INSC_757)* upheld the PMLA's attachment, adjudication, confiscation and prosecution machinery, with its own forums and safeguards. The compendium uses it to close Chapter 12. The PMLA hierarchy is constitutionally sound and keeps its jurisdiction whatever the insolvency status of the person whose property is attached. The judgment did not consider the IBC; use it for the PMLA side of the argument only.

## Open points and risks

1. ***Vantage Point II*** is the main adverse authority and is pending in the Supreme Court. The interim terms reported in the compendium (development allowed, no alienation, ED verification of disbursements) are garbled [?].
2. **The 2020 BPSL NCLAT judgment** set aside a PAO on s.32A grounds, and was never reviewed on merits because ED's appeal ended in consent. *Kalyani Transco* upheld the NCLAT judgment as regards the plan. Watch for attempts to treat that as approval of the whole judgment.
3. ***Nitin Jain*** remains an adverse Delhi High Court ruling on s.32A in liquidation, with the law left open in appeal.
4. ***Kalyani Transco* on jurisdiction.** The compendium contradicts itself. Do not cite the judgment for the NCLT bar until the paragraph is read.
5. ***Varrsana Ispat*.** *Value Wise* calls the Supreme Court's dismissal a confirmation; the compendium says it was non-speaking. Rely on the NCLAT reasoning and on *Kiran Shah* and *Value Wise*, not on the dismissal.
6. **Writ petitions** by RPs, liquidators and SRAs are entertained in practice. The alternate-remedy objection is weak where the challenge is framed as one of jurisdiction.
7. ***P. Mohanraj*** is reportedly before a larger bench (per *Value Wise* para 13). The outcome may change how the s.14 moratorium arguments are framed.
8. **Compendium views that are not holdings**: the reading of *Axis Bank* as a forum ruling; the "recognition vs setting aside" distinction; the procedural-only reading of the IBBI circular.

## After the 2026 Amendment

The IBC (Amendment) Act, 2026 (Act 6 of 2026) brought most provisions into force on 26.05.2026 (S.O. 2625(E), 22.05.2026).
- **Sections 60(5), 63, 231 and 238 are not reported as amended.** *Embassy Property* and *Value Wise* continue to govern the NCLT's limits. Check the amended text before relying on this.
- **The liquidation moratorium has moved.** Old s.33(5) is omitted, and s.33(1)(b) now imports s.14(1)(a) and (c). *Value Wise* treated the old s.33(5) and s.14 alike. Its reasoning (para 12: the moratorium reaches only proceedings that add to civil debt) should carry over, but the point is new. LawBase analysis: clause (c) concerns enforcement of a security interest, and ED does not attach as a creditor (*Axis Bank* para 141, as quoted in *Rajiv Chakraborty* para 68).
- **Section 31(6)** extinguishes pre-approval claims against the corporate debtor and its assets. Expect SRAs to ask the NCLT to declare a subsisting attachment extinguished under s.31(6). Object that the NCLT cannot rule on a PMLA attachment, and that an attachment is not a "claim". The question is **open**.
- **Section 7(5)** (mandatory admission) and the substituted **s.12A** do not change the forum analysis.
- **Not yet notified:** Chapter IV-A (creditor-initiated insolvency, ss.58A-58K), s.59A (group insolvency) and ss.240B-240C (cross-border). Their forum provisions are untested against the PMLA.
