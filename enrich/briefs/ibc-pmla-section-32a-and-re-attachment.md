## Short answer

1. **Section 32A is valid and conditional.** It ends the corporate debtor's liability for pre-CIRP offences, and bars action against its plan-covered property, only when (i) a resolution plan is approved under s.31, (ii) the plan changes management or control, and (iii) the new controller is not a former promoter, manager or related party, and is not a person whom the investigating agency has reported or complained against as an abettor or conspirator (*Manish Kumar*, SC 2021, paras 252-254, 258).
2. **It protects the company and its property, never the people.** Directors, officers in default and anyone "directly or indirectly involved" stay liable to prosecution, and their own property stays attachable (s.32A(1), second proviso; s.32A(2), Explanation (ii); *Manish Kumar* paras 253-254).
3. **Nothing happens on admission.** Immunity starts on approval of the plan, not on admission or during CIRP (*Manish Kumar* para 252; *Value Wise*, NCLAT 2026, para 15). In liquidation, the Delhi High Court has held that the bar on attachment starts when the NCLT approves the liquidation measure or sale (*Nitin Jain*, Delhi HC 2021, paras 98, 101(R)-(T)).
4. **Whether a subsisting attachment ends by itself on plan approval is unsettled.** The only appellate ruling saying it does, *Vantage Point II* (NCLAT, 14.10.2025), is under appeal in the Supreme Court (CA 1567 of 2026, admitted 06.02.2026). Every Supreme Court disposal so far (BPSL handover order, *V Hotels*, *Udaipur*, *Shiv Charan*) released property by **restitution under s.8(8) PMLA**, by consent, and expressly declined to decide the point.
5. **Do not concede that consent orders decide s.32A.** The 11.12.2024 BPSL order says in terms that the Court expressed no opinion on s.32A(2) or on ED's power to attach during CIRP (quoted verbatim in *Bhushan Power & Steel v. UoI*, Delhi HC 30.01.2025, para 2.16). *V Hotels*, *Udaipur* and *Shiv Charan* are non-precedential by their own terms (per compendium).
6. **Re-attachment is argued as "the immunity never arose", not as "ED withdraws the immunity".** No authority recognises a power to withdraw a validly acquired immunity. The consent orders reserve liberty to ED if the successful resolution applicant (SRA) proves to be connected with old management or a beneficiary of the proceeds (per compendium).
7. **Test the change of control on substance.** Use the see-through approach of *ArcelorMittal* (s.29A) and the relevant-time approach of *Phoenix ARC* by analogy. Object before the plan is approved and before implementation is substantial; *MBL Infrastructures* shows courts will not easily unwind an implemented plan.
8. **Duty to cooperate survives.** Section 32A(3) obliges the corporate debtor and any protected person to assist investigators (*Manish Kumar* para 255). A liquidator likewise cannot refuse information to PMLA authorities (*Nitin Jain* para 100).
9. **Keep the PAO chronology bullet-proof.** Where the plan is approved close to the date of the provisional attachment order (PAO), the date and mode of issue and service will be examined document by document (*V Hotels*, Bombay HC 28.11.2024, per compendium).
10. **Any challenge to a re-attachment goes to the PMLA forums.** The NCLT/NCLAT has no jurisdiction over ED's attachment (*Value Wise* para 16; *Embassy Property*, SC). See the companion brief `ibc-pmla-forum-and-jurisdiction`.

## 1. Section 32A and s.8(8) PMLA are different mechanisms

**Proposition.** Section 32A IBC ends liability and bars action by force of statute once its conditions are met. Section 8(8) PMLA, and its second proviso read with Rule 3A of the Restoration Rules, 2016, is a court-ordered restoration of attached or confiscated property to a claimant with a legitimate interest. The two are distinct.

- **What the Supreme Court actually did in BPSL.** On ED's affidavit, the Court directed ED to hand over the attached BPSL properties to JSW Steel "in view of Section 8(8) of the PMLA read with Rule 3A", stressing that the PAO had been passed after NCLT approval of the plan, and recording that it expressed no opinion on the interpretation of s.32A(2) or on ED's power to attach during CIRP (*ED v. JSW Steel*, SC 11.12.2024, order quoted in full in *Bhushan Power & Steel v. UoI*, Delhi HC 30.01.2025, para 2.16 *(corpus: DLHC010218602024)*). ED's affidavit took the stand that s.32A was not retrospective.
- **Consent restitutions.** *Udaipur Entertainment World* (SC 10.10.2025), *V Hotels* (SC 11.11.2025) and *Shiv Charan* (SC 16.07.2026 [?]) all used the second proviso to s.8(8) on ED's affidavit and left the law open (per compendium).
- **IBBI Circular IBBI/CIRP/87/2025 (04.11.2025).** Para 2 advises insolvency professionals that, where ED has attached the corporate debtor's assets, they may apply to the Special Court under s.8(7) or 8(8) PMLA for restitution (quoted in *Value Wise*, NCLAT 30.06.2026, para 14 *(corpus: EDPORTAL_18_2026-06-30)*). Compendium view (not a holding): the circular is procedural and creates no substantive right to restitution.

**Use.** When an SRA or RP says "s.32A applies, release the property", answer that release of attached property is ordered by the PMLA forum, and point to the route the Supreme Court itself used. Offer restitution under s.8(8) on ED's terms where the facts justify it, rather than conceding that s.32A operates automatically.

## 2. Object and constitutional validity

**Authority.** *Manish Kumar v. Union of India*, SC, 19.01.2021, [2021] 14 SCR 895 = (2021) 5 SCC 1 *(corpus: 2021_INSC_28)*. Status: good law. Paragraph numbers in this brief follow the SCR text in LawBase; other reports number differently (SCR para 257 is para 280 in SCC OnLine).

**Held.**
- Section 32A was upheld against challenges under Articles 14, 19, 21 and 300A. The Court treated it as an economic measure meant to attract resolution applicants who would otherwise stay away from a debtor exposed to prosecution for its former management's acts (paras 256-257).
- Para 252 breaks s.32A(1) into four conditions: approval under s.31; change in management or control; the new controller not being a promoter, manager or related party; and the new controller not being a person against whom the investigating authority has material showing abetment or conspiracy and has filed a report or complaint. If that last condition fails, the corporate debtor's liability "will remain unaffected".
- Para 254: the property bar in s.32A(2) needs the same conditions, and the Explanation gives "action against the property" the widest meaning (attachment, seizure, retention, confiscation). The bar also protects a person who buys the debtor's property in CIRP or liquidation, subject to the same limits.
- Para 258: the immunity rests on conditions; "The new management cannot be the disguised avatar of the old management."
- Paras 253 and 257: the wrongdoers "remain liable".

**Clean-slate parallels, not s.32A authorities.**
- *Ghanshyam Mishra & Sons v. Edelweiss ARC*, SC 13.04.2021, (2021) 9 SCC 657: claims not in an approved plan are extinguished, including government dues (per compendium). It concerns civil claims only.
- *CoC of Essar Steel v. Satish Kumar Gupta*, SC 15.11.2019, (2020) 8 SCC 531: the CoC's commercial wisdom is not justiciable, and the SRA takes a fresh slate (per compendium). Compendium view (not a holding): commercial wisdom answers whether a plan is commercially sound, not whether the money behind it is clean.
- *Ebix Singapore v. CoC of Educomp*, SC 13.09.2021, (2022) 2 SCC 401: a CoC-approved plan cannot be withdrawn by the applicant (per compendium). Compendium view (not a holding): ED should therefore put its attachment position on record before plans are submitted.

**Use.** Cite *Manish Kumar* paras 252-254 and 258 for the conditions and para 253 for continuing individual liability. Do not let *Ghanshyam Mishra* or *Essar Steel* be cited as if they decided criminal liability or PMLA attachment.

## 3. Conditions relating to change in management or control

**Proposition.** The change must be real and must pass control to someone outside the old promoter group and outside the investigation.

- **Statutory test.** Section 32A(1)(a) and (b), read with *Manish Kumar* para 252 (above).
- **BPSL as the origin of the dispute.** The NCLT approved JSW Steel's plan on 05.09.2019. ED passed a PAO on 10.10.2019. The NCLAT stayed it on 14.10.2019 and, by its final judgment of 17.02.2020, declared the attachment illegal and without jurisdiction in view of s.32A (*JSW Steel v. Mahender Kumar Khandelwal*, 2020 SCC OnLine NCLAT 431). These dates are recited in *Bhushan Power & Steel v. UoI*, Delhi HC 30.01.2025, paras 2.5-2.15 *(corpus: DLHC010218602024)*, and *Rajiv Chakraborty* para 27 cites the NCLAT decision. ED's appeal against it (CA 3362 of 2020) ended in the consent handover of 11.12.2024, without any ruling on s.32A.
  - **Corpus and compendium disagree here.** The compendium describes the 2020 NCLAT decision as an interim order. The corpus recital shows it was the NCLAT's final judgment, with the interim stay being the order of 14.10.2019.

**Use.** Do not cite the NCLAT 2020 judgment as settled. ED's challenge to it was never decided on merits. Before conceding any s.32A plea, check each limb of s.32A(1)(a)-(b) against the SRA's shareholding, directors, financiers and related parties (see section 9).

## 4. Immunity of the corporate debtor from prosecution for pre-CIRP offences

**Proposition.** Once the conditions are met, the company cannot be prosecuted for pre-CIRP offences from the date of approval, and a prosecution instituted during CIRP stands discharged (s.32A(1) and first proviso).

**Authorities.**
- ***Bhushan Power & Steel Ltd. v. Union of India***, Delhi HC (Manmeet Pritam Singh Arora J.), W.P.(CRL) 1261/2024, 30.01.2025 *(corpus: DLHC010218602024)*. Status: good law, but expressly subject to the Supreme Court appeals on the plan (para 7.2), since decided in favour of the plan in *Kalyani Transco*. Not in the compendium.
  - ED's counsel accepted that, under s.32A(1), the company could not be prosecuted after plan approval if the conditions were met. ED added that the company's role in its "earlier avatar" would still have to be examined in the trial of the former promoters (para 4.1).
  - The Court set aside the cognizance order and the proceedings in the PMLA complaint **only as against the company** (paras 7-7.1).
  - It clarified that the company's role, as described in the prosecution complaint, "will necessarily have to be examined" in the trial of the former promoters and directors, particularly where s.70 PMLA is invoked (para 6.3).
- ***Gangakhed Sugar and Energy Ltd. v. CBI***, Delhi HC (Chandra Dhari Singh J.), CRL.M.C. 2881/2023, 23.12.2024 *(corpus: DLHC010148402023)*. Status: good law. A CBI case, not PMLA; not in the compendium.
  - Para 65 sets out four points the court checks before applying s.32A. The offence must predate CIRP. The plan must be approved. New management must have taken control. The resolution applicant must not be, or be related to, the accused.
  - The FIR was quashed against the company only (para 72). Proceedings against former directors and guarantors continue (para 73). The company must assist the investigation under s.32A(3) (para 74).
  - The Court relied on the CBI not having alleged any link between the resolution applicant and the accused (para 66). This is a warning: if the agency does not raise the connection point, the court will not look for it.
- ***Tata Steel BSL Ltd. v. Union of India***, Delhi HC, W.P.(Crl.) 3087/2019, 2020 [date ?], 2020 SCC OnLine Del 1985 [?] (per compendium). The SFIO complaint against the company was set aside under s.32A after Tata Steel acquired 72.65% under an approved plan. The prosecution of former promoters and officers was preserved.
- ***Piramal Capital and Housing Finance Ltd. (formerly DHFL) v. ED***, Special Court (PMLA), Mumbai, 02.02.2026 (per compendium; the exact nature of the order, discharge or closure, is marked [?]). The Special Court applied s.32A to close the PMLA case against the company. It relied on the plan's approval by the NCLT (07.06.2021), the Bombay High Court's discharge of the company in the connected CBI case (16.11.2021), and the Supreme Court's dismissal of the former promoters' challenge to the plan (01.04.2025). It kept the case against individuals alive. Status: trial-court order; persuasive only.

**Use.** Where the conditions are genuinely met, ED can itself concede the company's discharge, as it did in *Bhushan Power & Steel v. UoI*. Always ask for the clarification recorded at para 6.3 of that order. Without it, the trial of the individuals may be argued to have lost the company's conduct as a foundation.

## 5. Protection of property and the IBC-PMLA interaction

**Proposition.** Section 32A(2) bars attachment, seizure, retention or confiscation of the corporate debtor's property covered by an approved plan (or sold in a qualifying liquidation sale), for a pre-CIRP offence, on the same conditions.

**Authorities.**
- ***Rajiv Chakraborty, RP of Era Infra Engineering v. ED***, Delhi HC (Yashwant Varma J.), W.P.(C) 9531/2020, 11.11.2022 *(corpus: DLHC010046592020)*. Status: good law.
  - The s.14 moratorium does not prevent ED acting under ss.5 and 8 during CIRP (paras 78-80, 93).
  - Section 32A is the "later" expression of legislative intent and fixes how far the PMLA gives way to the IBC (para 108). Once its trigger events occur, it erects "an impregnable wall" against PMLA action; until then, s.238 cannot be read more widely than s.32A (para 109).
  - The legislature chose to bar attachment or confiscation "only when a Resolution Plan had been approved or where a measure towards liquidation had been adopted" (para 105).
  - The RP may approach the competent PMLA authorities for relief over tainted property (para 113). **The writ petition was dismissed** (para 114), with liberty to seek release "in accordance with law" (para 115).
  - A caution for ED: following *Axis Bank* paras 149-165, a PAO gives ED no "superior or overriding right in property", and priority is settled independently (paras 112-113, 116).
  - The judgment contains no ruling on the personal liability of promoters; for that, cite *Manish Kumar* and *Nitin Jain* para 101(Q).
  - **Corpus and compendium disagree here.** The compendium says the petition "succeeded" to the extent of confirming s.32A. The judgment dismisses it; the s.32A passages explain when the bar begins.
- ***Kalyani Transco v. Bhushan Power and Steel Ltd.***, SC (B.R. Gavai CJ, Satish Chandra Sharma and K. Vinod Chandran JJ.), 26.09.2025, 2025 INSC 1165 (per compendium; not in corpus). Status: binding; it supersedes the recalled judgment of 02.05.2025 (2025 INSC 621).
  - It sets out the object and effect of s.32A at paras 116-117 (per compendium).
  - It treats ED's subsisting attachment and continued prosecution of the company as reasons why the CoC could not hand over unencumbered assets, so the delay was not the resolution applicant's fault (paras 118-124, per compendium).
  - It records at para 2.22 the reservation in the 11.12.2024 order (per compendium).
- ***Anil Kohli, RP of Dunar Foods v. ED***, NCLAT, 03.07.2025 [?] (per compendium). Section 32A is conditional and takes effect only on approval of a compliant plan, not on admission or by anticipation.

**Compendium view (not a holding).** The compendium reads *Kalyani Transco* as also holding that the NCLT cannot decide the validity of an ED attachment. Its own compilers' note in Chapter 12 says the judgment does not decide that question. Treat *Kalyani Transco* as authority on the object of s.32A and on delay, not on jurisdiction.

**Use.** Expect SRAs to cite *Kalyani Transco* paras 118-124 to excuse delay whenever an attachment subsists. Answer that those paragraphs turn on BPSL's history of stays.

## 6. Does the attachment cease automatically, or is an order needed?

**The adverse line.**
- ***Vantage Point Asset Management Pte. Ltd. v. Gaurav Misra, RP of Alchemist Infra Realty Ltd.***, NCLAT Principal Bench (Ashok Bhushan, Chairperson; Barun Mitra, Member (T)), Comp. App. (AT) (Ins) 1495 of 2024, 14.10.2025, 2025 SCC OnLine NCLAT 1652 (per compendium; one chapter reads 1655). **Status: under appeal**, SC Civil Appeal 1567 of 2026, admitted 06.02.2026.
  - **Facts (per compendium).** The PAO came on 24.01.2019. The Delhi High Court stayed PMLA proceedings on 13.02.2019. CIRP was admitted on 23.03.2022. The NCLT approved the plan on 04.07.2024 but sent the SRA to the PMLA authorities.
  - **Held (per compendium).** Section 32A operates on approval of a plan effecting change of control. A pre-CIRP provisional attachment ceases from that date, and the SRA need not go to the PMLA forum.
  - **Special facts.** The NCLAT noted that s.8 proceedings had not begun, that depositors formed the CoC majority and that the plan refunded them in full. ED's objection to jurisdiction was not accepted on those facts.
  - **Vantage Point I** (13.08.2024) was recalled on 09.01.2025 because ED had not been heard, and has no precedential value.
  - **Interim terms in the Supreme Court.** The compendium reports that the Supreme Court allowed development to continue, barred alienation and allowed disbursement subject to ED verification. The detail is garbled [?].

**The opposing line.**
- *Rajiv Chakraborty* paras 113-115: relief over attached property is sought from the PMLA authorities "in accordance with law" *(corpus: DLHC010046592020)*.
- *Value Wise* para 16: "the adjudicatory mechanism created under the PMLA alone will have jurisdiction to deal with any challenge to it" *(corpus: EDPORTAL_18_2026-06-30)*.
- *Union of India (ED) v. Udaipur Entertainment World*, Rajasthan HC (Division Bench), D.B. Special Appeal Writ 685/2024, 28.03.2025, paras 18-19, 21, 24-27 *(corpus: RJHC020983432024)*. This was an interim order. It restored a stay of an NCLT order that had vacated a confirmed PMLA attachment. It found a serious question whether the NCLT had "inherent jurisdiction" to do so, noted that ED had not been impleaded, and held that *Manish Kumar* does not decide the IBC-PMLA interplay.
- The Supreme Court routes in BPSL, *V Hotels*, *Udaipur* and *Shiv Charan* all used s.8(8) PMLA rather than treating the attachment as having lapsed.

**Compendium view (not a holding).** A tribunal may *recognise* that an attachment has ceased by force of s.32A, but may not *set aside or recall* the attachment itself. No court has adopted this distinction.

**Use.**
- Never let *Vantage Point II* be cited as settled. Stress the pending appeal, the recall of Vantage Point I, its special facts, and the conflict with the PMLA-forum line.
- Seek impleadment in every NCLT/NCLAT proceeding touching attached property.

## 7. Stage at which s.32A operates

- **Not on admission or during CIRP.** *Manish Kumar* para 252; *Value Wise* para 15 (s.32A "operates only where there is a successful insolvency resolution process"); *Anil Kohli* (per compendium).
- **Resolution: from approval under s.31.** This is the "defining moment". That phrase comes from *Nitin Jain* para 96 and was adopted in *Rajiv Chakraborty* paras 26 and 105.
- **Liquidation: from approval of the sale or liquidation measure.** ***Nitin Jain, Liquidator of PSL Ltd. v. ED***, Delhi HC (Yashwant Varma J.), W.P.(C) 3261/2021, 15.12.2021, 2021 SCC OnLine Del 5281 *(corpus: DLHC010126002021)*.
  - The Court held that reconciliation of the two statutes must be answered "solely on the anvil of Section 32A" (para 101(H)).
  - The power to attach under s.5 PMLA ceases once a measure under Regulation 32 of the Liquidation Regulations is approved by the NCLT (paras 98, 101(R)-(S)).
  - On the facts, cessation took effect from the NCLT's approval of the sale as a going concern (para 101(T)). ED was restrained from further action against the liquidation estate (para 102).
  - The bar covers only the corporate debtor's property. It does not cover persons in charge or ED's rights against other property (para 98).
  - **Later history.** ED's appeal, LPA 512/2021, was disposed of on 27.02.2025 on ED's statement that it would transfer Rs. 274.60 crore of sale proceeds to the liquidator. The Division Bench expressly expressed no opinion on the questions of law *(corpus: DLHC010404622021)*.
  - **Corpus and compendium disagree here.** The compendium says the restraint in *Nitin Jain* rested on harmonious construction "not on s.32A". The judgment rests it expressly on s.32A(2) (paras 97-98 and 101(H)-(T)).
- **Liquidation alone does not trigger s.32A.** *Value Wise* para 15 *(corpus: EDPORTAL_18_2026-06-30)*.
- **Attachment after approval.** In BPSL the PAO followed NCLT approval. The Supreme Court treated that fact as "peculiar" in ordering handover under s.8(8), and decided nothing on s.32A (order of 11.12.2024, as quoted in DLHC010218602024).
- **Chronology disputes.** *V Hotels Ltd. v. ED*, Bombay HC (Division Bench), W.P.(L) 32216 of 2024, 28.11.2024 [date also read as 26.11.2024 ?] (per compendium). The Court examined document by document whether the PAO predated plan approval, including a bounced e-mail and signs that an earlier date had been inserted. The Supreme Court's later consent order did not adopt the High Court's reasoning.
- **Commencement.** Section 32A was inserted by Act 1 of 2020 with effect from 28.12.2019 (*Rajiv Chakraborty* para 104). ED's stand before the Supreme Court in BPSL was that it is not retrospective (order of 11.12.2024, para 4, as quoted in DLHC010218602024).

**Use.**
- Time PAOs before plan approval, and in liquidation before the NCLT approves the sale or measure.
- Record the issue and service of every PAO in a form that can be proved.
- Where an asset is attached after approval, rely on a pre-CIRP investigation and on the s.32A conditions not being met, not on the date alone.

## 8. Continuing liability of promoters, directors, officers and related persons

**Proposition.** Section 32A(1), second proviso, keeps designated partners, officers in default, persons in charge of or responsible for the business, and anyone "associated with the corporate debtor in any manner" and directly or indirectly involved in the offence, liable to prosecution. Section 32A(2), Explanation (ii), keeps their property attachable.

**Authorities.**
- *Manish Kumar* paras 253-254 and 257 *(corpus: 2021_INSC_28)*.
- *Nitin Jain* para 101(Q): the cessation is limited to the corporate debtor *(corpus: DLHC010126002021)*.
- *Bhushan Power & Steel v. UoI* paras 6.2-6.3 *(corpus: DLHC010218602024)*.
- *Gangakhed Sugar* para 73 *(corpus: DLHC010148402023)*.
- ***Shiv Charan v. Adjudicating Authority (PMLA)***, Bombay HC 01.03.2024; on appeal, ***Deputy Director, ED v. Shiv Charan***, SC, Civil Appeal Nos. 9692-9693 of 2024, 16.07.2026 [?] (per compendium). **Consent order; non-precedential.**
  - Bank balances and fourteen residential units were restored to the SRAs under the second proviso to s.8(8).
  - The company was deleted from the prosecution under s.32A. The prosecution of former directors, persons in control, conspirators and abettors continues.
  - The benefit depends on the SRAs being unconnected and not beneficiaries of the proceeds, with liberty to ED.
  - The Supreme Court declined to decide whether s.32A by itself nullifies a pre-CIRP attachment, and neither affirmed nor reversed the High Court.
- *V Hotels* (SC) and *Udaipur* (SC): same structure (section 10).

**Use.** In every consent or s.32A disposal, insist on three things: the express preservation of the prosecution of individuals; the continued attachment of their personal assets; and the clarification that the company's past conduct remains part of the trial.

## 9. Re-attachment, failure of the s.32A conditions and sham change of control (Chapter 10)

### 9.1 Framing

**Compendium view (not a holding).**
- If the conditions were never satisfied, no immunity ever arose, so a later attachment does not "withdraw" anything.
- No general power to withdraw a validly acquired immunity is recognised.
- Much of this area is argued by analogy from s.29A and substance-over-form cases, because direct s.32A precedent is thin.

The statutory text supports the first point. *Manish Kumar* para 252 says that where the abetment or conspiracy condition is met, the debtor's liability "will remain unaffected".

**Open point.** Section 32A(1)(b) speaks of a person against whom the investigating authority "has submitted or filed a report or a complaint". The cases do not say whether the report must exist when the plan is approved. Where ED has material against a resolution applicant before approval, file the report or complaint, or at least place the material before the NCLT, before approval.

### 9.2 Testing genuineness: the see-through method (analogy)

- ***ArcelorMittal India Pvt. Ltd. v. Satish Kumar Gupta***, SC, 04.10.2018, [2018] 12 SCR 362 = (2019) 2 SCC 1 *(corpus: 2018_INSC_935)*. Status: good law, but a s.29A case. Section 29A looks at de facto control, and eligibility is tested on the date the plan is submitted (per compendium). Section 32A operates on and after approval, so the dates differ.
- ***Phoenix ARC Pvt. Ltd. v. Spade Financial Services Ltd.***, SC, 01.02.2021, (2021) 3 SCC 475 (per compendium). Collusive transactions are not financial debt, and related-party status is judged at the time of the transaction, so a party cannot escape by an engineered exit. This is a method only, not a s.32A holding.

**Use.** Map the SRA's shareholding, directors, funding chain, family members, advisers and former employees. Trace whether its acquisition money comes from the former promoters or from the proceeds under investigation.

### 9.3 Limits once a plan is implemented

- ***Bank of Baroda v. MBL Infrastructures Ltd.***, SC (Kaul and Sundresh JJ.), 18.01.2022, 2022 SCC OnLine SC 48 (per compendium). The Court found a technical breach of s.29A(h) but declined to unwind a plan that had been substantially implemented. Status: good law; **adverse** on timing. Compendium view (not a holding): the same leniency should not automatically extend to s.32A, because s.32A extinguishes criminal liability.
- ***ED v. V Hotels Ltd.***, SC, Criminal Appeal 2925 of 2025, 11.11.2025 (per compendium). **Consent order; non-precedential.**
  - The setting aside of the PAO was confirmed by consent, and restoration to the SRA was ordered under the second proviso to s.8(8). The amounts deposited under interim directions of 02.07.2025 were released with interest.
  - The company was dropped from the prosecution; individuals were not.
  - ED was given liberty to act, including by questioning the plan, if the SRA's independence or non-benefit fails.
  - **The compendium is inconsistent on this order.** Its case note says the order was made by consent without going into merits. Its appendix says the Court held that ED had no lien because the plan had been substantially implemented. Verify against the order before citing either reading.

**Use.** Object to a sham change of control at the earliest stage: before CoC approval if possible, before NCLT approval at the latest, and in any event before implementation is substantial.

### 9.4 Bona fide third parties who acted on an approved plan

- ***Udaipur Entertainment World Pvt. Ltd. v. Union of India***, SC, 10.10.2025 (Civil Appeals arising from SLP(C) 10734/2025 and 26154-26155/2025 [?]) (per compendium). **Consent order; non-precedential; all questions of law open.**
  - On ED's affidavit, the attachment was lifted in part and property restored to the SRA solely for genuine homebuyers, under the second proviso to s.8(8).
  - Eleven identified units stayed attached.
  - The company was deleted from the prosecution under s.32A, while individuals remain prosecuted, with liberty to ED.
  - The compendium reads it as protecting innocent buyers through restitution, not as a ruling on re-attachment.
- The earlier Rajasthan High Court interim order in the same matter (*corpus: RJHC020983432024*, para 26) gave the opposite risk equal weight. If the attachment were lifted and the units passed to buyers, confiscation after trial could be frustrated.

### 9.5 Forum for a re-attachment dispute

A challenge to a re-attachment, like any attachment, goes to the PMLA Adjudicating Authority, the Appellate Tribunal and the High Court under s.42 PMLA, not to the NCLT/NCLAT.
- *Value Wise* para 16 *(corpus: EDPORTAL_18_2026-06-30)*.
- *Embassy Property Developments v. State of Karnataka*, SC, (2020) 13 SCC 308 (citation as given in *Value Wise* para 14).
- The compendium cites *Kalyani Transco* here, but see the caution in section 5.

**Drafting model for ED's consent terms.** Include each of the following:
- restitution under the second proviso to s.8(8), not under s.32A;
- specified property to remain attached where needed;
- an express preservation of the prosecution of individuals and of attachment of their assets;
- a recital that the company's conduct remains in issue in their trial;
- liberty to ED if the SRA is connected to former management or benefits from the proceeds;
- a recital that the order is without prejudice and not a precedent.

## Open points and risks

1. **Automatic cessation** (*Vantage Point II*) is pending in the Supreme Court. Until it is decided, NCLT benches may follow the NCLAT.
2. ***Nitin Jain* is adverse and still stands.** The Division Bench left the law open. In liquidation, the bar on attachment may begin when the NCLT approves the sale or measure. The compendium's statement that *Nitin Jain* did not rest on s.32A is not borne out by the judgment.
3. **The NCLAT's 2020 BPSL judgment** declared a post-approval PAO illegal under s.32A. ED's appeal ended in a consent handover, so the ruling was never reviewed on merits. *Kalyani Transco* upheld that NCLAT judgment as regards the plan. Expect SRAs to say the Supreme Court thereby approved the whole of it. Answer with para 2.22 of *Kalyani Transco* and the express reservation in the 11.12.2024 order.
4. **Timing of the s.32A(1)(b) report.** It is unclear whether a report or complaint against the SRA filed after approval revives liability. No authority found.
5. **Kalyani Transco and delay.** Paras 118-124 may be used to excuse SRA delay whenever an attachment subsists.
6. **Inconsistencies inside the compendium**: *Kalyani Transco* on jurisdiction; *V Hotels* SC (consent without merits, or no lien because of substantial implementation); the description of the 2020 BPSL NCLAT decision as interim. Read the orders before relying on the compendium's summaries.
7. **Compendium views that are not holdings**: the "recognition vs setting aside" distinction; the "never arose vs withdrawal" framing; the non-transfer of *MBL* leniency to s.32A; the procedural-only reading of the IBBI circular.

## After the 2026 Amendment

The IBC (Amendment) Act, 2026 (Act 6 of 2026) brought most provisions into force on 26.05.2026 (S.O. 2625(E), 22.05.2026).
- **Section 32A is unamended.** Everything above about its conditions and limits stands.
- **Section 31(5)-(6) and Explanations I-III codify the clean slate.** Pre-approval claims "against the corporate debtor and its assets" are extinguished. Explanation I preserves claims against promoters, guarantors and co-obligors.
  - **Open question:** is a PMLA attachment a "claim"? ED's position is that it is not. Attachment creates no debt and moves no title; it is a restraint pending adjudication (*Rajiv Chakraborty* paras 67-69, 80, 94-101; *Axis Bank*, Delhi HC 02.04.2019, para 141 *(corpus: DLHC012396152018)*). Section 32A is the specific provision on criminal liability and property.
  - Expect SRAs to plead s.31(6) where s.32A's conditions are doubtful. Keep s.31 (civil claims) and s.32A (criminal liability and property) analytically separate.
- **Old s.33(5) is omitted.** The liquidation moratorium is now in s.33(1)(b), which imports s.14(1)(a) and (c). *Nitin Jain* and *Value Wise* were decided on the old text. The s.32A(2) bar on property sold in liquidation is unchanged.
- **Section 12A is substituted.** Withdrawal is allowed only after the CoC is constituted and before resolution plans are invited, with 90% CoC approval. A settlement is not a resolution plan approved under s.31, so on the text s.32A does not apply to it (LawBase analysis; no authority found).
- **Not yet notified:** creditor-initiated insolvency (Chapter IV-A, ss.58A-58K), group insolvency (s.59A) and cross-border insolvency (ss.240B-240C). How s.32A would apply under them is untested.
