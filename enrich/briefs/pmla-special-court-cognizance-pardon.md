## Q1. Is the PMLA Special Court a "hybrid" of Magistrate and Sessions Court?

### Comparison: the PC Act Special Judge

- **Cognizance and trial.** Section 5(1): the Special Judge takes cognizance without committal and tries by the Magistrates' warrant-case procedure.
- **Deemed status.** Section 5(3): deemed a Court of Session. Section 5(4): deemed a Magistrate for ss.326 and 475.
- **How the Supreme Court describes it.**
  - *Krishnaswami Naidu*, [1979] 3 SCR 928: "for some purposes … a Sessions Judge and for some other purposes … a Magistrate".
  - *A.R. Antulay v. R.S. Nayak*, [1984] 2 SCR 914: a court of original criminal jurisdiction.
  - *Bangaru Laxman*, [2011] 13 SCR 268: "dual power".

### The PMLA position

The PMLA Special Court is a designated Court of Session. It is magisterial at the front end but conducts a Sessions trial, so it is hybrid only in a narrower sense than the PC Act Special Judge.

**1. What it is.**
- **Section 43(1).** Courts of Session are designated as Special Courts.
- **The committal bar.** Ordinarily s.193 CrPC stops a Court of Session taking cognizance without committal. *Gangula Ashok*, [2000] 1 SCR 468 shows that bar applies to a Special Court absent an express provision.

**2. Cognizance without committal.**
- **Section 44(1)(b)** is that express provision. Cognizance is taken "upon a complaint made by an authority authorised … **without the accused being committed to it for trial**".
- **Complaint is the only route.** The police-report route was omitted by Act 20 of 2005.
- *V. Arul Kumar*, [2016] 2 SCR 486: such a clause "waiv[es] the otherwise mandatory requirement of Section 193".

**3. Magisterial functions at the front end.**
- **Complaint procedure.** *Tarsem Lal v. ED*, [2024] 6 SCR 864:
  - A complaint is governed by ss.200–205 CrPC.
  - As a rule, summons issue rather than a warrant.
  - An accused appearing on summons is not in custody, and s.45 does not apply.
  - After cognizance, ED "is powerless to exercise power under Section 19" and must seek custody from the Special Court [para 21].
- **Remand before cognizance.**
  - Section 19(3), as amended by Act 13 of 2018: production before a "Special Court or Judicial Magistrate or a Metropolitan Magistrate".
  - *V. Senthil Balaji*, [2023] 12 SCR 853: the remanding court's s.167 role includes checking s.19 compliance.
- **The statute treats it as a Magistrate for one purpose.** Section 44(2) reads "Magistrate" in s.439 to include the Special Court.

**4. Trial as a Court of Session.**
- **Section 44(1)(d).** Trial follows the procedure "as it applies to a trial before a Court of Session".
- **Section 46(1).** The Special Court is deemed a Court of Session. This is the key difference from PC Act s.5(1).
- **Section 47.** Appeals and revisions lie as if from a Court of Session.

**5. It also tries the predicate offence.**
- **Sections 44(1)(a) and (c).** The Special Court tries the scheduled offence too, and another court that has taken cognizance of it must commit that case on ED's application.
- *Rana Ayyub v. ED*, [2023] 3 SCR 892 so holds. *Vijay Madanlal Choudhary*, [2022] 6 SCR 382 upheld s.44.

## Q3. Can a CJM or a Judicial Magistrate First Class (JMFC) tender pardon in a PMLA case before the Special Court takes cognizance?

- **Where the power comes from.**
  - PMLA has **no** equivalent of PC Act s.5(2). The power comes only from ss.306–308 CrPC (BNSS ss.343–345), applied through PMLA ss.46(1) and 65.
  - Section 306 applies on both limbs: (a) exclusive triability, where BNSS now says "Special Judge appointed under any other law"; and (b) punishment, since PMLA s.4 prescribes 3 to 7 years, or 10.
- **JMFC: never.** A JMFC may pardon only while "inquiring into or trying the offence" (*Abu Salem*). A PMLA offence is triable and cognizable only by the Special Court under s.44(1)(a)–(b), notwithstanding the CrPC.
- **CJM before cognizance: undecided for PMLA.**
  - **Against (the *Harshad Mehta* line):** exclusive jurisdiction "notwithstanding the Code"; cognizance only on a complaint to the Special Court; Explanation (i) to s.44(1) speaks of the Special Court's jurisdiction "during investigation, enquiry or trial"; s.71 overriding effect; and the s.306(4)–(5) committal machinery can never operate.
  - **For (the *P.C. Mishra*, *Arul Kumar* and *Srinivasulu* line):** s.19(3) keeps Magistrates in the pre-cognizance phase, and s.65 applies the CrPC to investigation.
- **CJM after cognizance: no power.** On the *Deivendran* reasoning, a pardon then would be without jurisdiction.
- **Reported practice.** Every PMLA pardon found was granted by the **Special Court** (*Maiank Mehta*, *Rajiv Saxena*, *Tajinder Pal Singh*). No CJM pardon in a PMLA case was found.
- **Safe course.** Apply to the Special Court, and only with the prosecution joining (*Pascal Fernandes*). The court does not examine the agency's subjective satisfaction (*Tajinder Pal Singh*).
- **A predicate pardon does not settle the PMLA case, and the High Courts are split.** *Mohan Lal Rathi*, *Sanjay Kansal* and *Vem Krishna Keerthan* hold that PMLA proceedings continue; *Kankani* upheld a discharge. Where the witness is needed, tender pardon separately in the PMLA case.
- **Accomplice as witness without pardon.** This is lawful (*Girish Sharma*; *Laxmipat Choraria*), but the CG High Court has criticised it at the bail stage (*Arun Pati Tripathi*, para 30).
