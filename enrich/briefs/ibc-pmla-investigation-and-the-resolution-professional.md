## Short answer

- **CIRP does not stop the investigation.** Section 14 IBC stays recovery and enforcement against the corporate debtor and its assets. It does not stay recording an ECIR, search, s.50 summons and statements, arrest, a prosecution complaint, cognizance or trial. The tests are:
  - the substance of the proceeding, not its label (*P. Mohanraj*, paras 28, 43);
  - whether it can add to the debtor's debt-liability (*Value Wise*, NCLAT 30.06.2026, para 12).
- **The ECIR is an internal ED document** and need not be supplied to the corporate debtor or its RP (*Vijay Madanlal Choudhary*, conclusion (xviii)). The IBC does not displace the PMLA's own machinery.
- **Individuals get no shield from CIRP.** The moratorium protects only the corporate debtor (*P. Mohanraj*, para 77). Directors and signatories remain prosecutable even after dissolution or plan approval (*Ajay Kumar Radheyshyam Goenka*, SC 2023). Section 32A never protects individuals (*Manish Kumar*; *Nitin Jain*, para 101(Q)).
- **Arrest before cognizance.** Section 19 is available during CIRP on its usual conditions. After the Special Court takes cognizance of the complaint, ED cannot arrest the accused named in it. Custody can then be sought only from the Special Court, at most under s.309(2) CrPC (*Tarsem Lal*, para 21(d), (f), (i)-(j)).
- **Prosecute the company during CIRP if needed.** The first proviso to s.32A(1) assumes such prosecutions exist and discharges the corporate debtor only from approval of a compliant plan. "Prosecution" there means criminal proceedings properly so called, begun on an FIR or an investigating agency's complaint (*P. Mohanraj*, paras 33-34). After a qualifying plan, drop only the corporate debtor.
- **Serve the RP, not the old board.** Once CIRP starts, the IRP/RP represents the corporate debtor in PMLA proceedings (ss.17, 18, 23 and 25(2)(b) IBC). The suspended directors answer for themselves.
- **The RP must cooperate.** In liquidation the liquidator holds the records and must give the PMLA authorities what they ask for (*Nitin Jain*, para 100). The RP should take part in confirmation proceedings, which the moratorium does not halt (*Dr. Jain Video on Wheels*).
- **The RP's challenge lies in the PMLA forums, not the NCLT.** An RP who wants an attachment lifted must go to the PMLA authorities (*Rajiv Chakraborty*, paras 113 and 115), or to the Special Court under s.8(7)/8(8) under the IBBI Circular of 04.11.2025 (*Value Wise*, para 14). The NCLT cannot review ED's action (*Embassy Property*; *Dreamz Infra*; *Value Wise*, para 16).
- **Record fresh, accurate reasons when acting long after insolvency.** *Nita Puri* (Delhi HC, 2025) quashed an SFIO direction over a company in liquidation. The order read audit reports as finding transactions they did not find, ignored judgments discrediting those reports, and could not be cured by reasons added later in an affidavit.

## Part A. Investigation, search, summons, arrest and prosecution during CIRP (Chapter 3)

### 3.1 Registering an ECIR before or during CIRP

**Proposition.** An ECIR is an internal ED record, not a statutory FIR. It need not be supplied to the accused, and disclosing the grounds at arrest is enough. The PMLA is a self-contained penal statute with its own forums, which the IBC does not displace.

**Authority.** ***Vijay Madanlal Choudhary v. Union of India***, SC (A.M. Khanwilkar, Dinesh Maheshwari, C.T. Ravikumar, JJ.), 27.07.2022, [2022] 6 SCR 382 : 2022 INSC 757 : (2023) 12 SCC 1 *(corpus: 2022_INSC_757)*.
- **ECIR (conclusion (xviii)(a)):** an ECIR "cannot be equated with an FIR"; it is "an internal document of the ED".
- **Other holdings (per compendium):** ED officers are not police officers for s.25 of the Evidence Act. Section 50 statements may be used, subject to safeguards. Sections 24 and 45 are valid.
- **Limits:** it does not consider the IBC. Some aspects are reportedly under review; the core holdings govern.
- **Stance:** pro-ED.

**ED use.** An RP has no right to a copy of the ECIR merely because the company is in CIRP. Record in the file when the ECIR was registered relative to the CIRP commencement date. That date matters for the look-through argument on attachment (*Dunar Foods*; see the companion brief) and for s.32A.

### 3.2 Investigation and attachment continue despite the moratorium

**Proposition.** Criminal investigation and enforcement are not the civil recovery that s.14 suspends. *P. Mohanraj* brings s.138 NI Act proceedings against the corporate debtor within the moratorium only because they are compensatory in substance. It does not cover natural persons, and on the Delhi High Court's reading it does not reach the PMLA. Pre-CIRP attachments survive (*Varrsana Ispat*, *Rotomac*, *Kiran Shah*); *Manoj Kumar Agarwal* is disapproved.

**Authorities.**
- ***P. Mohanraj v. Shah Brothers Ispat Pvt. Ltd.***, SC (R.F. Nariman, Navin Sinha, K.M. Joseph, JJ.), 01.03.2021, [2021] 14 SCR 204 : (2021) 6 SCC 258 *(corpus: 2021_INSC_133)*. Paragraph numbers follow the SCR; SCR para 76 is cited as para 100 in SCC OnLine.
  - **Para 28:** counsel conceded, and the Court recorded, that criminal proceedings not directly related to transactions evidencing the debtor's debt fall outside s.14(1)(a).
  - **Paras 33-34:** a moratorium "does not extinguish any liability, civil or criminal" and only suspends proceedings; s.32A is what extinguishes the corporate debtor's criminal liability.
  - **Para 43:** a s.138 case is a civil remedy in criminal form.
  - **Para 76:** confiscation of proceeds of crime "is by the government acting statutorily and not as a creditor".
  - **Para 77:** the moratorium protects "only" the corporate debtor; the persons under s.141 stay liable during it.
  - **Para 78:** s.138/141 proceedings against the corporate debtor are covered by s.14(1)(a).
  - **Status:** binding. The NCLAT in *Value Wise* (para 13) records that the Supreme Court has doubted it in *Dineshchand Surana v. UCO Bank* and referred it to a larger Bench *(as quoted; not read)*. **Mixed;** the RP's usual opening citation.
  - **The compendium says it left natural persons open.** Para 77 decides the point.
- ***Ajay Kumar Radheyshyam Goenka v. Tourism Finance Corporation of India Ltd.***, SC (3J), 15.03.2023, (2023) 10 SCC 545 [?]; also cited as 2023 SCC OnLine SC 266 *(as cited in a Delhi HC judgment, corpus DLHC010148402023)*.
  - **Held (per compendium):** s.14 does not hold s.138 criminal proceedings in abeyance as against the natural persons. Their prosecution continues during the moratorium and survives the corporate debtor's dissolution or plan approval. A plan cannot extinguish their personal liability.
  - **Status:** binding. **Pro-ED.** The SC judgment itself is not in LawBase.
- ***Rajiv Chakraborty, RP of EIEL v. ED***, Delhi HC (Yashwant Varma, J.), 11.11.2022, W.P.(C) 9531/2020 *(corpus: DLHC010046592020)*.
  - **Held:** ED is not a creditor (paras 67-69). *P. Mohanraj* does not apply, because it rests on debt recovery (paras 79-80). "it would be incorrect to read Section 14 as completely shutting out actions under Sections 5 and 8 of the PMLA" (para 83). The NCLAT line in *Varrsana*, *Rotomac* and *Kiran Shah* is correct (paras 86-93).
  - **Result:** the writ was dismissed (para 114).
  - **Status:** good law. **Pro-ED.**
- ***Varrsana Ispat*** (NCLAT, 2019 SCC OnLine NCLAT 236), ***Rotomac Global*** (NCLAT, 2019 SCC OnLine NCLAT 961), ***Kiran Shah*** (NCLAT LB, 2022 SCC OnLine NCLAT 2) and ***Manoj Kumar Agarwal*** (NCLAT, 2021 SCC OnLine NCLAT 121, disapproved). Citations are as given in *Rajiv Chakraborty*'s footnotes; for details see the companion brief.

**ED use.** When an RP opens with *P. Mohanraj*, answer from the judgment itself: paras 28, 43, 76 and 77, then *Rajiv Chakraborty* paras 79-83. Keep investigation steps clearly separate from any step that recovers money from the estate.

### 3.3 Search of the corporate debtor's premises and the validity of the underlying investigation

**Proposition.** Section 14 places no bar on search, summons, investigation or inquiry. The compendium notes one possible exception by analogy: a fresh investigative direction issued years after insolvency or liquidation ended, contrary to an earlier finding of no fraud, may be quashed without genuinely fresh material.

**Authority.** ***Nita Puri v. Union of India***, Delhi HC (Sachin Datta, J.), 28.08.2025, W.P.(C) 261/2025 *(corpus: DLHC010003032025)*. The neutral citation 2025:DHC:7433 [?] is per compendium and not verified.
- **Facts:** a former director of Moser Baer India Ltd., which was then in liquidation, challenged the MCA's order of 05.09.2024 under s.212(1)(c) of the Companies Act directing an SFIO investigation.
- **Wrong premise (paras 35-41):** the order said forensic audits had "noticed" preferential, undervalued, fraudulent or extortionate (PUFE) transactions. Neither audit found any.
- **Material ignored (paras 52-63):** parts of the order were copied from an audit report without its disclaimers. Judgments discrediting a later audit were not considered.
- **No supplementing by affidavit (paras 64-65):** reasons added in the counter-affidavit could not save the order (*Mohinder Singh Gill*; *Opto Circuit*).
- **Result (paras 70-72):** the order and all consequential proceedings were quashed.
- **The compendium's framing differs.** It presents the case as a stale investigation reopened after insolvency without fresh material. The judgment does not turn on delay; it turns on a wrong factual premise and failure to consider relevant material.
- **Status:** Companies Act case; relevant by analogy only. Check for an LPA. **Adverse** by analogy.

**ED use.** Before acting on IBC-stage forensic or transaction audits, read what they actually find and note any orders that have commented on them. Record the reasons for an ECIR, summons, search or PAO in the order itself, not in a later affidavit.

### 3.4 Summons and statements under s.50 PMLA

**Proposition.** CIRP does not stop ED issuing s.50 summons, calling for documents or recording statements. A summons is investigative: it neither recovers from the corporate debtor nor alienates its assets. What s.14 may restrict is coercive recovery or enforcement against the debtor or its assets.

**Authorities.**
- ***Vijay Madanlal Choudhary*** (3.1): governs the character of s.50 statements.
- ***Rajiv Chakraborty*** (3.2).
- ***Sundaresh Bhatt, Liquidator of ABG Shipyard v. Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs***, SC, 26.08.2022, (2023) 1 SCC 472; Civil Appeal 7667/2021.
  - **Held:** during the moratorium customs could assess and quantify duty, but could not recover it by selling or retaining the goods. The customs claim ranks under s.53.
  - **How the compendium uses it:** by analogy, a moratorium against recovery does not stop inquiry or fact-finding.
  - **Caution:** a customs claim is a creditor's claim against the debtor, whereas PMLA attachment is not (*Axis Bank* para 141; *P. Mohanraj* para 76). Distinguish it if an RP cites it against attachment.
  - **Status:** binding; not in LawBase. **Neutral.**
- ***Value Wise Consultancy Pvt. Ltd. v. Deputy Director, ED***, NCLAT Principal Bench (N. Seshasayee, J.; Arun Baroka and Indevar Pandey, Members (T)), 30.06.2026, Company Appeal (AT) (Ins) 1226 and 1227/2022 *(corpus: EDPORTAL_18_2026-06-30)*.
  - **Facts:** ED had issued s.50 notices telling the corporate debtor's customers not to release payments.
  - **Held (para 16):** any challenge to ED's action, "including any notice issued under Sec.50", lies only in the PMLA adjudicatory mechanism, not before the IBC tribunals.
  - **Status:** NCLAT. **Pro-ED.**

**ED use.** Keep summoning and recording statements during CIRP. Summon the RP for the company's records and the former officers in their own right. Do not attach recovery conditions to a summons; that is where s.14 arguments gain ground.

### 3.5 Arrest of promoters, directors, KMPs and others during CIRP

**Proposition.** CIRP gives no personal immunity from arrest; s.14 protects only the corporate debtor. Promoters' personal assets do not enter the moratorium merely because CIRP has begun. Section 32A does not free former promoters, directors, persons in control or abettors. Every arrest must still meet s.19 and the constitutional safeguards. After cognizance, ED cannot arrest the accused named in the complaint.

**Authorities.**
- ***Tarsem Lal v. Directorate of Enforcement, Jalandhar Zonal Office***, SC (Abhay S. Oka and Ujjal Bhuyan, JJ.), 16.05.2024, [2024] 6 SCR 864 : 2024 INSC 434 *(corpus: 2024_INSC_434)*. Conclusions are at para 21.
  - **(d)** After cognizance on a complaint, the Special Court cannot remand under s.167(2) CrPC; the power is "at the highest" under s.309(2).
  - **(f)** If ED seeks such remand, the court must hear the accused and record brief reasons.
  - **(i)** After cognizance, ED and its officers "are powerless to exercise power under Section 19" to arrest a person shown as an accused in the complaint.
  - **(j)** ED may apply to the Special Court for custody for further investigation. It may still arrest under s.19 a person not named in the complaint.
  - **Status:** binding. **Adverse** to ED's freedom of action.
- ***Ajay Kumar Radheyshyam Goenka*** and ***P. Mohanraj*** (para 77) (3.2).
- ***Alpha & Omega Diagnostics (India) Ltd. v. Asset Reconstruction Company of India Ltd.***, NCLAT, 31.07.2017, Company Appeal (AT) (Ins) 116/2017. Assets that do not belong to the corporate debtor, including promoters' personal properties given as security, do not come under the moratorium. The compendium cites it by analogy; it is not an arrest case. **Status:** NCLAT; not in LawBase. **Pro-ED.**
- ***Manish Kumar v. Union of India***, SC, 19.01.2021, [2021] 14 SCR 895 : (2021) 5 SCC 1 *(corpus: 2021_INSC_28)*. Section 32A is valid; it immunises the resolved corporate debtor but not the persons responsible (per compendium). SCR para 257 corresponds to SCC OnLine para 280.
- ***Nitin Jain, Liquidator of PSL Ltd. v. ED***, Delhi HC, 15.12.2021, W.P.(C) 3261/2021 *(corpus: DLHC010126002021)*, para 101(Q). The cessation under s.32A is limited to the corporate debtor. The PMLA "do[es] not stand diluted" against persons who were in control of it.
- ***Udaipur Entertainment World Pvt. Ltd. v. Union of India*** (SC, 10.10.2025) and ***DoE v. V Hotels Ltd.*** (SC, 11.11.2025, Crl. A. 2925/2025). In both, by consent, the corporate debtor was dropped from the prosecution under s.32A and the prosecution of former directors, persons in control and abettors continued. Both are expressly non-precedential.
- **Compendium view (not a holding).** New, unconnected management that takes control under a qualifying plan should not be arrested or prosecuted for pre-CIRP offences merely because of the takeover. It must still cooperate and answer for its own conduct.

**ED use.** Decide on arrests of promoters, directors and KMPs before filing the prosecution complaint. After cognizance, seek custody only from the Special Court, with reasons. Answer any personal-shield argument with *P. Mohanraj* para 77, *Goenka* and *Nitin Jain* para 101(Q). Since the 2026 Amendment, the Explanation to s.14(3)(b) on sureties also confirms that guarantors' assets are outside the moratorium.

### 3.6 Prosecution complaint, cognizance and trial during CIRP

**Proposition.** The moratorium does not bar ED from filing a prosecution complaint during CIRP, the Special Court from taking cognizance, or the trial from continuing. The first proviso to s.32A(1) assumes a prosecution may be pending against the corporate debtor and discharges it only from approval of a qualifying plan. After that, only the corporate debtor is discharged; individuals remain prosecutable.

**Authorities.**
- ***P. Mohanraj*** paras 33-34 (3.2). "Prosecution" in the first proviso to s.32A(1) means "criminal proceedings properly so-called", begun by an FIR or by a complaint of an investigating authority. This confirms that a PMLA complaint is the kind of prosecution s.32A addresses, not s.14.
- ***Rajiv Chakraborty*** paras 105-109. Section 32A is the end point: after plan approval or adoption of a liquidation measure, PMLA powers against the corporate debtor and its property cease. Before that point they continue.
- ***Vantage Point Asset Management Pte. Ltd. v. Gaurav Misra, RP of Alchemist Infra Realty Ltd.***, NCLAT, 14.10.2025, Company Appeal (AT) (Ins) 1495/2024 (one entry reads 1498 [?]).
  - **How the compendium uses it here:** s.32A discharge depends on approval of a compliant plan and on the change-of-control test being met.
  - **Status:** **under appeal**, SC Civil Appeal 1567/2026, admitted 06.02.2026. **Adverse** on automatic release of attachments.
- **Practice illustrations (per compendium).**
  - Prosecution complaints were filed and cognizance taken during insolvency in the DHFL matter and during liquidation in the Shree Ganesh Jewellery House matter.
  - In the DHFL matter the Special Court (PMLA), Mumbai, on 02.02.2026 later closed the proceedings against the resolved company under s.32A, while expressly continuing against the former promoters. That is a trial-court order and persuasive only.

**ED use.** File when the evidence is ready; CIRP is no reason to wait. Name the corporate debtor where its liability is real, because s.32A discharge is conditional. When a qualifying plan is approved, apply to delete only the corporate debtor and keep the ECIR and the case against individuals and their properties alive. Check the resolution applicant's independence before conceding s.32A.

### 3.7 Criminal investigation and prosecution versus civil proceedings against the corporate debtor; the jurisdictional basis

**Proposition.** The test is the substance of the proceeding, not its label. Coercive recovery against the corporate debtor or its estate falls under the moratorium; genuinely criminal, penal or investigative action does not. Section 60(5) IBC does not let the NCLT judicially review public-law decisions of government or statutory authorities, which keeps PMLA action outside NCLT jurisdiction. Assets attached in an investigation that predates CIRP are not released to the estate.

**Authorities.**
- ***Embassy Property Developments Pvt. Ltd. v. State of Karnataka***, SC, 03.12.2019, (2020) 13 SCC 308.
  - **Held:** s.60(5)(c) does not make the NCLT a court of plenary jurisdiction. Public-law decisions under another statute are challenged by writ or before the special forum.
  - **Status:** binding. Not in LawBase as a Supreme Court record, but applied by that citation in *Value Wise* (para 14), *Rajiv Chakraborty* and *Nitin Jain*. **Pro-ED.**
- ***Value Wise*** (para 12). Sections 14 and 33(5) cover proceedings that may add to the debtor's existing debt. They do not cover penal public-law proceedings that cannot add to that debt, "even if they affect the net asset" available for CIRP or liquidation. Parliament did not make the IBC "a holy Ganges" to wash away PMLA criminality (paras 11-12).
- ***Anil Kohli, RP of Dunar Foods Ltd. v. ED***, NCLAT, 03.07.2025 [?], Company Appeal (AT) (Ins) 389/2018 [?]. The date of the investigation, not of the PAO, decided whether the asset was pre-CIRP tainted property; the release application was dismissed. **Status:** NCLAT; fact-specific. **Pro-ED.**
- ***P. Mohanraj*** (paras 28, 43): the substance test.

**ED use.** Frame every step as investigative or penal. Where the RP says a step is "recovery", show that it cannot add to the corporate debtor's debt (*Value Wise* para 12).

## Part B. The IRP/RP and PMLA-attached assets (Chapter 7)

The compendium gives RPs this practical guidance (compendium view, not a holding):
- disclose the attachment to the CoC and bidders at once;
- preserve and insure attached assets;
- treat ED as an authority to cooperate with, not an adversary;
- disclose prominently in the information memorandum;
- challenge only before the PMLA forum, never the NCLT/NCLAT.

### 7.1 Taking custody and control under ss.17, 18, 20 and 25 IBC

**Proposition.** The RP takes custody of the corporate debtor's assets, but a PMLA attachment continues over them. *Manoj Kumar Agarwal*'s view that attached property becomes available to the IBC process during CIRP is no longer law; it was disapproved by the NCLAT Larger Bench in *Kiran Shah*.

**Authorities.** *Manoj Kumar Agarwal* (disapproved; history only); *Kiran Shah* (controlling); *Varrsana Ispat*; *Rotomac*. *Rajiv Chakraborty* paras 94-101 add that attachment is a symbolic taking of custody for preservation and moves no title. The RP's possession and ED's attachment can therefore co-exist.

**ED use.** Tell the RP in writing that the attached assets stay attached, and that the RP's statutory custody does not permit sale, encumbrance or transfer of them.

### 7.2 Preservation, insurance, valuation and operation of attached assets

**Proposition.** While moratorium or s.32A litigation is pending, the RP's duty to preserve and, where needed, value attached property continues. A pending challenge is no excuse to stop preserving the asset.

**Authority.** ***Rajiv Chakraborty*** *(corpus: DLHC010046592020)*. The compendium places this duty here as its own framing of s.25(1) IBC. The judgment itself decides the moratorium, title and s.32A points (paras 67-116) and contains no separate holding on preservation.

**ED use.** Ask the RP to confirm insurance and upkeep of attached assets, and to give ED valuations prepared in CIRP. Those valuations help in confirmation and equivalent-value questions.

### 7.3 Duty to disclose attachment and PMLA proceedings to the CoC and resolution applicants

**Proposition.** An undisclosed attachment distorts valuation and bidding, so the RP should disclose it and its origin from the outset. This is the compendium's framing, not a holding.

**Authority.** ***Anil Kohli (Dunar Foods)*** (3.7). It shows the stakes: there, an asset attached after CIRP stayed outside the estate because the investigation predated CIRP. It does not itself lay down a disclosure duty.

**ED use.** Write to the RP early, listing attached assets and pending PMLA proceedings, and ask that they be disclosed in the information memorandum and to bidders. An undisclosed attachment later becomes a bidder's ground to seek relief, which ED would then have to resist.

### 7.4 Standing to contest attachment or seek possession, release, restoration or restitution

**Proposition.** An NCLT cannot entertain an RP's attempt to recover property attached and confirmed before CIRP. It cannot decide public-law questions in the PMLA field. The RP's route is the PMLA authorities or the Special Court.

**Authorities.**
- ***Ramanathan Bhuvaneshwari, RP of Dreamz Infra India Pvt. Ltd. v. ED***, NCLT Bengaluru, 28.07.2025, I.A. 28/2024 in C.P.(IB) 113/BB/2022. The compendium reads the RP's first name both as "Ramanathan" and as "Sathyanathan" [?].
  - **Facts:** attachment and confirmation (2022) predated CIRP (2023).
  - **Held:** applying *Embassy Property*, the NCLT's powers are limited to ss.31 and 60 IBC, and it cannot decide whether an ED attachment should continue. Relying on *Dunar Foods* and *Kalyani Transco*, s.238 does not override the PMLA for tainted assets. The application was dismissed.
  - **Status:** first instance; persuasive. **Pro-ED.**
- ***Embassy Property*** (3.7) is the authority to cite for the principle.
- ***Value Wise*** (paras 14 and 16): no IBC tribunal can entertain any plea against ED. The IP's route is the Special Court under s.8(7)/8(8) (IBBI Circular No. IBBI/CIRP/87/2025 of 04.11.2025), or the PMLA appellate route.
- ***Rajiv Chakraborty*** (paras 113 and 115): an attachment does not leave the RP facing a fait accompli. The RP may approach the competent PMLA authorities, and remained free to seek release in accordance with law.
- **Caution.** *Rajiv Chakraborty* para 113 also says a PAO gives ED no superior or overriding right in the property. RPs will use that before the PMLA authorities when arguing priority.

**ED use.** Object at the threshold to every s.60(5) application against ED. Point the RP to the Special Court and the IBBI circular. Before any s.8(7) order is pressed, check that no appeal against the s.8(3) confirmation is pending (*Nav Nirman Builders*, SC 06.02.2026; see the companion brief).

### 7.5 Cooperation with ED while protecting the estate

**Proposition.** The s.14 moratorium does not suspend PMLA confirmation proceedings, so the RP must take part in them and protect the estate. The point is confined to the confirmation stage.

**Authorities.**
- ***Vikram Kumar, RP of Dr. Jain Video on Wheels Ltd. v. ED***, NCLT New Delhi, 02.09.2025, I.A. 4373/2021 and 5680/2023 in C.P.(IB) 843(ND)/2018.
  - **Facts:** the RP asked the NCLT to stay the confirmation proceedings as s.14 "proceedings". The Adjudicating Authority confirmed the attachment during CIRP anyway, and the RP then sought a declaration that this breached s.14.
  - **Held:** confirmation proceedings belong to the PMLA's special penal scheme, are not recovery proceedings and fall outside s.14. Both applications were dismissed.
  - **Status:** first instance; read narrowly. **Pro-ED.**
- ***Nitin Jain*** para 100 *(corpus: DLHC010126002021)*. The liquidator holds the corporate debtor's records and must give the PMLA authorities the material and information they ask for. The same reasoning applies to an RP, who has the same control of records under ss.17-18 IBC (ED's argument, not a holding).

**ED use.** Serve confirmation notices on the RP and expect the RP to appear. Ask for records under s.50 and cite *Nitin Jain* para 100 if the RP or liquidator resists.

### 7.6 Funding and conduct of litigation over attached property

**Proposition.** *Vantage Point II* (NCLAT, 14.10.2025) held that s.32A ends a pre-CIRP attachment automatically on plan approval, without an application to the PMLA authorities. The earlier order, *Vantage Point I* (13.08.2024), was recalled on 09.01.2025 because ED had not been impleaded, and cannot be cited. *Vantage Point II* is under appeal in the Supreme Court.

**Authority.** ***Vantage Point v. Gaurav Misra***, NCLAT Principal Bench (Ashok Bhushan, J., Chairperson; Barun Mitra, Member (T)), Company Appeal (AT) (Ins) 1495/2024, 2025 SCC OnLine NCLAT 1652 (also read as 1655 [?]).
- **Facts the NCLAT relied on:** no s.8 proceedings had begun; depositors formed the majority of the CoC; and the plan refunded depositors in full.
- **Status:** **under appeal**, SC CA 1567/2026, admitted 06.02.2026. Interim safeguards against alienation were reportedly ordered [?]. **Adverse.**
- **Compendium note:** the compendium misprints this entry with the *Dr. Jain Video on Wheels* details.

**ED use.** Treat it as unsettled and confine it to its facts. Its main practical lesson is procedural: insist on being impleaded and heard wherever plan proceedings touch attached assets.

### 7.7 The controlling authority on value maximisation, statutory cooperation and creditor protection

**Proposition.** The NCLAT Larger Bench in *Kiran Shah* approved *Varrsana Ispat* and *Rotomac* and declined to follow *Manoj Kumar Agarwal*. A pre-CIRP PMLA-attached property is not, for that reason, available to the resolution process. The effect of s.32A after a plan is approved is a separate question.

**Authority.** ***Kiran Shah, RP of KSL and Industries Ltd. v. ED***, NCLAT (Larger Bench), Company Appeal (AT) (Ins) 817/2021, 2022 SCC OnLine NCLAT 2 *(citation as given in Rajiv Chakraborty, fn. 18)*. Its date is unclear; the compendium reads it as 03.01.2022 and as 08.01.2023 [?].
- ***Rajiv Chakraborty*** (para 89) quotes it.
  - It held *Manoj Kumar Agarwal* contrary to stare decisis.
  - It treated the Supreme Court's dismissal of the appeal in *Varrsana* as binding.
  - It recorded only a status quo order, not a stay, in the Supreme Court appeal against *Axis Bank*.
- **Status:** controlling within the NCLAT. **Pro-ED.**

**ED use.** Cite it first whenever an RP or resolution applicant tries to pull attached assets into CIRP.

### 7.8 Restitution through RP-ED cooperation

**Proposition.** In a fit case, and with its consent, ED may agree to restitution under the second proviso to s.8(8) PMLA. It can separate tainted from untainted consideration to protect genuine homebuyers. Doing so does not dilute ED's attachment powers.

**Authority.** ***Udaipur Entertainment World Pvt. Ltd. v. Union of India***, SC, 10.10.2025, appeals arising out of SLP(C) 10734/2025 and 26154-26155/2025. On ED's affidavit and by settlement:
- the attachment was partly lifted, and units were restored to the resolution applicant only for genuine homebuyers;
- eleven units stayed attached;
- the corporate debtor was dropped from the prosecution under s.32A, while individuals' prosecution continued;
- ED kept liberty to act if the resolution applicant proved connected or a beneficiary.

The Court said it is not a precedent and left all questions of law open (paras 2-13). **Status:** consent order. **Neutral.**

**ED use.** When agreeing to restitution:
- retain specific units where needed;
- preserve the prosecution of individuals and the attachment of their properties;
- make relief conditional on the resolution applicant's independence;
- reserve liberty to act if that condition fails.

## Part C. Who represents the corporate debtor in PMLA proceedings (Chapter 8)

The compendium advises an IRP/RP, on appointment, to:
- take over all pending litigation;
- tell every forum of the change in management;
- review the stage of each PMLA proceeding;
- engage counsel, with CoC approval where the Regulations require it;
- keep a central litigation register.

This is practice guidance, not law.

### 8.1 Statutory basis: proceedings by the corporate debtor and their continuation through the IRP/RP

**Proposition.** Section 17 IBC vests management in the IRP from appointment, suspends the board, and obliges officers to report to the IRP and give access to records. This displaces the old board, including its power to instruct counsel in the corporate debtor's cases. No case is cited; the point rests on the statute.

**ED use.** Notices addressed to the corporate debtor during CIRP should go to the IRP/RP. Serving only the suspended board risks an order later being set aside for want of proper representation.

### 8.2 Statutory basis: proceedings against the corporate debtor before or during CIRP

**Proposition.** Section 25(1) (duty to preserve and protect assets) and s.25(2)(b) (power to represent the corporate debtor in judicial and quasi-judicial proceedings and appoint counsel) give the RP authority to appear for it before the Adjudicating Authority, the Appellate Tribunal and the courts in PMLA matters. That authority does not widen the NCLT's jurisdiction over the merits of an attachment. The RP or resolution applicant represents the corporate debtor only; the former promoters remain separately exposed. No case is cited; the point rests on the statute.

**ED use.** Keep two tracks:
- the corporate debtor, represented by the RP;
- the former promoters and directors, represented by their own counsel.

Do not let former management speak for the company, and do not let the RP speak for the individuals.

### 8.3 ED-initiated attachment, adjudication and prosecution involving the corporate debtor

**Proposition.** *P. Mohanraj* is included only by analogy, and its reasoning does not extend to the PMLA (*Rajiv Chakraborty*; *Goenka*). *Embassy Property* is the general public-law foundation: s.60(5) does not let the NCLT review a public authority's decisions merely because a CIRP debtor is affected. The PMLA-specific bar is derived from it.

**Authorities.**
- ***P. Mohanraj*** (3.2) and ***Ajay Kumar Radheyshyam Goenka*** (3.2).
- ***Embassy Property*** (3.7).
- ***Kalyani Transco v. Bhushan Power and Steel Ltd.***, SC (B.R. Gavai, CJI; Satish Chandra Sharma and K. Vinod Chandran, JJ.), 26.09.2025, 2025 INSC 1165. The compendium cites it for the point that the NCLT, whose powers are limited to ss.31 and 60, cannot decide the validity of an ED attachment, confirmation or confiscation. It also notes paras 118-124, where ED's continuing prosecution and attachment were counted among the reasons for delay in implementation.
  - **Status:** binding; supersedes the recalled judgment of 02.05.2025 (2025 INSC 621). Not in LawBase; the paragraph references are per compendium. **Mixed.**
- ***Value Wise*** (para 14) states the same jurisdictional rule directly *(corpus)*.

**ED use.** Resist any use of the RP's s.25 role to bring the merits of an attachment before the NCLT. Cite *Embassy Property*, *Value Wise* and *Kalyani Transco*.

### 8.4 Whether the IRP/RP must appear and defend the corporate debtor in PMLA proceedings

**Proposition.** The compendium retains *Manoj Kumar Agarwal* only as history; it is disapproved by *Kiran Shah* and is not current law on this question. No case directly decides whether the RP must defend. The answer follows from ss.17 and 25 and from *Dr. Jain Video on Wheels* (7.5): confirmation proceedings continue during CIRP, so an RP who stays away loses the corporate debtor's chance to be heard.

**Authorities.** *Manoj Kumar Agarwal* (NCLAT, 09.04.2021 [?], 2021 SCC OnLine NCLAT 121; **disapproved**); *Kiran Shah* (7.7).

### 8.5 Representation where former directors or KMPs are accused of the scheduled offence or money-laundering

**Proposition.** The timing of the ECIR or investigation relative to CIRP, not the date of the formal attachment order, decides whether a tainted asset stays outside the estate (*Dunar Foods*). The compendium places this estate-composition point here because the former management's pre-CIRP conduct is in issue.

**Authority.** ***Anil Kohli (Dunar Foods)*** (3.7). It is not a holding on the RP's representative capacity or disclosure duty.

**ED use.** Where the former directors are accused, the RP's interest (maximising the estate) and theirs (avoiding liability) diverge. Do not accept statements on behalf of the corporate debtor from accused former officers. Ask the RP to confirm the company's position on the record.

### 8.6 Conflict where the RP depends on former management for records and instructions

**Proposition.** An RP who depends on former management under investigation for records faces a practical tension. *Rajiv Chakraborty* illustrates the setting, but its holdings are on the moratorium, title and s.32A only.

**Authorities.**
- ***Rajiv Chakraborty*** *(corpus: DLHC010046592020)*: no holding on this point.
- ***Nitin Jain*** para 100 *(corpus: DLHC010126002021)*: the liquidator, not former management, holds the records and must give them to the PMLA authorities.

**ED use.** Summon the RP or liquidator for documents in the company's custody, and the former officers for their personal knowledge. Under ss.17(2)(d) and 19 IBC, officers must report to the IRP, give access to records and cooperate. Non-cooperation by them is a matter for the NCLT under the IBC and for ED under s.50 PMLA.

### 8.7 Who bears litigation costs and controls strategy: RP, CoC, resolution applicant or liquidator

**Proposition.** No case decides this. By analogy only, the compendium cites *Nita Puri*. It suggests that a successor may need to fund a challenge to an investigation begun long after insolvency or liquidation, and that an investigation lacking a sound basis may be quashed.

**Authority.** ***Nita Puri v. Union of India*** (3.3) *(corpus: DLHC010003032025)*. The judgment quashed the SFIO direction for a wrong factual premise and failure to consider relevant material (paras 39, 58, 63, 71), not for delay.

**ED use.** Expect resolution applicants and liquidators to fund such challenges. Pre-empt them by recording accurate reasons in the order itself, based on what the audits actually say.

### 8.8 Effect of non-appearance or inadequate defence by the RP

**Proposition.** No case decides this directly. The compendium derives it from *Vantage Point*, where an order was recalled because ED had not been heard: an absent party risks an order that cannot stand, so RPs should take part and seek impleadment (compendium view, not a holding).

**Authority.** ***Vantage Point*** (7.6): *Vantage Point I* (13.08.2024) was recalled on 09.01.2025 for want of ED's impleadment.

**ED use.** The same logic protects ED: insist on impleadment in plan proceedings that touch attached assets. If the RP does not appear in PMLA proceedings, record the service and proceed; the record should show the RP had notice.

## Open points and risks

1. **Post-cognizance custody.** *Tarsem Lal* allows custody only through the Special Court, "at the highest" under s.309(2). The compendium flags a likely debate on how far that differs from s.19 in practice (compendium view). Plan arrests before the complaint.
2. ***P. Mohanraj* referral.** *Dineshchand Surana v. UCO Bank* has sent the s.138 question to a larger Bench *(as quoted in Value Wise para 13)*. Any change is likely to narrow the moratorium further, which suits ED, but verify the outcome.
3. **New management's exposure.** The compendium's view that unconnected new management should not be prosecuted merely because of the takeover is not a holding. ED keeps the right to examine whether the resolution applicant is truly unconnected; *V Hotels*, *Udaipur* and *Shiv Charan* reserved that liberty by consent.
4. **Stale-investigation challenges.** *Nita Puri* is a Companies Act case. It rests on a wrong factual premise and ignored material, not on delay as such, but it will be cited against late PMLA action. Accurate, self-contained reasons are the defence.
5. **The RP's duties.** No judgment in the compendium lays down an RP's duty to disclose attachments or to defend PMLA proceedings. These rest on ss.17, 18, 25 IBC and on the compendium's guidance. *Nitin Jain* para 100 (records) is the only corpus-verified holding on cooperation.
6. ***Rajiv Chakraborty* para 113.** It says a PAO gives ED no superior right in the property and leaves the RP free to seek release. Expect RPs to rely on it in PMLA forums, especially for property that is only of equivalent value.
7. ***Vantage Point II*** is under appeal. Until it is decided, its practical lesson (impleadment) matters more than its holding.
8. **Practice illustrations** (DHFL, Shree Ganesh Jewellery House) are per compendium and are not reported decisions on the law.

## After the 2026 Amendment

The IBC (Amendment) Act, 2026 (Act 6 of 2026; most provisions in force from 26.05.2026) touches this topic in these ways.
- **s.32A is unamended.** The first proviso to s.32A(1), read with *P. Mohanraj* paras 33-34, still governs discharge of the corporate debtor from prosecution.
- **s.14 now has s.14(2A) (critical supplies) and an Explanation in s.14(3)(b) on sureties.** Neither extends the moratorium to investigation, arrest or prosecution. The surety Explanation supports the point that promoters' and guarantors' personal assets are outside the corporate debtor's moratorium.
- **The liquidation moratorium is now in s.33(1)(b),** which imports s.14(1)(a) and (c); old s.33(5) is omitted. *Value Wise*'s reasoning on s.33(5) should carry over, but no decision on the new clause is noted.
- **s.31(5)-(6) codify the clean slate.** Whether s.31(6) ("claims against the corporate debtor and its assets") could reach a pending PMLA prosecution of the company is unsettled. ED's position: a prosecution is not a "claim", and s.32A, which is unamended and specific, alone governs the corporate debtor's criminal liability.
- **s.7(5) as substituted makes admission mandatory.** Expect CIRP to start more often while ED's investigation is still open. Register the ECIR and record key dates promptly, since timing drives both the look-through and s.32A.
- **Group insolvency (s.59A) and Chapter IV-A creditor-initiated insolvency** are enacted but not yet notified. Who represents each group entity in PMLA proceedings is not yet addressed by the IBC.
