## Short answer

- **Most CIRPs are genuine.** A CIRP is not fraudulent just because PMLA proceedings are running alongside it. What ED needs is specific signs of collusion, raised before the NCLT itself.
- **Section 65 lets the NCLT stop a collusive CIRP.** The objection must be raised before the NCLT, not for the first time on appeal (*Beacon Trusteeship*, SC, 2020). The NCLT may act on its own motion (*Hytone*, NCLAT, 2021). It must form a reasoned prima facie view and hear the parties first (*Unigreen*, NCLAT, 2017).
- **Look through form to substance.** A "loan" with no real disbursal against the time value of money is not financial debt. A party that was related when the transaction took place cannot get onto the CoC by divesting later (*Phoenix ARC*, SC, 2021). Section 29A looks at de facto control (*ArcelorMittal*, SC, paras 29 and 56-57) *(corpus: 2018_INSC_935)*.
- **In builder insolvencies, ask the NCLT to check that the insolvency is genuine.** *Arena Superstructures* (All HC, 24.02.2025) *(corpus: UPHC011069462024)* found an engineered insolvency after homebuyer money was siphoned off. It referred the matter to ED to trace the funds (para 194).
- **CoC approval or a s.12A withdrawal does not show that the settlement money is clean.** In *Sterling Biotech*, the NCLAT (28.08.2019) decided only that s.29A does not apply to a s.12A settlement. It expressly left ED, CBI, MCA and SEBI free to proceed. The source of the funds was never examined.
- **Commercial wisdom is about the deal, not the money.** *Vallal RCK* (SC, 2022) protects a 90%+ CoC decision unless it is capricious. It says nothing about whether the funds are proceeds of crime.
- **The Sandesara settlement order is not a precedent.** In *Hemant S. Hathi / Chetan Sandesara v. CBI* (SC, 19.11.2025), CBI, PMLA, FEO, SFIO and Black Money Act proceedings were quashed on a deposit of Rs 5,100 crore. The Court said in terms that the order is not a precedent (para 12(vi)). Answer every attempt to use it with that paragraph.
- **The diverted asset may be tainted.** Prove that before a lender's priority is argued. A lender whose security predates the crime keeps priority over untainted "value" property only (*Axis Bank*, Del HC, paras 149-165, 161) *(corpus: DLHC012396152018)*. In Delhi a PAO gives ED no priority over secured creditors (*Rajiv Chakraborty*, paras 110-113) *(corpus: DLHC010046592020)*.
- **Avoidance proceedings and PMLA confiscation run on separate tracks.** Applications under ss.43-51 and 66 survive plan approval (*Venus Recruiters*, Del HC DB, 2023). Section 32A discharge of the company never covers former promoters or officers (*Manish Kumar*, SC, paras 253-254 and 257) *(corpus: 2021_INSC_28)*.
- **2026 Amendment.** Section 12A has been substituted: withdrawal is now possible only after the CoC is constituted and before resolution plans are invited, with 90% CoC approval. Section 7(5) now makes admission mandatory. Both changes affect the case law below (see the last section).

## Part A. Fraudulent, collusive or sham CIRP (Chapter 11)

### A1. Starting a CIRP for a purpose other than genuine resolution

- ***Swiss Ribbons Pvt. Ltd. v. Union of India*** — SC (R.F. Nariman J.), 25.01.2019, (2019) 4 SCC 17; [2019] 3 SCR 535 *(corpus: 2019_INSC_95)*. Status: binding. **Neutral.**
  - Section 29A is valid legislative policy. It keeps defaulting and connected persons out of resolution, and, through s.35(1)(f), out of liquidation sales (paras 66-71, 69).
  - "Related party" in s.29A(j) is read down to persons connected with the resolution applicant's business (para 75).
  - Section 12A and its 90% threshold are valid. The NCLT can set aside an arbitrary refusal by the CoC (paras 52-53).
- **How ED should use it.** It gives the policy reason why defaulting promoters must not regain control at a discount. It says nothing on fraudulent CIRP or on where settlement money came from.
- **Corpus and compendium disagree.** The compendium says *Hytone* relied on "para 59" of *Swiss Ribbons* for the NCLT's duty to guard against mala fide CIRPs. In the SCR text, para 59 deals with how claims are verified. The passage *Hytone* meant may carry a different number in another report. Check before citing a paragraph [?].

### A2. Collusive financial debt, friendly s.7 petitions and manufactured default

- ***Phoenix ARC Pvt. Ltd. v. Spade Financial Services Ltd.*** — SC (Chandrachud, Malhotra and Banerjee JJ.), 01.02.2021, (2021) 3 SCC 475. Not in corpus. Status: binding. **Pro-ED.**
  - Financial debt needs actual disbursal against the time value of money. A transaction dressed up to look like one is not financial debt.
  - Related-party status for the first proviso to s.21(2) is judged at the time of the transaction. A party cannot buy its way onto the CoC by shedding the relationship later.
  - Signs of collusion included late and irregular paperwork, unregistered charges and money going round in circles through related entities.
- **How ED should use it.**
  - Check whether the creditor that started the CIRP is, in substance, linked to the promoters.
  - Trace circular flows through related entities back to the debtor.

### A3. Using the moratorium to obstruct investigation or attachment

- ***ArcelorMittal India Pvt. Ltd. v. Satish Kumar Gupta*** — SC (R.F. Nariman J.), 04.10.2018, (2019) 2 SCC 1; [2018] 12 SCR 362 *(corpus: 2018_INSC_935)*. Status: binding. **Pro-ED (by analogy).**
  - Section 29A is a "see through provision" that looks at de facto control (para 29).
  - Control means positive control over management or policy decisions (paras 44-50).
  - Facts reasonably close to the date of the plan can be examined to stop the old management from returning "in some other form" (paras 56-57).
- **The moratorium itself does not obstruct ED.** It never suspended a PMLA attachment. See *Axis Bank* para 146, *Rajiv Chakraborty* paras 78-83, and *Value Wise Consultancy* (NCLAT, 30.06.2026) para 12 *(corpus: EDPORTAL_18_2026-06-30)*. The real risk is an attempt to earn s.32A immunity by passing control to a hidden promoter. Use the *ArcelorMittal* see-through test against that.

### A4. Using a resolution plan to cleanse tainted assets or hand control back to connected persons

- ***Bank of Baroda v. MBL Infrastructures Ltd.*** — SC (Kaul and Sundresh JJ.), 18.01.2022, 2022 SCC OnLine SC 48. Not in corpus. Status: binding. **Adverse.**
  - The promoter's plan breached s.29A(h). "Such creditor" includes similarly placed creditors who invoked the guarantee.
  - The Court still refused to unwind the plan, because it had been substantially implemented and Rs 300 crore of fresh capital had been raised on the strength of it.
- **How ED should use it.** Object to eligibility and to the change of control before the plan is implemented. Argue that s.32A, which ends criminal liability, deserves no similar leniency: if the conditions in *Manish Kumar* para 252 were never met, the immunity never arose.

### A5. ARC-SRA-promoter links, undervaluation and hidden funding: s.65, the three cases

- ***Beacon Trusteeship Ltd. v. Earthcon Infracon Pvt. Ltd.*** — SC, 18.02.2020, CA 7641/2019. Not in corpus. Status: binding. **Neutral.** The NCLT must examine a properly raised allegation of collusive initiation under s.65. It cannot be raised for the first time on appeal.
- ***Hytone Merchants Pvt. Ltd. v. Satabadi Investment Consultants Pvt. Ltd.*** — NCLAT, 30.06.2021, CA(AT)(Ins) 258/2021. Not in corpus. Status: NCLAT. **Pro-ED.** Section 65 is preventive as well as punitive. The NCLT may reject, on its own motion, a s.7 application that is complete in form but shows visible signs of collusion.
- ***Unigreen Global Pvt. Ltd. v. Punjab National Bank*** — NCLAT, 01.12.2017, CA(AT)(Ins) 81/2017. Not in corpus. Status: NCLAT. **Neutral.** Before any adverse consequence under s.65, the NCLT must form a reasoned prima facie view and hear the parties. An inference drawn only from the debtor's net worth is not enough.
- **How ED should use these.** Give the NCLT enough material for a prima facie view early in the case:
  - the related-party chain;
  - any link between an ARC and the eventual applicant;
  - the plan price set against an independent valuation; and
  - how the transaction was funded.

### A6. Safeguards, disclosures and warning signs

- ***M/s Arena Superstructures Pvt. Ltd. v. State of U.P.*** — Allahabad HC DB (M.C. Tripathi and Prashant Kumar JJ.), delivered 24.02.2025, 2025:AHC:25736-DB *(corpus: UPHC011069462024; the LawBase metadata shows 23.02.2025)*. Status: no later history known. **Pro-ED.**
  - The builder collected about Rs 387 crore from homebuyers and siphoned much of it off. The Court found an "orchestrated insolvency" with the connivance of Noida officials (paras 128-132).
  - The transactions fell within PMLA, and ED was the appropriate agency to investigate (para 145).
  - Section 32A protects only the company under new management. Promoters remain liable (paras 146-150).
  - The finality of an approved plan does not cover an insolvency born of fraud (paras 158 and 190).
  - Before admitting a builder's insolvency, the NCLT or NCLAT should check that the proceeding is genuine and not filed by the builder to escape liability after siphoning funds (para 192).
  - The Court referred the matter to ED to trace the siphoned money and bring it back into the company (para 194).
  - The guidance is given in a real-estate setting. Timing alone does not prove collusion.
- ***Bikram Chatterji v. Union of India*** (Amrapali) — SC (Arun Mishra and U.U. Lalit JJ.), 23.07.2019, [2019] 9 SCR 289 *(corpus: 2019_INSC_799)*. Status: binding, with continuing supervision. **Pro-ED.**
  - The Court accepted the forensic audit, which found homebuyers' money diverted, including about Rs 2,996.20 crore. It recorded that "money laundering was resorted to" (para 147).
  - It cancelled the RERA registrations and leases and appointed a Court Receiver and NBCC (paras 148-150 and 154).
  - It directed ED to investigate the FEMA and FDI breaches, with the money-laundering aspect to be looked into as well (paras 151 and 154(vi)).
  - The attachments in the case were the Court's own.
  - **Corpus and compendium disagree.** The compendium describes this as an Article 142 route outside the IBC and says ED was directed to attach the directors' personal property. The judgment was passed in Article 32 petitions and does not rely on Article 142. ED was directed to investigate; the attachments were made by the Court.
- ***Bhupinder Singh v. Unitech Ltd.*** — SC, supervisory orders from 20.01.2020 (board superseded), CA 10856/2016. Not in corpus. Status: continuing supervision. **Pro-ED.** The Court took control of management outside the IBC. ED's attachments, about Rs 1,059.52 crore per the compendium, and ED's prosecution of the promoters ran alongside independently. Dissipation of assets from custody led to further coercive orders.
- ***Amit Katyal v. Meera Ahuja*** — SC, 03.03.2022, CA 3778/2020. Not in corpus. Status: binding. **Neutral.** The Court allowed homebuyer applicants to withdraw at the appellate stage, after a genuine settlement, using Article 142 with Rule 11 of the NCLT Rules. That does not vouch for the source of the settlement money.
- ***Vantage Point Asset Management v. Gaurav Misra (RP, Alchemist Infra Realty)*** — NCLAT, 14.10.2025, CA(AT)(Ins) 1495/2024. Not in corpus. Status: **under appeal**, SC CA 1567/2026, admitted 06.02.2026 with interim directions. An earlier order of 13.08.2024 was recalled for want of notice to ED. **Adverse.** The NCLAT held that s.32A operates automatically on approval of a plan that changes control, so a PAO made before CIRP lapses without any application to the PMLA fora. Never present it as settled.
- **Warning signs** *(Compendium view, not a holding; Appendix B checklist, our wording)*:
  - the petitioning creditor is linked in substance to the promoters;
  - related-party money is presented as financial debt;
  - admission is quick and uncontested;
  - promoter influence lies behind the resolution applicant;
  - an ARC is linked to the eventual applicant;
  - the plan price is low against independent valuation;
  - a s.12A settlement's funding is unexplained;
  - the CIRP is filed close to enforcement action. This calls for scrutiny but proves nothing alone.

## Part B. Settlement and withdrawal under s.12A, and where the money came from (Chapter 15)

### B1. Is the settlement money derived from, or does it represent, proceeds of crime?

- ***Sterling Biotech Ltd., In re*** — NCLT Mumbai, 08.05.2019, C.P.(IB) 490/MB/2018. Not in corpus. Status: **reversed** by the NCLAT on 28.08.2019. **Pro-ED (reasoning only).**
  - The promoters had absconded and were under ED and CBI investigation. The NCLT refused withdrawal on a settlement they had offered, even though over 90% of the CoC had approved it, and ordered liquidation.
  - It questioned how absconders could settle and where the money came from.
  - It is useful as persuasive reasoning on the source of funds, because the reversal did not deal with that point.
- ***Andhra Bank v. Sterling Biotech Ltd.*** (with the CoC's connected appeal) — NCLAT, 28.08.2019 [?] (the OCR shows 2016, which is impossible), CA(AT)(Ins) 601, 612 and 527 of 2019. Not in corpus. Status: NCLAT. **Mixed.**
  - Section 29A governs who may submit a resolution plan. A promoter behind a s.12A settlement is not a resolution applicant, so s.29A does not bar the withdrawal.
  - The CIRP was treated as withdrawn.
  - The Tribunal expressly left ED, CBI, MCA and SEBI free to continue their proceedings against the company and the promoters.
- **How ED should use these.** The appellate order decided eligibility only. Whether the settlement money was proceeds of crime remains open. Cite the express reservation of ED's proceedings.

### B2. The duty to disclose and verify the source of settlement funds

- **The law.** No reported decision in this set requires the CoC or the NCLT to verify where settlement money came from.
- **Compendium view (not a holding).** The CoC's record in *Sterling Biotech* shows no check on the source. The doctrine of commercial wisdom does not answer the source-of-funds question.
- **LawBase analysis.** ED's own powers do not depend on the CoC. Settlement money is "property". If it is derived from criminal activity, it is proceeds of crime under s.2(1)(u), and the ordinary powers of investigation and attachment apply. *Vijay Madanlal Choudhary* (SC, 2022) *(corpus: 2022_INSC_757)* upheld the width of that definition.

### B3. Settlements funded by promoters, related parties, overseas entities or third parties

- ***Srei Infrastructure Finance Ltd. and Srei Equipment Finance Ltd., In re*** — NCLT Kolkata, 11.08.2023, I.A.(IB) 428 and 434/KB/2023 in C.P.(IB) 294 and 295/KB/2021. Not in corpus. Status: NCLT. **Pro-ED.**
  - The CIRPs followed an RBI reference under s.227. The transaction auditor flagged about Rs 13,110 crore of transactions under s.66.
  - The Administrator rejected the promoters' s.12A offer of about Rs 32,000 crore. NARCL's plan of Rs 14,867.50 crore was approved, and the s.66 applications were left to proceed separately.
  - The rejection was an administrative decision on these facts, not a ruling on s.12A.
- ***Vallal RCK v. Siva Industries and Holdings Ltd.*** — SC (Gavai and Kohli JJ.), 03.06.2022, (2022) 9 SCC 803. Not in corpus. Status: binding. **Adverse.**
  - The CoC approved a promoter-side settlement of about Rs 318-323 crore by 94.23%.
  - The NCLT (12.08.2021) and NCLAT (28.01.2022) had rejected it and ordered liquidation.
  - The Supreme Court held that the tribunals cannot sit in appeal over a considered decision of 90%+ of the CoC unless it is wholly capricious, arbitrary, irrational or contrary to the statute. It allowed the settlement even though liquidation had begun.
- **How ED should use these.** Commercial wisdom answers whether the deal is good for creditors. It does not answer whether the money is clean. Where promoter conduct is under s.66 scrutiny, point to *Srei*.

### B4. Does withdrawal of the CIRP affect the ECIR, attachment, investigation or prosecution?

- **Short answer.** No. The withdrawal ends the insolvency process only.
- ***Nitin Jayantilal Sandesara v. Union of India*** (FEO declaration) — Special Court under the FEO Act, New Delhi, 28.09.2020. Not in corpus. Status: **quashed** by the Supreme Court on 19.11.2025, conditional on the deposit described below. **Pro-ED (historical).**
  - The promoters and their associates were declared Fugitive Economic Offenders after the CIRP had been withdrawn.
  - This shows that the criminal track survived the commercial settlement.
- ***Hemant S. Hathi v. CBI; Chetan Jayantilal Sandesara v. CBI*** — SC (Article 32), 19.11.2025, with a reconciliation order of 18.11.2025. Not in corpus. Status: **expressly non-precedential** (para 12(vi)). **Adverse.**
  - The Court accepted the Solicitor General's figure of Rs 5,100 crore as a full and final settlement.
  - It quashed the CBI, PMLA, FEO, SFIO and Black Money Act proceedings, effective only on full deposit by 17.12.2025, for distribution to the lender banks (paras 11-12).
  - The compendium does not record whether the deposit was made.
- **How ED should use these.** Meet any reliance on *Hathi* with para 12(vi): the order rests on peculiar facts and is not a precedent. A s.12A withdrawal does not end the PMLA case.

### B5. May ED attach settlement money, or assets acquired through a settlement?

- **The law.** No case in this set decides the point directly.
- **LawBase analysis.** Yes, on the ordinary principles, if the money or asset is shown to be proceeds of crime or property of equivalent value.
  - Section 2(1)(u) is wide, and *Vijay Madanlal* upheld it *(corpus: 2022_INSC_757)*.
  - Property of a person who is not accused can be attached (*B. Rama Raju*, AP HC, 2011, Issue A) *(corpus: HBHC010111342010)*. The holder may still show bona fide acquisition without knowledge.
  - Watch the timing. Property acquired before the scheduled offence is not proceeds of crime in the first sense (*Pavana Dibbur*, SC, 2023, para 27(c)) *(corpus: 2023_INSC_1029)*. In the P&H High Court's territory, *Seema Garg* also blocks "value" attachment of pre-offence property *(corpus: PHHC011207472019)*.
- **Compendium view (not a holding).** The compendium rests this on *Axis Bank*. *Axis Bank* deals with attachment against third-party lenders, not settlement money as such.

### B6. The constitutional and procedural basis of s.12A

- *Swiss Ribbons* upheld s.12A and the 90% threshold (paras 52-53) *(corpus: 2019_INSC_95)*.
- ***Brilliant Alloys Pvt. Ltd. v. S. Rajagopal*** — SC, 14.12.2018, SLP(C) 31557/2018, reported at (2022) 2 SCC 544 per the compendium. Not in corpus as a separate entry; *Swiss Ribbons* refers to it. Status: binding. **Neutral.** The bar in Regulation 30A on withdrawal after expressions of interest are invited is directory, because s.12A contains no such bar. The 2026 Amendment has now put a time limit into s.12A itself (see the last section).
- Neither case speaks to the source of the funds.

### B7. *Sterling Biotech* and comparable settlement structures

- ***Arun Kumar Jagatramka v. Jindal Steel and Power Ltd.*** — SC (D.Y. Chandrachud J.), 15.03.2021, (2021) 7 SCC 474; [2021] 3 SCR 114 *(corpus: 2021_INSC_187)*. Status: binding. **Pro-ED in part.**
  - A person ineligible under s.29A read with s.35(1)(f) cannot propose a s.230 scheme for a company in IBC liquidation (paras 67-69, 76 and 91).
  - The purpose of s.29A is that a person who caused the problem "cannot be a part of the process of solution" (paras 52-53).
  - **But** the Court distinguished a s.12A withdrawal. It comes at the start of the process, restores the earlier position and is a settlement. A plan or a sanctioned scheme is the culmination of the process and binds everyone. The two "cannot be equated" (paras 74-75).
- **Compendium view (not a holding).** The compendium cites *Jagatramka* for "substance over form" against promoter-funded s.12A settlements. That is the compilers' own extension. Paras 74-75 can be turned against it. Use *Jagatramka* for s.230 schemes and plans, and argue s.12A cases on the source of funds instead.
- *Amit Katyal* (A6) is a homebuyer settlement in which money flowed from the promoter to the homebuyers. Do not let it be cited for promoter-funded settlements of unclear origin.

### B8. Due diligence where a matter is withdrawn on settlement

**Compendium checklist, not law** (our wording). Before accepting settlement funds:
1. get a written statement of the exact source;
2. check whether the source is linked to the old promoters, related parties or entities under investigation;
3. check the settling party and the source against pending ECIRs, FIRs and SFIO complaints;
4. make sure the CoC minutes record that the source question was considered;
5. do not treat a high approval percentage as an answer;
6. have it recorded that the withdrawal does not affect any independent criminal proceeding.

## Part C. Competing claims over diverted and attached assets (Chapter 16)

### C1. Property held by a promoter, director, KMP, relative, guarantor or related entity but bought with the debtor's money

- ***Deputy Director, ED v. Axis Bank*** — Delhi HC (R.K. Gauba J.), 02.04.2019, 2019 SCC OnLine Del 7854; (2019) 259 DLT 500 *(corpus: DLHC012396152018)*. Status: under appeal. The SC passed only a status quo order on 30.08.2019, with no stay. **Mixed.**
  - When attaching, the State "does not stand as a creditor" (para 141). The s.14 moratorium cannot stop a PMLA attachment (para 146).
  - Tainted property can be attached even against a bona fide transferee for value (para 161).
  - For untainted "value" property, a bona fide interest acquired before the criminal activity is protected. The date of the criminal activity is the cut-off (paras 162-165).
  - The accused, and a third party who acquired from the accused, carry the burden of proving the facts on which they seek release (conclusions (v) and (ix)).
- ***B. Rama Raju v. Union of India*** — AP HC DB, 04.03.2011, 2011 SCC OnLine AP 152 *(corpus: HBHC010111342010)*. Status: good law, read with the present s.24 and *Vijay Madanlal*. **Pro-ED.**
  - Property held by a person not charged with the scheduled offence is equally liable to attachment and confiscation (Issue A).
  - Attachment needs neither mens rea nor knowledge. A non-accused holder may still prove bona fide acquisition (Issues C-D).
- **How ED should use these.** Show first that the asset was bought with the debtor's diverted money, which makes it tainted. Only if that fails, fall back on "value" property, and then test the date and bona fides of any lender's charge.

### C2. Tracing siphoned or diverted money into property, securities and layered assets

- *Phoenix ARC* (A2) lets ED attack "financial debts" that are really circular related-party flows.
- *Vijay Madanlal* *(corpus: 2022_INSC_757)* upheld the width of "proceeds of crime", including equivalent value where tainted property cannot be traced or is held abroad.
- **LawBase analysis.** Reports from transaction audits under ss.43-51 and 66 are ready-made tracing leads. In *Srei*, about Rs 13,110 crore was flagged. Ask the RP or Administrator for them under s.50 PMLA. *Nitin Jain* (Del HC, 2021, para 100) *(corpus: DLHC010126002021)* holds that a liquidator must give PMLA authorities the records and information they ask for.

### C3. Legal title and beneficial ownership: avoiding diverted value

- ***Anuj Jain, IRP for Jaypee Infratech Ltd. v. Axis Bank Ltd.*** — SC, 26.02.2020, (2020) 8 SCC 401. Not in corpus. Status: binding. **Pro-ED.**
  - Jaypee Infratech mortgaged its own land to secure its parent's debts, without consideration. The Court held these were preferential transactions under s.43 and avoided them.
  - The parent's lenders were not financial creditors of the subsidiary.
- **How ED should use it.** Use it as a model for showing that an asset pledged for another entity's benefit is value taken out of the debtor.

### C4. ED says the property is proceeds of crime; the RP or liquidator wants it restored to the estate

- ***Venus Recruiters Pvt. Ltd. v. Union of India*** — Delhi HC. The Single Judge (26.11.2020) was **reversed** by the Division Bench in *Tata Steel BSL Ltd. v. Venus Recruiters*, 13.01.2023, LPA 37 and 43 of 2021, 2023/DHC/000257. Not in corpus. Status: the DB view governs. **Pro-ED.**
  - Avoidance applications survive the end of CIRP and approval of the plan. The RP may pursue them.
  - Recoveries are distributed as the NCLT directs, separately from the plan.
- ***Rajiv Chakraborty, RP of Era Infra Engineering v. ED*** — Delhi HC (Yashwant Varma J.), 11.11.2022, W.P.(C) 9531/2020, 2022/DHC/004739 *(corpus: DLHC010046592020)*. Status: good law in Delhi; the writ was dismissed (para 114). **Pro-ED on the moratorium; adverse on priority.**
  - ED is not a creditor (paras 67-69). Section 14 does not shut out ss.5 and 8 PMLA (para 83). Attachment transfers no title (paras 94-101).
  - **But** a PAO gives ED no superior or overriding right in the property. Priority and distribution are settled independently (paras 110-113 and 116).
  - The RP may seek release from the PMLA authorities (paras 113 and 115).
- **How ED should use these.** The estate's avoidance claim and ED's confiscation claim are separate. Which one prevails over a particular asset depends on whether the asset is tainted. Settle that before the PMLA fora, not before the NCLT.

### C5. Section 32A discharge from prosecution, distinguished from avoidance under ss.43-51 and 66

- ***Tata Steel BSL Ltd. v. Union of India*** — Delhi HC, 2020 [?], W.P.(Crl.) 3087/2019. Not in corpus. Status: High Court. **Mixed.**
  - After an approved plan passed 72.65% and control to an unconnected buyer, the SFIO complaint, cognizance and summons against the company were set aside under s.32A.
  - The Court expressly preserved the prosecution of the former promoters and officers.
- ***Bhushan Power & Steel Ltd. v. Union of India*** — Delhi HC (Manmeet Pritam Singh Arora J.), 30.01.2025, W.P.(Crl) 1261/2024 *(corpus: DLHC010218602024)*. Status: the order was made subject to the outcome of the appeals against the plan in the Supreme Court; those were later decided in *Kalyani Transco* (26.09.2025). **Mixed.**
  - Cognizance and process in the PMLA complaint were set aside as against the company only, under s.32A(1) (paras 7-7.1).
  - On ED's submission, which was not opposed, the Court clarified two things. First, the company's role in the offence under its old management must still be examined at the trial of the former promoters and directors. Second, there are allegations under s.70 PMLA (para 6.3).
- **How ED should use these.** One company can be clean on the criminal side and still owe money on the civil avoidance side. Section 32A discharge never reaches individuals (*Manish Kumar* paras 253 and 257). Ask for a para 6.3-style clarification whenever a company is discharged.

### C6. Constitutional basis for tracing and confiscation

- ***Vijay Madanlal Choudhary v. Union of India*** — SC, 27.07.2022, [2022] 6 SCR 382 *(corpus: 2022_INSC_757)*. Status: binding. Some issues were referred for reconsideration on review; per the compendium, the core holdings stand. **Pro-ED.** The Court upheld the wide definition of proceeds of crime, the attachment and confiscation scheme, and PMLA as a self-contained regime.

### C7. Benami holders, trusts, shell companies and bona fide purchasers

**Two-step approach** (the compendium's grouping, corrected against the corpus):
1. **Is there a real link between the property and the offence?**
   - ***Seema Garg v. Deputy Director, ED*** — P&H HC DB, 06.03.2020 *(corpus: PHHC011207472019)*. Status: the compendium says an SLP was dismissed; that is not verified. A dismissal without reasons would not declare the law. **Adverse.** "Value of any such property" covers only property with a direct or indirect link to the proceeds. Property acquired before the criminal activity cannot be attached unless the tainted property is held abroad (paras 16 and 19). The Court disagreed with *Axis Bank*.
   - *Pavana Dibbur* (SC, 2023) para 27(c) *(corpus: 2023_INSC_1029)*: property bought before the scheduled offence has no connection with its proceeds. The complaint there was quashed on a different ground, that s.120-B alone was not a scheduled offence (para 27(e)).
2. **If there is a link, can the holder prove bona fide acquisition for lawful consideration without knowledge?** See *B. Rama Raju*, Issues A and C-D *(corpus: HBHC010111342010)*.

**Corpus and compendium disagree.** The compendium places *B. Rama Raju* in a narrow "Satyam line" on "value" property. In fact *B. Rama Raju* upheld the PMLA broadly. The narrow reading followed in *Seema Garg* comes from a different Andhra Pradesh judgment, *Satyam Computer Services v. DoE*, 2019 (3) ALD 472.

### C8. Which forum decides title, tracing, beneficial ownership and priority?

- The PMLA fora decide whether property is tainted. The NCLT has no jurisdiction over a PMLA attachment. See *Value Wise* paras 14 and 16 *(corpus: EDPORTAL_18_2026-06-30)* and *Embassy Property Developments v. State of Karnataka* (SC, 2019, (2020) 13 SCC 308, as cited in *Value Wise*).
- Writ jurisdiction remains open. In *Rajiv Chakraborty*, the High Court entertained the writ despite the alternative remedies (paras 11-12).
- ***P. Mohanraj v. Shah Brothers Ispat*** — SC, 01.03.2021, [2021] 14 SCR 204 *(corpus: 2021_INSC_133)*. Status: binding, but the NCLAT in *Value Wise* (para 13) records that its s.138 holding was referred to a larger bench in *Dineshchand Surana v. UCO Bank*. **Mixed.**
  - Its s.138 holding does not carry over to PMLA (*Rajiv Chakraborty* paras 79-80).
  - The Court itself said that confiscation is by the government "acting statutorily and not as a creditor" (para 76).
  - The moratorium protects only the corporate debtor (para 77).

### C9. How recovered property and sale proceeds are treated and distributed

- ***Udaipur Entertainment World Pvt. Ltd. v. Union of India*** — SC, 10.10.2025. Not in corpus. Status: **consent order; expressly non-precedential**; questions of law left open. **Mixed.**
  - On ED's affidavit, part of the attached project was restored to the SRA for genuine homebuyers under the second proviso to s.8(8) PMLA.
  - The attachment stayed on eleven units.
  - The company was dropped from prosecution under s.32A, while the individuals stayed prosecuted. ED kept its liberty to act if the SRA proved to be connected.
- See the companion brief on liquidation and restitution for the Special Court route, IBBI Circular IBBI/CIRP/87/2025, and *Nav Nirman* (SC, 2026).

## Open points and risks

1. ***Vantage Point II* is pending in the Supreme Court.** If the NCLAT's view is affirmed, a s.32A plan approval would end pre-CIRP attachments without any application to the PMLA fora. Until then, oppose it as sub judice and as contrary to *Value Wise* para 16.
2. **The *Hathi* order will be cited by other fugitives.** It is non-precedential (para 12(vi)).
3. **No law requires anyone to verify the source of settlement funds.** ED's protection lies in its own investigative and attachment powers, not in IBC procedure. The checklist in B8 is the compilers' view.
4. ***Jagatramka* cuts both ways.** Paras 74-75 distinguish s.12A withdrawals, so do not overstate it against promoter settlements.
5. **"Value" property is split between High Courts.** *Axis Bank* (Delhi) allows it, subject to lenders' prior rights. *Seema Garg* (P&H) bars pre-offence property. The Supreme Court has not resolved the split.
6. ***MBL Infrastructures*.** Courts are slow to unwind an implemented plan even when the applicant was ineligible. Object before implementation.
7. **Compendium paragraph references.** Several cannot be verified, including *Swiss Ribbons* "para 59" and the *Axis Bank* paras 105, 127 and 128. Cite from the corpus text.

## After the 2026 Amendment

The IBC (Amendment) Act, 2026 (Act 6 of 2026) brought most provisions into force on 26.05.2026.
- **Section 12A has been substituted.** Withdrawal is now possible only after the CoC is constituted and before resolution plans are invited, with 90% CoC approval. *LawBase analysis:* this closes, for the future, the late-stage windows used in *Brilliant Alloys* (after an EOI), *Vallal RCK* (after liquidation began) and, arguably, *Amit Katyal* (at the appellate stage under Article 142). It does nothing to test the source of funds. ED's attention should move to the shorter window between CoC constitution and the invitation for plans.
- **Section 7(5) now makes admission mandatory,** displacing *Vidarbha*-type discretion. *LawBase analysis:* respondents may argue that this narrows the preventive, suo motu rejection in *Hytone*. Section 65 itself is not stated to be amended. Raise collusion under s.65 specifically, with material, rather than relying on discretion to refuse admission.
- **The clean slate is codified** in s.31(5)-(6) and Explanations I-III. Whether a PMLA attachment is a "claim" extinguished under s.31(6) is open. Section 32A is unamended and remains the specific provision on offences, read with *Manish Kumar*.
- **Creditor-initiated insolvency and group insolvency.** Chapter IV-A (ss.58A-58K) and s.59A are enacted but **not yet notified**.
